# Independent review of the degree-strip constructor

**Verdict.** The mathematical constructor terminates for each fixed integer degree $`d\ge2`$. Its returned cutoff certifies every pair with lower degree $`d`$ at thresholds at or above the cutoff. The proof gives a finite exact decision procedure for the remaining pairs in that one strip. The cutoffs produced by the current constructor make the residual calculation impractical even at low degree. The result is an effective version of the previous proportional-range theorem. The known existential uniform old-size cutoff does not turn this into a single finite executable check of the unrestricted conjecture.

## Coefficient estimate and exceptional endpoint

I derived the normalization from the original marked ordered-block exponential generating function, expanding about mark one. The old coefficient is $`A_k=h^k[z^s]V^kH^{k+1}`$ with $`s=n-kj`$, and the forward coefficient is $`B_k=h^{k-1}[z^{s+1}](1-z)V^kH^{k+1}`$. Here $`h=1/j`$ and $`H=2\log(2)/(2-\exp(z\log(2)))`$. This normalization uses the same positive affine coordinate in both rows.

Write $`W=V^k((1-z)H)^{k+1}`$ and $`R=(W-1)/(1-z)`$. On the radius-two circle, $`|V|\le2`$, $`|(1-z)H|\le8`$, and $`|1-z|\ge1`$. The usual bound for $`|1-e^{a+ib}|`$ follows from $`e^a\ge1/8`$, $`|1-e^a|\ge|a|/8`$, and $`1-\cos b\ge2b^2/\pi^2`$. All other poles are outside the circle, and $`W(1)=1`$ makes $`R`$ analytic. Thus $`|[z^i]R|\le M_k2^{-i}`$ with $`M_k=2^k8^{k+1}+1`$. For $`k\ge1`$, the old remainder is $`h^k[z^s]R/(1-z)^k`$. For $`k\ge2`$, the forward remainder is $`h^{k-1}[z^{s+1}]R/(1-z)^{k-1}`$. Bounding the geometric convolution by $`2M_k`$ times the largest nonnegative binomial entry gives errors at most $`2M_kh(2d+4)^{k-1}`$ and $`2M_kh(2d+4)^{k-2}`$ respectively. Telescoping the main-term binomial products changes them by at most $`h k(k+1)(2d+4)^{k-1}`$ and $`h k(k+1)(2d+4)^{k-2}`$. Both fit inside the coded constant with slack.

For the $`k=1`$ forward remainder, the quotient is just $`R`$ and the relevant index is $`s+1\ge j+1`$. For $`k=0`$, $`j2^{-n}\le1/j`$ since $`n\ge2j`$. At a carry, the only extra coefficient is $`B_{d+1}=h^d V(0)^{d+1}H(0)^{d+2}`$, bounded by $`(2\log 2)^{d+2}/j`$. The model forward coefficient at this index is zero on the closed strip. These cases account for every coefficient used in the checker.

For the final projective root near the left strip endpoint, I checked the reversed polynomial argument against the precise strict inequalities in `seam_sign`. The lower bound on $`B_d/A_d`$ is $`1/[4(c-d+h)]`$. It follows by convolving the coefficient growth of $`H^2`$ with the nonnegative series $`V^dH^{d-1}`$. Independently, on the radius-two circle the $`H^2`$ remainder after its principal part has modulus at most 66. Hence its coefficient error is at most $`66\,2^{-r}`$ and the slope bound is immediate from $`r\ge10`$. In the ten smaller cases, the rational lower estimate using $`\log 2>69/100`$ has minimum $`19/50`$ for $`(r+1)([z^{r+1}]H^2/[z^r]H^2-1)`$, above the required $`1/4`$. The seam region $`c<d+1/2`$ forbids a carry when $`j\ge2`$. The certificate also has $`h\le\text{pad}`$, as required for its denominator bound. The final signs imply strict directed interlacing in the original mark.

## Termination

I checked the hard-rod input against the cited primary graph theorem and Lemmas 2.5 and 3.4. Every lattice graph is claw-free. Deleting at most one length of terminal vertices deletes a simplicial clique, so the two compatibility statements apply. Taking the two root-count inequalities gives directed monotone ordering. A repeated root would remain a root over a whole interval in the length parameter, contradicting a nonzero polynomial coefficient of highest parameter degree on an open degree strip. The delay identity and monotone interlacing force any zero derivative of a finite root to propagate to a smaller length, ending at the explicitly moving linear root. This verifies simple finite roots and strict derivative sign. [Chudnovsky and Seymour, Theorem 1.1 and Lemmas 2.5 and 3.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2006.06.001)

The projective old polynomial has degree exactly $`d`$ everywhere on the closed strip. Its leading coefficient has modulus equal to the sum of the positive model coefficients, and its only projective root at 1 occurs at $`c=d`$, with multiplicity one. Ordinary derivatives at a rational point give the shifted coefficient signs for exact Descartes counting. As all model roots are real, the sign variation counts roots strictly to the right, even if the point itself is a root.

On an infinite nested path of cells, the parameter interval shrinks to one point. For each finite root, its simplicity and the strict model difference sign stabilize both tests. Dyadic brackets of scale $`2^{-\lfloor\mathrm{depth}/2\rfloor}`$ give endpoint displacement at least one bracket grid spacing while the parameter variation and rational endpoint-isolation error are only $`O(2^{-\mathrm{depth}})`$. Padding is smaller still. The separate fences converge to points strictly between the limiting roots. They permit greedy selection without a later bracket invalidating an earlier choice. At the seam, the final bracket ends at 1 and the seam inequality tends to a positive model penultimate coefficient minus zero. The old endpoint sign at its left endpoint follows by the same simple-root displacement argument. Hence every infinite path would be accepted eventually, a contradiction. A binary refinement tree with no infinite path is finite.

An implementation issue was found in the initially reviewed source. Python 3.11 and later cap conversions of decimal integers by default. Even before padding, the error constant for $`d=1000`$ has 4,518 digits, so serialization could fail on a mathematically terminating input. I reported this to the author, who is removing that cap before parsing or serialization. The cutoff claim is about abstract exact arithmetic given resources to complete it.

## Proposed explicit height bound

The stronger degree-dependent estimate can use integer resultants. For an integer polynomial $`P`$ without zeros on $`[0,1]`$, the endpoint values have modulus at least one. Every interior extremal value is a nonzero root of the nonzero polynomial $`\mathrm{Res}_s(P',P-u)`$, with constant $`P`$ handled directly. Dividing by its power of $`u`$ and applying the reciprocal Cauchy root bound gives an explicit positive lower bound from coefficient heights. Apply this to the projective old discriminant and to the old/difference resultant after removing its entire power of $`s=c-d`$. The resultants' nonvanishing follows from simple projective old roots and strict motion of each finite hard-rod root. Use the padded difference $`(1-y)\partial_s f`$ in the resultant to account for its vanishing at the projective boundary.

The discriminant separates the old roots quantitatively since all pair distances are at most one. A difference bound $`\beta`$ at a projective root also bounds its distance to 1 below by $`\beta/\sup|f_s|`$, and $`f(s,0)\ne0`$ supplies separation from 0. A separate start-of-strip resultant between $`f(0,y)/(1-y)`$ and the padded difference gives a uniform estimate for the finite roots near the seam by continuity and an explicit derivative bound. The largest root there can still use the coefficient-growth inequality. These observations support a cutoff $`2^{O(d^7\log d)}`$ with the proposed conservative constants, subject to verifying the full constant bookkeeping in the forthcoming manuscript.
