# Independent review of the explicit degree-dependent cutoff

**Verdict.** The written argument proves the stated partial theorem with $`J_d=(64(d+2))^{32768(d+2)^7}`$ for every integer $`d=\lfloor n/j\rfloor\ge2`$. I found no missing boundary, sign, carry, or exponent. This is a paper proof with independently checked inequalities and no Lean or Rocq coverage. It yields an effective growing-degree regime and a bounded residual range for any prescribed maximum degree. The residual cutoff is impractically large, and the unrestricted conjecture remains open.

Norm estimates control the values and the first derivatives throughout the unit square.

## Cutoff arithmetic

With the manuscript's notation I obtained $`\gamma=H^{-P}`$, $`\eta=H^{-K}`$, $`L=2H^{1+dK}`$, $`\sigma=2^{-11}H^{-S}`$, $`\beta=2^{-11D}H^{-U}`$, and $`\tau=2^{-11D-4}H^{-(U+K+4)}`$. This gives exactly $`J^*=2^{7+d+11D}H^{5+U+(d+1)K}`$. For $`M=d+2\ge4`$, the stated coarse bounds follow by substituting $`d+1\le M`$, $`B\le2M`$ and $`D\le4M^2`$. In particular the power of $`64M`$ contributed by $`H`$ is at most $`21120M^7`$. Absorbing the power of two costs at most another $`50M^2`$ in that base. Their sum is below $`32768M^7`$ for every $`M\ge4`$. I also checked each displayed integer inequality exactly for degrees 2 through 10,000 as a sanity check.
