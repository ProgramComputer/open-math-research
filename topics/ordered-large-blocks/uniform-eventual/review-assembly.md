# Independent mathematical review of the uniform eventual theorem

**Verdict.** The argument establishes an existential integer $`N`$ such that for every $`n\ge N`$ and every integer $`j\ge2`$, both $`O_{n,j}`$ and $`O_{n+1,j}`$ have simple negative roots in the claimed strict consecutive order. I found no substantive mathematical gap in the eventual theorem. The author of the proposed proof and any prior verdicts were withheld. This was an independent agent review of the supplied mathematical files and dependencies.

## Original statement and quantifiers

I checked the ordered-partition entry under *Set partitions and trees*. Its enumerator counts ordered set partitions of the labeled set $`[n]`$, marking each block of size at least $`j`$. The ordered sequence construction gives the denominator $`1-S_j(z)-tT_j(z)`$ with $`S_j(z)=\sum_{a=1}^{j-1}z^a/a!`$ and $`T_j(z)=\sum_{a\ge j}z^a/a!`$. The degree is $`\lfloor n/j\rfloor`$, reached by minimum-sized large blocks and leftover singletons, and the constant term is positive. In the catalog's increasing-root convention, the old row alternates left of the new row at equal degrees. This puts the new row first in increasing *modulus*. At a carry the new row occupies both ends. [Alexandersson's statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#openProblemsSetPartitions), [catalog convention](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedInterlacing.htm).

The original conjecture asks for all $`n`$. The proof leaves an existential finite set of possible exceptions, since for $`j>n+1`$ both rows are constant. A finite check through a specified size needs to be linked to the eventual cutoff, or supplemented by a proof of any remaining sizes, to settle the original claim.

The contradiction supplies one old-size cutoff independent of both $`j`$ and rank. Sort both multisets by increasing modulus, counting multiplicity. If an old row has positive degree, every old or new root occurs in the tests at old $`k`$, next $`k`$, and next $`k+1`$ when present. Any failure thus supplies a bad old rank. Degree-zero steps are immediate. Bounded $`j`$, bounded degree, bounded $`h=m-k`$, and $`h/m`$ bounded below are covered by the four respective preliminary results. In the last case the compact endpoint has a limiting pole angle as close to $`\pi/j`$ as the prescribed rank fraction requires, and the larger window includes the next rank.

For the remaining subsequence $`j,m,h\to\infty`$ and $`h=o(m)`$, the centered saddle has $`p=m-h\sim m`$, residual $`s+hj\sim hj`$, and $`w=r/(1-r)\asymp hj/m`$. Its variance is $`\asymp mw(1+w)`$, so $`\sigma^2/j^2\asymp h/j+h^2/m`$. Extracting extended limits for $`j/\sigma`$ and, when that limit is zero, for $`hj/m`$, gives exactly the sparse, theta, moving-pole and bounded-radius hypotheses. If $`hj/m\to\infty`$ the $`h^2/m`$ term dominates, so $`h/\sqrt m\to\infty`$. If $`hj/m`$ stays bounded, density forces $`h/j\to\infty`$ and $`m/j^2\to\infty`$. Fixed shifts of $`h`$ and the carry leave all applicable asymptotic conditions intact.

## Identification of the principal terms and exact ranks

Use the physical coordinate $`Z`$ and let

$$
P_q(Z)=2-e^Z+(1+q)T_j(Z).
$$

At any simple physical pole $`Z_\star`$, the contribution to the coefficient is $`C_\star Z_\star^{-n}`$ with $`C_\star=-1/(Z_\star P_q'(Z_\star))`$. A positive spatial rescaling multiplies the row by the corresponding positive factor and keeps $`C_\star`$ unchanged.

- In the moving coordinate $`Z=\lambda z`$ with $`\lambda=\log 2`$, one has $`P_q(\lambda z)=H(z)D(z)`$. Thus $`-G(z)/(zD'(z))=C_\star`$. Substitution $`z=e^{-w/j}`$ gives exactly the stated $`K_j(w)=G(z)/(w C_j(w)F'(w))`$.
- In the bounded-radius coordinate $`Z=\rho z`$, the denominator is $`P_q(\rho z)=(1+Qz^jV(z))/T(z)`$. Differentiating at the pole gives $`C_\star=T(z)/(j+\mathcal M(z))`$.
- The upper moving $`w`$ branch is the lower physical pole. Its residue is the conjugate of the residue of the upper physical pole used in the bounded-radius proof. Both cosine phases equal $`n\arg Z_+-\arg C_+`$ modulo $`2\pi`$. Their branch shift is independent of $`n`$, including at a carry. With the compact convention $`\beta=\arg P_q'(Z_+)`$, this common phase is $`(n+1)\arg Z_++\beta-\pi`$ modulo $`2\pi`$, which accounts for the minus sign in the compact normalizer's cosine.

In a fixed interval about $`r=e^{-\epsilon/2}`$ the moving parameter satisfies $`u/j=-\log r+o(1)`$. For this continuation $`m/j^2\to\infty`$ and $`u\le\epsilon j`$ lies within the enlarged moving disk and still satisfies $`u^2/M=o(1)`$ with $`M=n/j`$. Both proofs select the same two poles of least physical modulus. The residue equality therefore identifies the phases exactly throughout a common interval. This interval contains many complete bands since its logarithmic parameter length is of order $`j`$ and the phase derivative there is of order $`m/j^2`$. Matching complete bands fixes the rank offset for both rows. Angular exclusion and the absence of zeros in band gaps propagate the count. The compact argument covers the inner disk in the original parameter, and the moving exclusion covers the next range in $`|1+q|`$. The inequalities $`|q|-1\le |1+q|\le |q|+1`$ and the padded overlaps join the two exclusions without leaving intermediate original-modulus annuli.

The moving estimate needs a consecutive difference of order $`o(1/j)`$ at an old zero. The factor $`|re^{w_b/j}-1|=O(|b|/j)`$ keeps the nonprincipal Gaussian sum at that scale. For the circle, splitting the integrand with $`B=1-z`$ and taking out the $`e^{ij\theta}`$ factor bounds the variation polynomially in $`u`$ and $`M`$ with a separate $`1/j`$. The potentially harmful $`\log j`$ factor does not enter. The fixed outer contour term is geometrically small in $`n`$, hence $`O(1/j)`$ uniformly since $`n\ge j`$. These estimates remain valid for superexponential threshold growth in $`M`$. The count formula $`m-K=m/u+O(m/u^2+m/j+1)`$ brackets the requested rank at $`u=m/(2h)`$ and $`2m/h`$, because each error is $`o(h)`$ in the moving regime.

At very small radius, the two tilted masses at degrees zero and one imply $`\mathcal W(r)\asymp r`$, and the analytic logarithmic derivative bounds are $`O(r)`$. The separation of scaled log radii between bounded targets is of order $`r(b_2^2-b_1^2)/j^2`$. Thus the principal phase derivative is of order $`Mr/j^2`$, and $`\varphi\asymp 1/j`$ even when $`r\to0`$. The small-arc poles are controlled by a Gaussian sum in $`Mr/j^2`$, and all others contribute at most $`Cj e^{-cMr}`$. The Cauchy error in principal normalization is at most $`Cj e^{-M(D-x_\pi)}`$. These estimates and the consecutive multiplier give the signed error $`o(1/j)`$ under the stated hypotheses. Choosing endpoint means $`hj/(2m)`$ and $`2hj/m`$ gives $`Mr/j^2\asymp h/j\to\infty`$ at both endpoints, and the connecting interval keeps a diverging lower bound on $`Mr/j^2`$. The count error $`O(mr/j^2+1)`$ is $`o(h)`$, with defects $`h/2+o(h)`$ and $`2h+o(h)`$. The target and neighboring new rank are therefore inside complete bands. Complex exclusion, phase univalence, and conjugation give simple real roots in these bands on both sides of the join.

## Critical and sparse exterior regimes

In the theta case I checked the ordinary local limit and its signed difference. The difference $`L(x+1)-L(x)`$ inserts $`e^{-i\theta}-1`$ in Fourier inversion, giving $`\sigma^2[L(x+1)-L(x)]\to \phi'(u)=-u\phi(u)`$ at standardized displacement $`u`$. Near $`r=1`$, factoring the single geometric pole gives enough decay beyond a fixed multiple of $`1-r`$. Shifting the saddle a fixed number of indices in either direction supplies geometric majorants on any compact annulus. The remaining factor $`1+m/(p_t+1)`$ is bounded for small relative shifts and grows at most linearly with $`|t-h|`$ when $`p_t`$ is small. The single-variable endpoint $`p_t=0`$ uses the geometric-plus-analytic decomposition at the large index $`e_m`$, where $`1-r\ge c/j`$ suffices as $`r\to1`$. At a carry the contrast coefficient at $`t=-1`$ is exact, given by taking $`L(-1)=0`$ and the prescribed positive mass $`L(0)`$ for the law with $`p_{-1}=m+1`$.

Writing $`\mathfrak q=e^{-a^2}`$, the triple product has winding numbers zero and one on radii $`\mathfrak q^{1/2}`$ and $`\mathfrak q^{-1/2}`$. Restoring the removed monomial yields precisely $`h`$ and $`h+1`$ zeros in the two disks. The one intervening root converges to $`-1`$ after scaling and is simple and negative by Rouché and conjugation. The adjacent scale changes by the factor $`\mathfrak q^{-1}>1`$. The signed contrast tends to $`-az\Theta_{\mathfrak q}'(z)`$, positive at $`-1`$ by the other product factors. The sign at the old zero puts it before the matching new root in reciprocal modulus. Any nonreal roots inside the smaller disk contribute in conjugate pairs and positive coefficients exclude positive roots, so the actual negative-root count before the annulus has the parity of the all-root disk count. The carry shifts the matched reciprocal rank by one. [Jacobi's triple product](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.5.E9).

In the sparse regime the two saddles differ by $`\asymp j/\sigma^2`$ in log-radius. The tangent discrepancy is $`\ge c j^2/\sigma^2`$, and the displaced masses retain their saddle prefactor $`1/\sigma`$. Hence the geometric tail suppression works even for arbitrarily slowly diverging $`j^2/\sigma^2`$. The odd leading Gaussian term vanishes in the centered signed difference. The surviving Fourier bound is $`O((1+w)/\sigma^3)`$, yielding an $`O(1/e_h)`$ logarithmic error in the adjacent coefficient ratio. This is $`o(j/\sigma^2)`$ since $`(1+w)/j\to0`$. The nearby tail incurs a factor only linear in distance, and the distant terms with positive reciprocal index have polynomial costs absorbed by the exponential suppression starting at distance of order $`h`$. In the exceptional index-zero case for $`j>m`$, the bound $`(C(s+m)/(jh))^m`$ dominates both $`2^h`$ and the remaining signed cost, since $`x=(s+m)/(jh)\to0`$ and $`jh^2/(s+1)\le mh/x`$. At a carry the extra constant is negligible on the signed scale by the fixed outer carry bound when $`j/m\to\infty`$, and by exponential separation of the first two new balances when $`j/m`$ is bounded. Disk counts at the two-term balance and the same parity argument as in the theta case give the strict order.
