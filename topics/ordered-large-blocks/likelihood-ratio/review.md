# Independent audit of likelihood-ratio order for weighted ordered partitions

Agent-assisted review by Codex (`lower_sign_replay`), 26 November 2026. The proposed order and the strict row log-concavity follow under the specified coefficient and support hypotheses. The short kernel must have positive singleton weight, and the support endpoints need to be stated explicitly. This review concerns the ordered sequence model and the proposed fixed-tilt quantile coupling.

Let the short and long ordinary kernels be $`S`$ and $`B`$, with contiguous log-concave coefficient sequences supported respectively on $`1,\ldots,d`$ with $`d<j`$ and on an interval starting at $`j`$. In particular $`s_1,b_j>0`$. Assume $`s_1^2\ge2s_2`$, taking missing $`s_2`$ as zero. Set $`G=(1-S)^{-1}`$, $`R_k=B^kG^{k+1}`$, $`r_{n,k}=[z^n]R_k`$, and $`f_i=[z^i]BG`$. A repeat of singletons gives positive $`[z^n]G`$ at every $`n\ge0`$, so $`r_{n,k}>0`$ exactly when $`n\ge kj`$ and $`f_i>0`$ exactly when $`i\ge j`$, even for a finite long kernel.

## Source condition and consecutive minors

For small $`\rho>0`$ set $`q=S(\rho)\in(0,1)`$ and define the compounding probabilities $`f_0^{\mathrm{cmp}}=0`$ and $`f_i^{\mathrm{cmp}}=s_i\rho^i/q`$. These are log-concave with contiguous support. For exponent $`k+1`$, the compound negative binomial generating function is $`(1-q)^{k+1}G(\rho z)^{k+1}`$. In Xia–Lv Proposition 4.3, $`\eta=q`$ because the compounding distribution has zero mass at zero. The condition becomes $`k s_1^2\ge2s_2`$, with equality allowed. It holds for every $`k\ge1`$, including a singleton short support. The coefficient normalization and positive geometric scaling preserve log-concavity. The source's necessity calculation has a local index typo in the opening inequality of Appendix A.1, and its following line uses the correct $`P_1^2\ge P_0P_2`$. The proposed use is the sufficiency direction. [Xia and Lv, Proposition 4.3 and Appendix A.1](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0269964823000293)

Convolution of two nonnegative log-concave sequences with contiguous support remains log-concave. This follows by multiplying their order-two totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrices and expanding the two-row minors by Cauchy–Binet. The finite-index sums suffice for these formal coefficients. Thus $`r_{n,k}`$ is log-concave in $`n`$ for each $`k\ge1`$. Multiplying every block weight by $`u\ge1`$ preserves the kernel hypotheses and $`s_1^2\ge2s_2`$ since the left side scales by $`u^2`$ and the right by $`u`$.

For $`k\ge1`$, compare $`r_{n+1,k+1}/r_{n,k+1}`$ with $`r_{n+1,k}/r_{n,k}`$ where all four coefficients are positive. The common supported terms of $`R_{k+1}=R_k(BG)`$ yield a weighted average of the growth ratios of the log-concave $`R_k`$ at previously shifted indices. All are at least the unshifted ratio, and the new term with $`i=n+1-kj`$ is strictly positive with no previous counterpart. The comparison is thus strict. At $`n=(k+1)j-1`$ a direct determinant comparison is strict because the upper column is attained for the first time; below attainability the relevant determinant vanishes.

For $`k=0`$, write $`c_m=[z^m]G^2`$. For $`n\ge j`$, $`g_{n+1}/g_n`$ is $`n/(n+1)`$ times the $`i s_i c_{n-i}`$-weighted average of $`c_{n-i+1}/c_{n-i}`$ over short sizes $`i`$. Those base indices are at least $`n-j+1`$. For $`r_{n+1,1}/r_{n,1}`$ the corresponding $`B G^2`$ weighted average has base indices at most $`n-j`$, with a further nonnegative new term. The $`c`$ ratios are nonincreasing, so the factor $`n/(n+1)`$ gives strictness without relying on strict log-concavity of $`c`$. At $`n=j-1`$ the upper column again switches on strictly.

For row log-concavity, divide the convolution for $`r_{n,k+1}`$ by that for $`r_{n,k}`$ at an interior count $`k`$. Its term ratios $`r_{n-i,k}/r_{n-i,k-1}`$ are at most $`r_{n,k}/r_{n,k-1}`$ by the consecutive likelihood-ratio result. The term $`i=n-(k-1)j`$ has positive denominator and zero numerator, giving a strict inequality. Rows of degree zero or one have no internal inequality. Before a count is attainable its tail probability and relevant moments can stay zero. Thereafter the strict likelihood-ratio comparison gives strict stochastic growth for all nontrivial attainable tails.

## Nearby rows and one simultaneous coupling

For $`n\le m\le n+j`$ and $`k\ge1`$, write $`r_{m,k+1}/r_{m,k}`$ as the weighted average of $`r_{m-i,k}/r_{m-i,k-1}`$ with nonnegative weights $`f_i r_{m-i,k-1}`$. Terms with zero denominator also have zero numerator and can be discarded. Since $`i\ge j`$, the earlier size $`m-i`$ is at most $`n`$. The ratio monotonicity just proved bounds every term by $`r_{n,k}/r_{n,k-1}`$ where that ratio is needed. Thus the law for row $`m`$ is bounded above in likelihood-ratio order by the law for row $`n`$ shifted up by one. At the left endpoint the shifted law starts at one while the actual law starts at zero. At the right endpoint $`\lfloor m/j\rfloor\le\lfloor n/j\rfloor+1`$, so the determinant comparisons extend through the zero coefficients. The lower comparison follows by chaining the consecutive likelihood-ratio comparisons. A fixed tilt $`t>0`$ multiplies every adjacent ratio by $`t`$ in both comparisons, preserving their signs.

With one common uniform random variable $`U`$ and the inverse CDF for each of the row laws tilted by the same $`t`$, both stochastic comparisons hold pointwise in $`U`$. Hence $`X_n\le X_m\le X_n+1`$ simultaneously for every integer pair $`n\le m\le n+j`$. This is a coupling of the block counts. Defining a jump at position $`b`$ as an increase from row $`b-1`$ to $`b`$, two jumps at $`a<b`$ with $`b-a<j`$ would force $`X_b-X_{a-1}\ge2`$ with $`b-(a-1)\le j`$. This contradicts the coupled bound and proves the asserted spacing.

## Factorial long kernel and falling-binomial normalization

I also checked the stronger normalization under the additional factorial long kernel $`b_\ell=u/\ell!`$ for $`\ell\ge j`$. For each $`k\ge1`$, compare $`R_k`$ with $`(z\partial_z-(k-1)j)R_{k-1}`$ in likelihood-ratio order by the length index. The latter splits as

$$
k(zS') B^{k-1}G^{k+1}+(k-1)(zB'-jB)B^{k-2}G^k,
$$

where the second term is absent at $`k=1`$. The short derivative factor $`zS'`$ has support strictly below that of $`B`$, so the short term precedes $`R_k`$. Here the common convolution factor $`B^{k-1}G^{k+1}`$ is log-concave by the preceding hypothesis check. The long comparison uses the common log-concave factor $`B^{k-2}G^k`$ for $`k\ge2`$. Positive convolution with a log-concave coefficient sequence preserves likelihood-ratio order by the order-two Toeplitz minor argument.

For the long comparison, $`zB'-jB`$ starts at $`j+1`$, while $`z^jB`$ starts at $`2j`$. On their common support the ratio of their coefficients, first over second, is $`(\ell-j)(\ell-j)!/\ell!`$. Its successive ratio is $`(\ell-j+1)^2/((\ell-j)(\ell+1))<1`$ since the denominator minus the numerator is $`(\ell-j)(j-1)-1>0`$ for $`\ell\ge2j`$ and $`j\ge2`$. Thus $`zB'-jB`$ precedes $`z^jB`$. The series $`B^2G=B(BG)`$ is a positive mixture of shifts $`z^i B`$ with $`i\ge j`$, each after $`z^jB`$ by log-concavity of $`B`$. This proves the long comparison. The common upper series is $`R_k`$ in both parts, so linearity of the minors adds the two left terms. In particular $`r_k(n)/(n-(k-1)j)`$ is nondecreasing for $`n>(k-1)j`$, where $`r_k(n)=r_{n,k}/r_{n,k-1}`$. At $`n=(k-1)j`$ the slope expression is undefined but the unscaled ratio is zero.

At a fixed $`n`$ with $`r_{n,k}>0`$, the weights $`f_i r_{n-i,k-1}/r_{n,k}`$ sum to one. The factorization $`B^kG^{k+1}`$ realizes them as the length distribution of one designated long block and one designated short stretch, with both classes exchangeable separately. If $`L`$ is the total length of the long blocks, then $`\mathbb E[i\mid L]=(n+L/k)/(k+1)`$. Since $`L\ge kj`$, this is at least $`(n+j)/(k+1)`$. For interior $`k`$, one can give a long block size $`j+1`$ and fill the remaining labels by singletons in a short stretch, so $`\Pr(L>kj)>0`$ and the bound is strict. The slope comparison applied at $`n-i`$, including its zero boundary, gives

$$
\frac{r_{k+1}(n)}{r_k(n)}<\frac{k}{k+1}\frac{n-kj}{n-(k-1)j}
\qquad\left(1\le k<\left\lfloor n/j\right\rfloor\right).
$$

This is exactly strict log-concavity after division by the positive generalized binomial coefficient $`\binom{n/j}{k}`$ on the actual support. When $`j\mid n`$ this is Newton's normalization by the actual degree. At other remainders $`n/j`$ exceeds that degree. The factorial comparison for $`B`$ is an extra assumption relative to the broader likelihood-ratio argument. In addition to the algebra, an independent exact labeled recurrence checked 16,938 strict normalized minors and 18,885 slope comparisons for $`j=2,\ldots,12`$, $`n\le80`$, and common weights $`u=1,2,3`$.
