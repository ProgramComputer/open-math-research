# Independent referee report

**Verdict.** I find the proposed fixed-exterior-rank theorem correct in the stated regime. This is a partial theorem for Alexandersson’s [ordered large-block conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#openProblemsSetPartitions), which asks for real-rootedness and consecutive interlacing for the entire rows. The review checks a written argument. There is no claimed Lean or Rocq verification.

**Enumerator.** The primary source sums over ordered set partitions and counts all blocks of size at least $`j`$. Choosing the first block gives the independent integer recursion

$$
O_{n,j}(t)=\sum_{a=1}^n\binom{n}{a}t^{[a\ge j]}O_{n-a,j}(t),\qquad O_{0,j}=1.
$$

Splitting the ordered list into $`p`$ large blocks with $`p+1`$ small-block sequences gives $`[t^p]O_{n,j}/n!=[x^n]S^{\ast}(x)^{p+1}V(x)^p`$, where $`S^{\ast}=1/(1-S_j)`$ and $`V=\sum_{a\ge j}x^a/a!`$. This implies the displayed $`d_h`$ and verifies the degree and reciprocal correspondence. An exact independent check compared the first-block recursion against the integer exponential-series product for all 671 pairs $`2\le j\le12`$, $`0\le n\le60`$. Every row agreed and all its present coefficients were positive.

**Coefficient estimates.** Locally uniform convergence of the divided difference at the removable point $`1`$ follows by uniform convergence of the derivatives on a slightly larger disk. The only limiting zeros of the undivided denominator are $`1+2\pi i k/\log 2`$. A fixed disk strictly larger than the unit disk therefore supplies the nonvanishing and uniform Cauchy estimates for both normalized factors. Subtracting the pole in $`(1-z)T^2=A^2/(1-z)`$ leaves an analytic and uniformly bounded term on a fixed smaller disk. Its exponentially small high coefficients are dominated by the uniformly positive $`a^2`$. For the finitely many remaining coefficients, I checked the positivity argument in proportional-window.md. On $`|z|=2`$, the displayed bounds on $`e^{\rho(z-1)}`$ imply its Cauchy bound $`65/(4\rho^2)`$ and hence positivity at indices at least seven. I reproduced the six small-index ratios exactly by multiplying the exponential Taylor series by $`(2-e^z)^2`$, whose exponential coefficients at positive indices are $`2^n-4`$.

For a uniform weak composition of $`e`$ into $`p+1`$ parts, the probability of prescribed values on $`h\le p`$ parts with sum $`v`$ is $`\binom{e-v+p-h}{p-h}/\binom{e+p}{p}`$ for $`v\le e`$. This is bounded by the factorial quotient in the argument, so summing the exponential factors separately is justified. In the upper expansion, all terms with $`h\le p`$ are bounded in aggregate by $`(1+C p/(e+p))^{p+1}`$ after changing $`C`$. The last term is $`C^{p+1}R^{-e}`$ and is bounded when $`e`$ exceeds a fixed sufficiently large multiple of $`p+1`$. The lower bound uses the one-part estimate and Jensen for the exponential, giving the matching logarithmic order. For $`e=O(p+1)`$ bounded and uniformly positive coefficient factors suffice. This checks (1), including $`p=0`$ directly.

Factoring $`(1-z)^qT^{p+1}U^p`$ as $`((1-z)T^2)^qT^{p+1-2q}U^p`$ supplies coefficientwise positivity. Convolution with $`(1-z)^{-q}`$ gives (2), including the extra summand at index $`e+1`$. In the signed logarithmic-derivative convolution, positivity of the differences and monotonicity give the first $`O(p/(e+1))`$ bound. Using this bound once more, when $`i\le e/2`$ each normalized difference is $`O(p/e)`$. The remaining sum is $`O(pR^{-e/2})`$ using the bound by $`C_p(e)`$. This gives (3) with the required uniform constants.

**Tails and root counts.** In the ordinary range $`u+t`$ bounded below, the correction from the composition estimate and $`\binom{e+p}{p}=e^p/p!\prod_{r=1}^p(1+r/e)`$ is uniformly $`O(1+m^2/j)=o(m)`$. Every term in the increment formula decreases with $`t`$. The separation between the fixed adjacent increments that bound the two tails is at least $`cm`$ whenever those anchors are present. The endpoint correction of the extrapolated chord is only $`o(m)`$ times the distance plus one. Thus the weighted off-central tails are summable geometric bounds uniform up to $`t=m`$. When $`u\to0`$ the exceptional $`t=0`$ bound follows from $`\binom{s+m}{m}\le(s+m)^m/m!`$, and the factorial quotient in (4) equals $`(m-L)^L/\prod_{r=0}^{L-1}(m-r)\le1`$. At $`L=0`$ the bound on $`B_0`$ at $`L=1`$ exactly controls $`\log(y_1/y_0)=-\log B_0`$ there. The chord from 1 to 2 supplies the remaining right-tail estimate, multiplied at index $`t\ge2`$ by $`(y_0/y_1)^{t-1}`$. These bounds establish the two disk counts with multiplicity for all fixed ranks. The single root in each annulus is real by conjugation and negative by positive coefficients. No additional complex roots can intervene between the certified ranks.

**Signed comparison and carry.** Subtracting $`\gamma_{L+1}D`$ at the old zero leaves just index $`L`$ from the central pair. The sign is $`(-1)^L`$ and the magnitude is at least a positive multiple of $`(m/j)d_L y^L`$. The tail must use the coefficient *increments*, as it does here. At the exceptional index $`t=0`$ and $`L\ge1`$, put $`x=(s+m)/j\to0`$. Multiplication of (4) by $`1+j/(s+1)`$ costs at most $`1+m/x`$, and $`(1+m/x)(C_Lx)^m\to0`$ even when $`j/m`$ is arbitrarily large. When $`L=0`$ the exceptional entry is the surviving central term, and (2) makes its positive difference larger than $`m/j`$ by a diverging factor.

At the carry, the index shifts by one and the added constant is the contribution from exactly $`m+1`$ full-size blocks. The identity for its relative size cancels both the powers of $`j!`$ and the powers of $`\rho_j`$. Applying (1) and $`m!\le m^m`$ gives $`(m/j)(Cm/j)^m`$ since the ratio of the factorials in the large-excess estimates is $`m!^2/(m-1)!=m\cdot m!`$. At the old root, $`y/y_L\to1`$, so for fixed $`L`$ the scale $`y d_Ly^L`$ dominates $`y_0d_0`$ up to a uniformly positive factor. The carry constant is negligible on the signed scale and the extra negative factor reverses the sign to $`(-1)^{L+1}`$. Applying the single-row count to the new degree $`m+1`$ and residue zero gives a first balance $`o(y_0)`$ followed by the old scales with the indicated multiplicative increment ratios. For both degree cases the signs determine the comparison with the matching root inside the gap between its immediate neighboring new scales. No condition on later roots is needed.

For readability I suggest stating the conclusion explicitly in the theorem. Let $`a_L,b_L`$ be the old and new moduli from largest downward, with zero-based indexing. At equal degrees the asserted comparisons are $`a_L>b_L>a_{L+1}`$ wherever the indicated fixed ranks are included. At a carry they are $`b_L>a_L>b_{L+1}`$. I also suggest linking the positivity argument using the relative path appropriate to the final location of the proof.
