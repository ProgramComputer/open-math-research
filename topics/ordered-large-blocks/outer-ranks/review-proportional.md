# Independent review of the bounded-window input

**26 November 2026 · Agent-assisted review.** I checked that the [bounded proportional-window argument](../proportional-window.md) supplies the full root order on subsequences with bounded degree and growing threshold. It also supplies the positive short-block coefficient property used by the [renewal proof](renewal.md). I found no substantive gap in these inputs.

The ordered sequence EGF splits a row with exactly $`k`$ large blocks into $`k+1`$ short gaps and $`k`$ large blocks. The positive rescaling has degree $`\lfloor n/j\rfloor`$ and preserves consecutive comparisons at fixed $`j`$. For bounded $`k`$, extracting the simple short-block pole with its multiplicity $`k+1`$ gives the coefficient limit on the whole proportional window. Pascal's identity gives the difference estimate, including the new coefficient when its excess rises from $`-1`$ to zero.

In the graph approximation the selected grid points have spacing at least $`L`$. Removing the compulsory gaps gives the binomial coefficient in the proof. At most $`L`$ terminal vertices are removed when passing from parameter $`c`$ to $`u`$ with $`c-1\le u<c`$, so they form a clique. All the neighbors of each removed vertex outside that clique lie in a single interval of fewer than $`L`$ steps, so it is a simplicial clique. The published compatibility lemmas apply to both the smaller graph polynomial and its product with the variable. At a negative threshold their counts differ by one. The two bounds therefore force the larger polynomial's count to lie at those two adjacent integers. This proves the stated oriented interlacing and rules out a repeated root unless it persists across an interval of parameter values, which the nonzero polynomial dependence excludes. [Chudnovsky and Seymour, Lemmas 2.5 and 3.4](https://www.math.u-szeged.hu/~hajnal/seminars/kombszem/cikkek/sz_indep.pdf)

The delay derivative follows by differentiating the coefficients. If the derivative of a root vanishes, the delay identity and the oriented order force either equality at the same rank, which would make that root constant, or equality with the preceding rank one unit earlier. At the latter equality the derivatives agree by the local extremum condition. Each iteration lowers the rank. At rank one the preceding-rank option disappears, and in the one-root interval the derivative is positive directly. The publication text makes these stopping cases explicit.

Rouché on disks about the simple limit roots gives all the finite roots. At an integer limit there can be only one further root, counted with multiplicity. Conjugation and positivity make it simple and negative. For finite-limit roots the signed adjacent-row difference is controlled by the positive limiting root derivative. At the escaping root, the top coefficient times the root tends to the negative next coefficient of the limit, and the relative change of the top coefficient dominates the other changes. The latter follows from the pole asymptotic for growing excess and the strict coefficient increase for bounded excess. The positive constant terms and the sign at negative infinity give the orientation in both degree cases. The case of a constant old row reduces to a following polynomial of degree at most one.

For the coefficient increase, I checked the separation of the pole of $`(2-e^{\rho z})^{-2}`$, the stated Cauchy bound on the radius-two circle and the initial ratios.
