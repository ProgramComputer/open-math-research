# Blind review of the ribbon lattice path matroid argument

**Review date:** 2026-11-26. **Reviewer:** Independent agent-assisted review using fresh context, the anonymous argument, the two supplied Lean source files, and the public primary sources.

**Verdict:** The mathematical argument establishes the precise connected-ribbon claim $Z_M=N_{r+1}$, and proves that all $r$ roots are simple and negative. The rank-zero case should be stated explicitly if degenerate ribbons are admitted. I found no substantive mathematical obstruction. The Lean files give partial coverage.

## Exact statement

Per Alexandersson's issue #2650, opened November 24, 2026, states in item 4 that the $Z$-polynomial of every connected ribbon lattice path matroid of rank $r$ equals the Narayana polynomial $N_{r+1}(t)$. It describes ribbons by the absence of $2\times 2$ squares. The author's catalog repeats the scope and the independence of the row composition. The issue also discusses the broader real-rootedness problem for Ferrers or nested matroids. The argument addresses the ribbon assertion. The coefficient of $t^i$ is $\binom{r+1}{i}\binom{r+1}{i+1}/(r+1)$ for $0\le i\le r$. [Original issue, item 4](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2650) · [SymCat, graphs and matroids](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#openProblemsGraphs)

## Mathematical checks

1. **Deletion.** Braden and Vysogorets, Lemma 2.7 and Theorem 2.8, equation (6), in §2.4, printed p. 6, give the required vanishing rule and deletion identity. The identity assumes the matroid is simple and the deleted element is not a coloop. The cycle matroid of the simple graph in the manuscript is simple. Either side supplies a path joining the endpoints of the common edge after deletion, so the edge lies on a cycle and is not a coloop. Theorem 3.2 and its proof in §3.1, printed pp. 11–12, apply to subgraphs joined at exactly one edge with both sides still graph-connected after deletion. A cycle matroid of either side after deletion can be disconnected. The proof needs graph connectivity.

   For every flat $F$ avoiding $e$ such that $F\cup\lbrace e\rbrace$ is a flat, contract $F\cup\lbrace e\rbrace$. The two sides meet at just the vertex resulting from contracting $e$, possibly merged with additional vertices of either side. Every circuit of the resulting graph belongs to one side, since a simple cycle crossing between sides would have to use the shared vertex twice. The contracted cycle matroid is therefore a direct sum. Each $H_i-e$ contains an path from $u$ to $v$. If $F\cap E(H_i)$ contained every edge of that path, $e$ would lie in the closure of $F$. Thus each side has a remaining edge, and each remaining edge is a nonloop because $F\cup\lbrace e\rbrace$ is a flat. Both summands have positive rank. Lemma 2.7 then makes each correction term in equation (6) zero. Ferroni, Matherne and Vecchi, Theorem 1.1 and §4.1.2, also discuss this vanishing when deriving the equivariant deletion formula for two glued cycles. [Braden and Vysogorets, version of record](https://doi.org/10.37236/9026) · [Ferroni, Matherne and Vecchi](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1673796)
2. **Polygon step.** A diagonal of a convex polygon divides the vertices into its two closed boundary arcs, which meet at the endpoints. A further edge with strict interior endpoints of opposite arcs would cross that diagonal, so every edge lies on at least one side. In a simple graph the diagonal is the unique edge common to the two sides. Each arc spans its own side after deletion. The deletion hypothesis therefore survives until all diagonals are gone, including when several diagonals share an endpoint. The cycle has rank $r$ on $r+1$ edges, with every proper edge subset independent. Proudfoot, Xu and Young give the stated coefficients for this uniform matroid in Proposition 4.9, printed p. 12, and record real negative interlacing roots in Example 5.3, printed p. 13. This polygon argument applies from $r=2$ onward. [Proudfoot, Xu and Young, version of record](https://doi.org/10.37236/7105)
3. **Ribbon step from the original presentation.** A lattice-path matroid has one interval $A_i=[q_i,p_i]$ for each North step, whose endpoints are the indices of North step number $i$ on the upper and lower paths. Both sequences are strictly increasing. With at least two ground elements, matroid connectivity forbids any intermediate common vertex of the bounding paths. At such a vertex every feasible path would pass through it, so the bases would separate as the choices on the corresponding nonempty prefix and suffix of ground elements. In particular the diagram has no separated ribbon components meeting only at a vertex. A $2\times2$ block is equivalent to an interior lattice point because the boundary follows grid edges. These are the hypotheses of the snake definition in Knauer, Martínez-Sandoval and Ramírez Alfonsín, §3, with the multi-fan correspondence in Theorem 3.2.

   More explicitly, for $1\le i\lt r$, if $q_{i+1}\gt p_i$, the two paths have equal North counts at time $p_i$ and meet. If $q_{i+1}\lt p_i$, their North counts differ by at least two at time $q_{i+1}$. On the diagonal line of constant step count $x+y=q_{i+1}$, at least one lattice point then lies strictly between the bounds, contrary to the absence of a $2\times2$ block. Thus $q_{i+1}=p_i$. Consecutive intervals share precisely one element, nonconsecutive intervals are disjoint, and the strict endpoint sequences give a unique-to-its-interval element at both ends of the interval list. There are no elements outside the intervals because such elements would be loops, and a loop or a coloop gives a singleton direct summand. Thus every element gives either a path edge joining its two consecutive interval vertices or a spoke from the hub to its sole interval vertex.

   To check the exact matroid correspondence, a spanning tree oriented away from the hub assigns one incoming edge to each interval vertex, giving a transversal. Conversely a transversal assigns one chosen edge to each interval vertex, and orienting each chosen edge toward its assigned vertex makes every interval vertex have indegree one and the hub indegree zero. Any cycle in this chosen multigraph would be a directed cycle by the indegree constraints. It cannot contain the hub and cannot lie in the path. Thus the $r$ edges form a spanning tree on $r+1$ vertices. Simplification preserves the ranked flat lattice by passing between each flat's parallel classes and their representatives. Intervals above each flat and their ranks, which give the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of the contractions in the defining sum for $Z$, are preserved as well. The simplified graph has its Hamiltonian boundary via the endpoint spokes, with each further spoke a noncrossing diagonal from the hub. These steps introduce no loop, coloop, or hidden restriction on how many interior vertices have spokes. [Knauer, Martínez-Sandoval and Ramírez Alfonsín](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2016.11.008) · [Full text, §3 and Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.00600) · [Definition of the Z-polynomial and ranked-lattice dependence](https://doi.org/10.37236/7105)
4. **Small ranks and zeros.** The cited snake definition excludes ground sets of size zero and one. Every rank-one matroid, including the singleton coloop and the parallel bundle, has a two-element ranked flat lattice, and the two terms in $Z$ are $1$ and $t$; hence $Z=N_2=1+t$. In rank zero the flat lattice has one element and $Z=N_1=1$, including a singleton loop or the empty matroid if either is admitted by the ribbon and connectivity conventions. There are then zero roots. In rank two the simplest graph is the triangle and there are no diagonals to delete.

   For $r\gt 0$, the supplied Jacobi identity follows by substitution in DLMF 18.5.8 with $\alpha=\beta=1$ and $\ell=r-i$. The weight $1-x^2$ is positive on $(-1,1)$ and the $r$ simple zeros lie in that interval. The inverse map $t=(x-1)/(x+1)$ sends these to $r$ distinct negative values. The multiplier $(1-t)^r/(r+1)$ is nonzero there, and the coefficient of $t^r$ is one, giving all the zeros. [Jacobi expansion](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E8) · [Jacobi orthogonality](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3) · [Zeros of orthogonal polynomials](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.2.vi)

## Lean coverage

- `Ribbon.lean` proves the combinatorial graph splitting in `polygonParallel`, and `ParallelAlong.commonEdge` and `ParallelAlong.endpointsAfterDeletion` recover its unique shared edge and connection after deletion. `polygon_eq_boundary` is an induction for an arbitrary graph invariant with a hypothesis that it respects this deletion. `polygon_narayana` and `fan_narayana` add a hypothesis evaluating that arbitrary coefficient function on the boundary. The supplied terms do not instantiate the invariant with the actual matroid $Z$, prove the required deletion law for $Z$, provide the cycle $Z$ evaluation, formalize the ribbon-to-graphic correspondence or simplification, or establish the root conclusion. The formal `boundary` at $n=2$ contains a single edge, and at $n=1$ is empty. This is consistent for the conditional identity and does not formalize a graph-theoretic cycle in either size.
- `RibbonExamples.lean` proves closed Boolean computations by `decide` for the quadrilateral, the diamond, and the diamond with its diagonal doubled, all of rank three. It checks the encoded Kazhdan–Lusztig recurrence coefficientwise on all flat contractions, and verifies encoded $Z$-coefficient equalities and the particular deletion equality. The rank table and subset-sum formula have the intended paper interpretation for these labeled multigraphs. An independent exact enumeration using connected components and the defining recurrence gave respectively 12, 13 and 13 flats and $P_M=1+2t,1+t,1+t$, with $Z_M=1+6t+6t^2+t^3$ in each case. The file itself contains no formal identification of these encoded calculations with a general Lean definition of matroid $P$ or $Z$.
- This is a source-level comparison and an independent mathematical and arithmetic check. The anonymous bundle supplied no pinned dependency configuration or kernel output, and I did not replay a Lean build or an independent kernel. The formal component is partial even if all supplied theorem declarations compile.
