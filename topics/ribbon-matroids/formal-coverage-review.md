# Blind mathematical review of the ribbon Narayana theorem

Agent-assisted review of the anonymized argument and Lean sources.

**Verdict.** I accept the written argument as a proof of Per Alexandersson’s ribbon conjecture. For every connected lattice path matroid with a nonempty ribbon diagram and rank $`r`$, it proves the exact identity $`Z_M(t)=N_{r+1}(t)`$ and strengthens real-rootedness to exactly $`r`$ simple negative roots. The proof also establishes the polygon-diagonal extension on paper. The supplied Lean statements cover the ribbon theorem end to end at the source level. The polygon extension and the optional empty-area singleton coloop require the scope qualifications below. This review inspected definitions and source statements. Build and independent-kernel verification are separate checks.

## Original statement

Item 4 is the rank-only Narayana identity for connected ribbon lattice path matroids. The source describes ribbons as skew shapes without $`2\times2`$ squares. SymCat confirms that this was the earlier conjecture and explains its independence from row lengths. The proof resolves that item in full. The issue’s broader question for all nested or lattice path matroids remains outside this result. [Alexandersson, issue #2650, item 4](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2650) · [SymCat, Graphs, matroids and arrays](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#openProblemsGraphs)

## Written proof

- The relevant deletion formula is Braden and Vysogorets’ Theorem 2.8, equation (6). The simple polygon graph gives a simple matroid, and either side’s boundary arc places the diagonal on a cycle. Each indexed flat $`F`$ excludes the diagonal $`e`$ and has $`F\cup\lbrace e\rbrace`$ flat. The endpoints of $`e`$ therefore lie in separate components of $`F`$, and each side contains an edge outside $`F\cup\lbrace e\rbrace`$. After contraction the sides meet at one vertex, so all circuits lie in a single side. The resulting direct-sum factors have positive rank because each missing edge is a nonloop by flatness of $`F\cup\lbrace e\rbrace`$. Lemma 2.7 kills every correction term. This is the vanishing used in their proof of Theorem 3.2, applied to their $`Z`$-formula. [Braden and Vysogorets, §2.4 and §3.1](https://doi.org/10.37236/9026)
- Any diagonal in the polygon separates the remaining graph into two sides, since the other diagonals are noncrossing. Both sides retain their boundary arcs and hence remain connected upon deletion. Every subsequent deletion keeps the whole boundary cycle and all vertices. A connected graph on $`r+1`$ vertices has cycle matroid rank $`r`$, and the boundary cycle gives $`U_{r,r+1}`$. Its $`Z`$-polynomial is the stated Narayana polynomial. [Proudfoot, Xu and Young, Proposition 4.9, p. 12](https://doi.org/10.37236/7105)
- A common intermediate vertex of the bounding paths forces the bases to factor across the corresponding nontrivial cut in step labels, contradicting matroid connectedness. The no-square condition excludes precisely the possible interior lattice vertices, since an interior lattice vertex must have all four incident unit cells in the region. This gives the snake hypotheses. The multi-fan correspondence gives a path on $`r`$ vertices and a hub with spoke bundles at the endpoints and possibly other path vertices. Simplifying the bundles preserves the ranked flat lattice and hence $`Z`$. For $`r\ge 2`$ the path and endpoint spokes form the required polygon boundary, and the remaining spokes are noncrossing diagonals. [Knauer, Martínez-Sandoval and Ramírez Alfonsín, §3 and Theorem 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2016.11.008)
- In the Jacobi expansion, setting $`x=(1+t)/(1-t)`$ changes the two factors $`(x-1)/2`$ and $`(x+1)/2`$ into $`t/(1-t)`$ and $`1/(1-t)`$. Cancelling the common denominator gives precisely the two binomial coefficients in the proposed identity. The $`r`$ distinct zeros of $`P_r^{(1,1)}`$ in $`(-1,1)`$ have $`r`$ distinct negative images under $`x\mapsto(x-1)/(x+1)`$. The leading Narayana coefficient is one, so these exhaust the degree-$`r`$ polynomial. Rank zero gives $`1`$. In rank one the ranked flat lattice has just the bottom and top. The degree bound makes the bottom KL contribution a constant and palindromicity makes it one, giving $`1+t`$ with one simple root at $`-1`$. [DLMF §18.5.8](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E8) · [DLMF §18.2(vi)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.2.vi)

## Lean statement comparison

- `RibbonNarayanaSpec.lean` describes all relevant finite presentations. Relabeling the ordered step positions by `Fin n` and taking the whole type as ground covers the ground set of a lattice path presentation. A Boolean word represents the north and east steps, the common final north count fixes both endpoint coordinates, and the prefix inequalities on each anti-diagonal express paths staying between the bounds. The basis equivalence quantifies over every set of step positions, and its endpoint condition fixes cardinality. The theorem `IsRibbonPresentation.rank_eq` verifies that this height equals matroid rank. The east step in a column occurs at exactly one height on each path, so the half-open band in `cell` selects exactly the unit cells in the diagram. `no_square` excludes the four cells of any $`2\times2`$ block. The common-circuit condition on every pair of distinct ground elements is ordinary matroid connectedness on this class.
- The formal invariant is the usual matroid $`Z`$. The shared modules build KL coefficients by rank-zero normalization, the strict half-rank degree bound and palindromicity. They prove the characteristic-polynomial recurrence using the interval Möbius formula, and identify the upper interval at a flat with the flats of its contraction. `matroidZ_eq_sum_contract` establishes the defining sum of $`t^{\mathrm{rk} F} P_{M/F}(t)`$ over all flats. This ties the implementation to the standard invariant by KL uniqueness. [Proudfoot, Xu and Young, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.3, p. 3](https://doi.org/10.37236/7105)
- `Ribbon.Source.IsRibbonPresentation.narayana` proves the coefficient identity for every natural index with the positive denominator cleared. In particular the $`r`$th coefficient is one, the constant is one, and all coefficients above $`r`$ vanish. The integer polynomial evaluated in the complex field is the same polynomial whose mapped root multiset has cardinality $`r`$. Each complex zero is real, has negative real part and has root multiplicity one, so the statement gives $`r`$ distinct simple roots. The formal route derives the separator basis model directly from the two paths, proves deletion invariance along that model, reduces to the uniform circuit basis rule, and uses a Rolle argument for the roots.
- `AuditRibbonSource.original` proves the separately declared `NarayanaClaim` with the actual `matroidZ`. It retains the all-index identity and extracts real-rootedness. `AuditRibbonSource.published` proves the separate `PublishedNarayanaClaim` with the full multiplicity conclusion for ribbons. These declarations have the same generality as the source-facing ribbon presentation. Neither statement makes a consecutive interlacing claim, which item 4 did not require.
- A conventional ribbon has a cell. For connected path presentations with at least two positions, this is already forced because two identical paths have a unique basis and give a direct sum of loops and coloops. Thus `nonempty` loses no case of the ordinary claim. It includes the one-cell ribbon $`U_{1,2}`$ and every rank-one ribbon with cells. If the term is extended to empty-area diagrams, the only connected positive-rank exception is the single north step giving a singleton coloop. The written rank-one argument covers it. The supplied Lean ribbon declarations omit that degenerate rank-one case. `AuditRibbonSource.rankZero` separately proves $`Z=1`$ for every finite rank-zero matroid on `Fin n`, including the empty ground and a singleton loop.
- The shared Lean theorem `matroidZ_delete_of_split` gives the abstract deletion identity under spanning, splitting and closure-witness hypotheses. The supplied application proves these hypotheses for the separator chain. There is no supplied graph-to-matroid bridge proving them for arbitrary noncrossing polygon diagonals or a formal induction on those diagonals. This distinction is genuine. A hexagon with the three diagonals joining its alternating vertices has a central triangular circuit sharing an edge with three other triangular circuits. In a simple multi-fan each triangular circuit contains a path edge that occurs in only that triangle, and each of its two spokes can lie in at most one other triangle. The hexagon example therefore has no ribbon presentation. The written polygon theorem should be labeled paper-proved, with its abstract deletion ingredient checked in Lean.
