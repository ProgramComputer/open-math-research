# Independent review of the geometric-center failure density

**Verdict.** The three notes prove the stated result for every fixed $d\ge2$ and $1\le r\le d$. In each positive-length compact interval inside $0<k/n<r/d$, both strict signs occur with limiting proportion $1/2$ and exact zeros have limiting proportion zero. The same proportions hold among all internal indices. These are tests at the actual coefficient ratios of the labeled-array polynomial. The conclusion measures the failure of a sufficient sign test and leaves real-rootedness of the polynomial undecided by those failed tests. [Original balanced-array conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

**Coefficient saddle and actual node.** The tilted probability identity gives the exact fixed-column-margin count after multiplying by $H(w)^n w^{-dk}$. The empty and singleton rows give both positive definite covariance and a unique Fourier maximum on the torus. Both estimates are uniform on compact intervals of quotas. The cumulant expansion through fourth order leaves an integrable Gaussian-polynomial error of relative order $n^{-3/2}$. The cubic term integrates to zero, and the coefficient of $1/n$ is smooth. The central difference of the log counts therefore gives

$$
\log t_{n,k}^{1/d}
=\log w(x)+\frac{(\log\det\Sigma(x))'}{2dn}+O(n^{-3/2}),\qquad x=k/n .
$$

The $1/n$ coefficient in the log count contributes $O(n^{-2})$ to the difference. This justifies the phase of the actual test to $o(1)$ after multiplication by $n$. For full cap, $w=x/(1-x)$ and $\Sigma=x(1-x)I_d$, so the correction also follows directly by expanding $t_{n,k}^{1/d}=\sqrt{k(k+1)/((n-k)(n-k+1))}$. For $d=2,r=1$, the exact factorial count gives the same correction as $\det\Sigma=x^2(1-2x)$.

**Oscillatory saddle and sign.** For $d\ge3$ and proper caps the cited torus lemma gives exactly the preferred diagonal conjugate pair. For $d=2,r=1$, $R=1+2iu\sin\alpha$ has exactly that pair and the real quadratic part is strictly negative for every $u>0$. For full caps the arithmetic-geometric mean bound is sharp at the diagonal pair. In $d\ge3$, a stationary angle tuple with mixed cosine signs has cosine sum at most $d-2<d\cos(\pi/d)$. A stationary tuple with a single cosine sign has all angles equal, including the case of zero cosines. In $d=2$ the cosine sum vanishes identically, and equality in the product bound requires both cosines to be zero. The stated full-cap formula for $\mathrm{Re}\lambda$ is positive for all $u>0$. Compactness gives uniform modulus gaps away from the two saddles and uniformly positive real quadratic coefficients.

The angular coordinates have normalized measure $(2\pi)^{-(d-1)}$ times Lebesgue measure, and the quadratic form has determinant $d$. Expanding the local exponential to first order in the cubic gives an odd term and a remainder bounded by $(n|p|^4+n^2|p|^6)e^{-cn|p|^2}$. Its integral is of relative order $n^{-1}$ on the scale of one saddle. This is an absolute $O(n^{-1})$ error in the positively normalized cosine formula even where the conjugate terms nearly cancel. The same estimate for the derivative follows by inserting $R_u/R$ near the two saddles and using the original product derivative away from them. At actual roots the derivative sign is $-\sin\Psi_n$ because $\theta'>0$ uniformly on the compact interval. The signs at neighboring integer phase nodes give existence, and the common crossing direction near each odd half-integer phase gives uniqueness, which also validates the compact root count. The phase is $n\theta-\nu\arg\lambda$. Multiplication by $(-1)^k$ is equivalent to subtracting $\pi k$ in the phase, and the determinant correction adds $h(x)w(x)\theta'(w(x))$. [Fixed-rank torus lemma](../fixed-rank-eventual.tex)

**Equidistribution and scope.** The real-analytic function $f(x)=\theta(w(x))$ has endpoint secant $\pi$ and initial derivative $d\sin(\pi/d)<\pi$, so $f'$ is nonconstant on every positive-length interior interval. Each nonzero Fourier mode then has finitely many resonances on a compact quota interval. After removing arbitrarily short neighborhoods of those resonances, blocks of length $\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor$ have bounded linear-phase sums and $o(1)$ phase errors per term. The normalized block errors, block sums, and omitted neighborhoods give the required Fourier limit. The arcs close to the two cosine zeros control both true zeros and sign disagreements with the approximating phase. Their measure vanishes with the tolerance. Exhaustion by compact intervals gives the full row proportions, with the remaining two edge intervals affecting an arbitrarily small fraction.

The stated theorem starts at $d=2$. If the intended scope includes literally every fixed dimension, $d=r=1$ has the same proportions by a separate calculation. There $P_n(q)=(1+q)^n$ and $t_{n,k}^2=k(k+1)/((n-k)(n-k+1))$. The polynomial value before multiplying by the parity factor has constant sign on each side of $2k=n$, with at most one zero. Thus the signs corrected by $(-1)^k$ alternate on each side. On every contiguous set of internal nodes the numbers of positive and negative tests differ by at most two.
