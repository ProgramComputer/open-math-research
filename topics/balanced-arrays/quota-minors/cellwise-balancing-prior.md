# Cellwise two-color balancing: attribution and scope

Screen date: 2026-11-26. Supporting calculation for [balanced-array issue #2](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2).

Fix common and exclusive cell sets in an ordered pair of binary arrays, and impose the same row interval on the two colors at each row. The interval may vary by row. Suppose the source red quotas are at most the source blue quotas in every column. In one column write $`a\le b`$ for the exclusive red and blue quotas, and balance them to $`a+\ell,b-\ell`$ with $`0\le\ell\le (b-a)/2`$. The conditional raw count improves by at least the product over columns of $`\binom{a+b}{a+\ell}/\binom{a+b}{a}`$. These binomial factors refer to the exclusive cells. The same multiplier is generally false with ambient column size $`n`$ and original quotas.

For clarity about the refinement of the orientation argument, add $`b-a`$ distinct dummy rows in the column, initially all red-exclusive, and recolor $`\ell`$ to blue on the target side. Pair augmented exclusive cells into red–blue pairs and disallow pairs of two dummy cells. In the source each legitimate coloring then has $`b!`$ pairings. In the target, pair red dummies to real blue and blue dummies to real red, then match the remaining $`a`$ real rows of either color. The number is $`(b-\ell)!(a+\ell)!/a!`$. Each fixed allowed undirected pairing gives a multigraph. A real row sees equal outdegree and indegree lower bounds because the two row intervals are the same there. Every source dummy forces red, while its target color may be either. Thus Kahn–Neiman's orientation comparison is applicable pairing by pairing. Dividing by the two multiplicities gives the exclusive-binomial factor.

In the diagonal one-step comparison from original quotas $`k-1,j+1`$ to $`k,j`$, with $`1\le k\le j`$, a column having $`c`$ common ones contributes factor $`(j+1-c)/(k-c)\ge(j+1)/k`$. Summing over the skeletons proves the strong coefficientwise log-concavity of $`(k!)^d a_k`$ in independent cell weights, since each product monomial marks its common cells by exponents two and its exclusive cells by exponents one. The row intervals may vary by row but must agree across the two colors.

The shared red–blue ordering is essential for the stated normalized comparison. With two rows and two columns, both row sums exactly one, and all cells exclusive, source quota profiles $`(2,0)`$ and $`(0,2)`$ have one coloring, while target profiles $`(1,1)`$ and $`(1,1)`$ have two. An exclusive-binomial factor of four therefore fails in mixed directions. Also, normalizing by the ambient $`\binom nk`$ coefficientwise would fail even for one full-cap column with $`n=4`$, at the monomial of four distinct weights in $`e_2^2`$ versus $`e_1e_3`$: the ratio is $`6/4`$, below the ambient factor $`9/4`$.

## Primary precedents

- Kahn–Neiman, *Conditional negative association for competing urns*, §4 Lemma 8 is the direct graph orientation theorem. Its proof already uses pairings at a saturated vertex, with multiplicity factors in (25)–(26). Lemma 10 is an especially close precedent: partitions of two colors with common pair constraints, an orientation comparison after adding more pairs, and a binomial factor obtained by dividing unequal pairing multiplicities in (31). Theorem 12 proves binomial-normalized log-concavity for the number of valid partial maps from a subset of one part of a bipartite graph, with interval bounds on the target occupancies. Its index is the total size of the partial domain. It does not fix a vector of equal column quotas in a binary table, or give the product of conditional exclusive-column ratios. The Wiley full-text endpoint returned HTTP 403, so lemma and equation numbers here are from the accessible author preprint. [Published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20395), [author preprint, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9).
- Leake–Mohammadi Yekta, *Log-concavity and Approximate Counting for Totally Unimodular Polytopes*, Theorem 3.1, proves the **unnormalized** coefficientwise midpoint inequality for bounded unimodular fibers. Binary table variables are bounded incidence edges. Add one slack edge from each row to an extra column, with slack in $`[0,U_i-L_i]`$ and row equality $`U_i`$, then set slack weights to one. The bipartite incidence matrix remains totally unimodular and the extra column quota varies affinely with the original quotas. Thus their result already covers the unnormalized coefficientwise midpoint comparison with row intervals. It does not give the conditional binomial multiplier or the factorial-normalized comparison. [Primary v2, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39917v2#S3.SS1).
- Wagner's *Negatively Correlated Random Variables and Mason’s Conjecture*, §3, relates weighted Rayleigh conditions to ordinary rank-sequence ultra-log-concavity and, in the symmetric case, monomial positivity of Rayleigh differences. Kahn–Neiman's *A strong log-concavity property for measures on Boolean algebras* gives conditional antipodal-pair criteria for scalar rank sequence normalizations. These are related methods for weighted set systems. Neither checked result gives the fixed-skeleton column factorials. [Wagner DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00026-008-0348-z), [Kahn–Neiman](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0243).
- Chan's Theorems 1.4–1.5 give normalized matching for the set of balls in one urn conditioned on occupancy events or other urn counts. They concern independent ball assignments and a single conditional urn set. They do not directly include jointly prescribed independent column subsets with distinct labels within each column. Wagner's degree-constrained subgraph results and the recent degree-constrained orientation enumeration papers handle distinct indexing or comparisons. [Chan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06273), [Wagner degree-constrained subgraphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412059), [degree-constrained orientations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12693).
- The project’s earlier orientation explanation already uses independent cell weights and heterogeneous intervals for the unnormalized mixed quota comparison, and labeled dummy rows to equalize pertinent quotas. Its full ambient-binomial normalization uses an additional row-symmetric probability argument. The present comparison refines that labeled-dummy construction by the exclusion of dummy–dummy pairs. [Project orientation note](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/main/topics/balanced-arrays/orientation-normalization.md).
