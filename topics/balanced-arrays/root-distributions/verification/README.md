## Fixed row sizes and exact Mahler brackets

The checker enumerates every column-margin vector by dynamic programming over labeled row supports for 144 sorted row-size profiles. The checked pairs $`(n,d)`$ are $`(3,3),(4,4),(5,4),(4,5),(6,4)`$. It includes each row-size profile with total divisible by $`d`$. At each profile it checks the total independent-row count, that balanced margins maximize the count, and both factors in the smoothing denominator. It also replays 28,724 individual transfers from a column larger by at least two to a smaller one.

The exact root-count certificate uses integer Schur recursion. Given $`B(z)=A(tz)`$ after clearing denominators at a dyadic radius $`t`$, let $`B^*(z)=z^mB(1/z)`$ and let $`b_0,b_m`$ be its constant and leading coefficients. If $`|b_m|>|b_0|`$, use $`b_mB-b_0B^*`$ and divide out its forced root at zero. If $`|b_0|>|b_m|`$, use $`b_0B-b_mB^*`$ with no division. In each case the degree decreases. A circle on which this procedure reaches a nonzero constant has no boundary roots, since a boundary root would pass to the next lower-degree polynomial. Rouché's theorem then gives the same number of inside roots under each corresponding transformation, taking account of the forced zero when applicable. A tie is skipped and never counted as a certificate. Dyadic subdivision gives exact brackets for the ordered moduli, including cases in which several distinct roots have the same modulus.

Multiplying the rational brackets in the product formula gives lower and upper bounds $`M^-(t)\le\mathcal M_A(t)\le M^+(t)`$. The checker verifies $`b_jt^j\le\binom mjM^-(t)`$ for 575 coefficient bounds and verifies $`M^+(t)\le A(t)`$ at the same certified radii. On 15 consecutive certified radii with a strict increase in the count, it also gives exact strict rational secant bounds

$$
(t/u)^{C(u)}<M^-(t)/M^+(u)\le M^+(t)/M^-(u)<(t/u)^{C(t)}
\qquad (u<t).
$$

The cases include actual balanced arrays with homogeneous caps, two cap types and three cap types, their hole reversals with forced zero factors, and explicit nonreal-root polynomials. For $`(1+q^2)^2`$ at radius one, the factored expression gives left count zero and right count four exactly.

## Nonvacuous dense finite example

Use $`n=d=64`$, uniform cap $`63`$, and $`\delta=3/2`$. The coefficients are independently generated by inclusion–exclusion over forbidden full rows,

$$
a_k=\sum_j(-1)^j\binom{64}{j}\binom{64-j}{k-j}^{64}.
$$

They satisfy the integer tests $`a_k^2>9a_{k-1}a_{k+1}`$ at each internal index. At each geometric radius the two neighboring terms relative to its central monomial are below $`1/3`$, and every farther ratio is no larger. Thus absolute monomial dominance gives an exact circle count at each geometric radius, and the alternating signs give simple negative roots in every intervening interval. To count at a specified rational radius, the checker compares its square to two neighboring geometric squares $`a_{k-1}/a_{k+1}`$. Exact integer or rational evaluation at the negative test point distinguishes the two possible counts by parity and excludes a boundary root.

For $`e^v=1/2,1,2`$, the certified counts at the respective test radii $`(e^v)^{64}`$ are $`21,32,43`$. The script checks both pressure bounds directly using the coefficient Mahler lower bound and positive polynomial evaluation as an upper bound. It also checks the finite radial estimate with its full $`E(|v|+\delta)`$, $`U`$ and $`\delta/8`$ terms. At $`v=0`$, exact rational logarithm bounds give an upper bound $`203/500`$ on its right side. This narrows the count from $`[0,63]`$ to $`[7,57]`$, while the independent dominance certificate gives exactly $`32`$.

For a rational input to a logarithm, the checker reduces by powers of two and uses the power series for $`2\,\mathrm{atanh}((x-1)/(x+1))`$ on $`1\le x\le2`$. It bounds the omitted positive tail geometrically. All decisions are exact and use no numerical root or logarithm approximations.

At $`n=10^6`$, $`d=250000`$, $`r=127000`$, the finite theorem with $`\delta=1/32`$ places between 324,834 and 341,833 roots in the disk of radius $`2^{-250000}`$. The computation bounds the error by $`17/2000`$ at $`v=-\log 2`$. This is a certified interval obtained without expanding the polynomial. Its exact count is unknown here.
