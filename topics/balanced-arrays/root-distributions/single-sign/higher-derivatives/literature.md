# Higher derivatives and degree-independent counts at geometric circles

Independent agent-assisted literature check and Jensen proof review by Codex (lower_sign_replay), 2026-11-26.

For a polynomial $`P(z)=\sum_{i=0}^D a_i z^i`$ with positive coefficients, write $`G_i=a_i^2/(a_{i-1}a_{i+1})`$ and let $`v=\sqrt{a_{k-1}/a_{k+1}}`$ for an internal index $`k`$. The geometric-circle real projection is $`A(\cos\theta)=\mathrm{Re}(P(ve^{i\theta})/[a_k(ve^{i\theta})^k])`$. Write $`A(x)=1+\sum_{j\ge1}c_jT_j(x)`$, where $`c_j`$ combines the two normalized coefficient magnitudes at distance $`j`$ and missing terms are zero. If all $`G_i\ge Q>1`$, then $`c_1\le 2Q^{-1/2}`$ and $`c_{j+1}\le c_j Q^{-j-1/2}`$. These follow by multiplying successive outward coefficient ratios, which start at $`G_k^{-1/2}`$ on both sides.

## Higher derivatives

The endpoint argument checks out. For an order $`r\ge1`$ and an index $`j\ge r`$, the endpoint identity and consecutive ratio are

$$
T_j^{(r)}(-1)=(-1)^{j-r}D_r(j),
\qquad
D_r(j)=\frac{j^2\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(j^2-i^2)}{(2r-1)!!},
\qquad
\frac{D_r(j+1)}{D_r(j)}
=\frac{j+1}{j}\frac{j+r}{j+1-r}.
$$

The identities follow from the terminating shifted hypergeometric expansion of $`T_j`$, or by differentiating the Chebyshev differential equation at the endpoint and using parity. Both factors of the ratio are decreasing in $`j\ge r`$. Thus the absolute terms in $`A^{(r)}(-1)`$ have consecutive ratio at most $`2(r+1)Q^{-r-1/2}`$. The bound $`B_r=[2(r+1)]^{2/(2r+1)}`$ decreases with $`r\ge1`$ since the derivative of its logarithm has the sign of $`(2r+1)/(r+1)-2\log(2r+2)<0`$. When $`Q\ge B_m`$, the alternating sum is nonnegative for every $`r\ge m`$, so the coefficients of $`(x+1)^r`$ in $`A`$ are nonnegative for all such $`r`$. If $`c_m>0`$, $`A^{(m)}(x)>0`$ for $`x>-1`$. Indeed the leading coefficient of $`A`$ is positive, and its degree is at least $`m`$. For $`Q>B_m`$, the first alternating pair has strict positive difference whenever its first term is present, so positivity also holds at $`x=-1`$. Descartes' rule in $`x+1`$, or Rolle's theorem, bounds the interior real projection zeros by $`m`$. If $`c_m=0`$, the projection has degree less than $`m`$. Strict positivity on $`[-1,1]`$ persists at nearby radii with the same central index and gives the open and closed disk bounds by the winding argument below. [NIST shifted hypergeometric identity 18.5.11_2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E11_2).

For $`1<Q<B_m`$, take the degree-$`2(m+1)`$ symmetric polynomial with $`a_{k+j}=Q^{-j^2/2}`$, $`k=m+1`$ and $`|j|\le m+1`$. Every quotient equals $`Q`$ and $`v=1`$. Here $`A^{(m)}(-1)`$ is a two-term sum whose second absolute term is $`2(m+1)Q^{-m-1/2}>1`$ times the first. It is negative on an interval adjacent to $`-1`$. At equality the same endpoint derivative is zero. This witnesses sharpness for derivative positivity in the interior, with the endpoint included only in the strict guarantee. Sharpness of the disk-discrepancy threshold for general $`m`$ remains a separate question. In particular, $`(1+z)^{2(m+1)}`$ has minimum quotient $`((m+2)/(m+1))^2`$, with middle open and closed discrepancies both $`m+1`$, so any strict uniform threshold for the $`m`$ count bound must be at least that constant.

### Related positivity results

- The Laplace representation and the derivative identity for normalized Gegenbauer polynomials are classical. The representation takes a weighted average of $`(x+i\sqrt{1-x^2}\cos\varphi)^j`$ over $`\varphi`$ for $`|x|\le1`$. Fejér positivity on the unit circle transfers to the disk by harmonicity and then through this integral. Schoenberg-type positive definiteness of Gegenbauer expansions is a different positivity notion from pointwise positivity in $`x`$. [NIST 18.10.4](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.10.E4), [NIST 18.9.19](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.9.E19), [Musin, multivariate positive definite functions on spheres](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v168-n1-p01.pdf).
- Ruscheweyh's 1978 theorem is particularly close at the level of the auxiliary series. For $`\lambda\ge1/2`$, $`-1<x<1`$ and arbitrary positive nonincreasing weights, it gives nonvanishing in the closed unit disk of the polynomial with those weights multiplied by $`C_j^{(\lambda)}(x)/C_j^{(\lambda)}(1)`$. Setting its argument to $`1`$ gives a positive value by continuity from $`0`$. In the Gegenbauer proof of the derivative result, the weights proportional to $`c_{m+j}(m+j)C_j^{(m)}(1)`$ are nonincreasing by the endpoint ratio estimate above. Thus Ruscheweyh's theorem is an alternate direct route to interior positivity once the geometric coefficient estimate obtains the sharp threshold. It concerns the normalized Gegenbauer series and does not state the geometric-circle condition or its root count. [Ruscheweyh, *On the Kakeya–Eneström Theorem and Gegenbauer Polynomial Sums*, SIAM J. Math. Anal. 9 (1978), 682–686](https://doi.org/10.1137/0509049).
- Koumandos and Ruscheweyh give finite Gegenbauer-cosine sums with unweighted $`C_j^{(\mu)}(x)`$ in a small positive parameter range $`\mu\le0.345778\ldots`$ in their starlike-function theorem. Alzer and Kwong give monotonicity of specific weighted cosine sums in their Eq. (8) and absolute monotonicity of a family of rational functions in Corollary 1.9. These are related positivity results for different coefficients or functions. I found no occurrence of the shifted Chebyshev endpoint ratio criterion applied to this geometric-radius estimate. [Koumandos and Ruscheweyh (2006), abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00365-004-0584-3), [Alzer and Kwong (2022), pp. 3–4](https://rendiconti.dmi.units.it/volumi/54/019.pdf#page=3).
- Nguyen and Vishnyakova use the identical geometric radii and the $`m=1`$ constant $`B_1=2\sqrt[3]2`$ for eventual exact counts for entire functions with ordered second quotients. Their Rouché proof finds the real projection minimum for the normalized, symmetrized five central terms. This anticipates the angular mechanism in the first-order case. Their lemma does not give the endpoint Taylor coefficient conditions or a sequence of all-orders thresholds. [Nguyen and Vishnyakova (2021), Lemma 2.1 and its proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.04754#page=6).

## Jensen count bound

I independently checked the degree-independent estimate

$$
\max\lbrace |N_{<}(v)-k|,|N_{\le}(v)-k|\rbrace
\le \frac{e\pi^2}{8\log Q}.
$$

Let $`\lambda=\log Q`$ and normalize the entire even, $`2\pi`$-periodic function $`f(w)=A(\cos w)/A(1)`$, so $`f(0)=1`$. Regard its cosine weights as probabilities on nonnegative integers. The reference probability on such indices has weights proportional to $`1`$ at zero and $`2e^{-\lambda j^2/2}`$ at $`j\ge1`$, the law of the absolute value of a centered discrete Gaussian $`J`$ on $`\mathbb Z`$. The likelihood ratio of the cosine weights to the reference weights is nonincreasing by the outward ratio estimates, including the step from zero to one. This gives stochastic domination. For example, writing the ratio as a decreasing function of the reference index pairs its covariance with every increasing test function nonpositively.

For $`w=u+it`$ one has $`|\cos(jw)|\le\cosh(jt)`$. Apply stochastic domination to $`\cosh(jt)`$ and then complete the square in the moment generating function of $`J`$. The remaining ratio of the translated Gaussian lattice sum to its value at zero is at most one, as follows from Poisson summation and the nonnegative Fourier coefficients of this periodized Gaussian. This yields

$$
|f(u+it)|\le \mathrm E\cosh(tJ)=\mathrm E e^{tJ}
\le e^{t^2/(2\lambda)}.
$$

The last inequality is the standard centered discrete Gaussian moment bound in one dimension. [Micciancio and Peikert, Lemma 2.8, p. 14 of author preprint](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/efftrap.pdf#page=14).

Fix $`\pi<R<2\pi`$. Jensen's formula at zero bounds the sum of the weights $`\max(0,\log(R/|w|))`$ of the zeros by $`R^2/(4\lambda)`$, the circular mean of $`(\mathrm{Im}\,w)^2/(2\lambda)`$. For each distinct real projection zero $`0<\theta<\pi`$, use its four distinct orbit points $`\pm\theta`$ and $`\pm(2\pi-\theta)`$, evenness and periodicity supplying all four. Each orbit contributes at least

$$
2\bigl[\log(R/\theta)+\max(0,\log(R/(2\pi-\theta)))\bigr]
\ge 4\log(R/\pi),
$$

since $`\theta(2\pi-\theta)\le\pi^2`$. Different zeros in the upper semicircle give disjoint orbits. This doubles the lower bound per crossing compared with counting only $`\pm\theta`$.

For a boundary-free radius the winding of $`P(ve^{i\theta})/[a_k(ve^{i\theta})^k]`$ is the centered disk count. If $`L`$ is the number of distinct interior upper-semicircle projection zeros, each of the $`L-1`$ intervening half-plane arcs permits absolute argument change at most $`\pi`$, and the first and last arcs each permit at most $`\pi/2`$ because their outer endpoints are on the real axis. For $`L=0`$ the argument change is zero since both real endpoints lie in the same half-plane. Reflection doubles the argument change, so the absolute winding is at most $`L`$. Therefore at the geometric radius with free boundary the absolute discrepancy is at most $`R^2/[16\lambda\log(R/\pi)]`$. Choosing $`R=\pi\sqrt e`$ gives the displayed bound.

For a radius with boundary zeros, normalize the corresponding projection at nearby radii in the same way, holding $`k`$ fixed. The new functions are still even and periodic, and converge uniformly on the closed disk of radius $`R`$ to $`f`$. On that fixed contour the upper bound $`e^{(\mathrm{Im}\,w)^2/(2\lambda)}`$ is at least one. An additive uniform error $`\epsilon`$ thus increases the log upper bound by at most $`\log(1+\epsilon)`$. Jensen and the orbit count now apply with that error to boundary-free radii tending to $`v`$ from below and above. The resulting counts eventually equal $`N_{<}(v)`$ and $`N_{\le}(v)`$, respectively. Zeros exactly on the Jensen circle contribute zero and can also be handled by continuity in $`R`$.

For $`P(z)=(1+z)^{2m}`$ the middle radius is one, the minimum second quotient is $`Q_m=(1+1/m)^2`$ at the middle index, and the counts are $`0`$ and $`2m`$. The discrepancy is $`m`$ and $`m\log Q_m\to2`$. Thus the order $`1/\log Q`$ is best possible for the general polynomial class as $`Q\downarrow1`$. This example leaves the leading universal constant between $`2`$ and $`e\pi^2/8`$. For the balanced arrays, the coefficient bound $`\log G_i\ge4d/(n+2)`$ gives a count discrepancy at most $`e\pi^2(n+2)/(32d)`$ at every internal index. That consequence is conditional on the repository's orientation-comparison bound. The binomial example asserts sharp order for general positive polynomials.

## Radial and winding antecedents

- Iserles and Saff discuss the exact transform $`x^j\mapsto T_j(x)`$ and deduce projection zeros on $`(-1,1)`$ from winding on the circle in their proof of (5.4). It is the same projection-versus-winding mechanism for an unshifted polynomial whose roots all lie in the disk. Schelin instead computes the exact count for a general real polynomial by a Cauchy-index and Sturm-sequence calculation in the Chebyshev basis. These are direct antecedents for the argument-principle ingredient. Neither supplies the degree-independent dependence on the second quotients reviewed above. [Iserles and Saff, p. 351, Eq. (5.4)](https://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/saffeb/texts/107.pdf), [Schelin, *Counting Zeros of Real Polynomials within the Unit Disk* (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1137/0720072).
- In Ostrowski's Newton diagram, the strictly log-concave positive coefficients give consecutive tropical radii $`\alpha_j=a_{j-1}/a_j`$. The geometric radius is $`\sqrt{\alpha_k\alpha_{k+1}}`$, centrally placed within the coefficient's tropical interval on a logarithmic scale. Ostrowski's indexed modulus factors in §6 grow with the index or remaining degree. Applied with only the uniform tropical gap $`\log Q`$, those comparisons give an index window of order $`\log D/\log Q`$. Akian, Gaubert and Sharify give lower and upper product-modulus estimates using tropical roots. Their scalar Corollary 4.2 uses Jensen and Parseval on the original polynomial to bound a product of moduli. Its Jensen argument is different from counting zeros of the normalized real projection as an entire function of angle. [Ostrowski (1940), §6, printed p. 143](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/5617-11511_2006_Article_BF02546329.pdf#page=45), [Akian, Gaubert and Sharify, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.2967), [version of record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2016.11.004).
- Avendaño, Kogan, Nisse and Rojas give exact multiplicity counts on connected clusters formed by $`\log 3`$ neighborhoods of tropical log-radii, with a $`\log 2`$ modification at the extremal ends. Their Theorem 1.5 is degree-independent as an annular cluster theorem. When consecutive tropical log-radii are all separated by less than $`2\log 3`$, the neighborhoods join into one cluster, which can contain the entire degree and leaves the count at a prescribed internal geometric radius open. Bini, Noferini and Sharify have related separated-annulus estimates. The constants of these annular statements and the coefficient threshold for a sharp higher derivative address different bounds. [Avendaño, Kogan, Nisse and Rojas, Theorem 1.5 and Lemma 2.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.3681), [version of record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jco.2017.11.008), [Bini, Noferini and Sharify, Theorem 1.3](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/20868/1/1206.3632v2.pdf).
