# Source comparison and status

The application concerns Per Alexandersson’s balanced binary arrays. The scientific question here is how much the coefficients tell us about the unclassified roots between the two certified intervals. A disk count within one at every quota applies even when a numerical value for the dimension-uniform eventual height is unavailable. A complete proof of the array conjecture would also determine the type of every root in that interval. ## The same radius and the same angular constant in earlier work

Nguyen and Vishnyakova (2021, Lemma 2.1, quoted in their subsequent Lemma 3.2) use the same normalized geometric radii. For an entire function with nondecreasing second quotients starting at $`\sqrt[3]{16}`$, they obtain the prescribed disk count for all sufficiently large indices. Under their hypotheses the count is exact. In the present finite-polynomial problem the quotients may vary in either direction, every internal index is covered, and a negative sign can occur. A new lower threshold $`9/4`$ gives a one-root error bound in that setting. Their proof also minimizes a symmetrized five-term comparison on this circle by locating the vertex of its quadratic expression in the cosine. This gives an earlier use of the same constant and the same quadratic mechanism. Here the monotonicity assertion concerns the entire rotated polynomial. [Nguyen–Vishnyakova, Lemma 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2021.124955) · [Subsequent paper, Lemma 3.2](https://doi.org/10.7153/mia-2022-25-06) · [Open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11757)

The positive sine kernels in the angular-monotonicity proof are classical. Convexity of the coefficients of the derivative gives positivity by finite summation by parts. An independent agent-assisted review supplied this proof. The coefficient threshold for this angular property is sharp by a symmetric quartic. Handelman (2013, §2) already discusses the constant-quotient family to which the quartic belongs. [Solodov (2016), §§1–2](https://doi.org/10.1070/SM8633) · [Handelman, §§1–2](https://doi.org/10.1216/RMJ-2013-43-1-149)

## Other localization criteria

Hutchinson’s and Kurtz’s global factor-four hypotheses guarantee real-rootedness. Handelman’s Theorem 1.1 uses absolute coefficient bounds for annular localization, and Theorem 1.2 treats the global factor-four hypothesis for positive coefficients. In the local factor-four result here, each other index needs only ordinary log-concavity. [Hutchinson (1923)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1923-1501248-1) · [Kurtz (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1992.11995845) · [Handelman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.6022)

Pellet-type and tropical criteria give an exact disk count when the central monomial dominates the sum of the magnitudes of the others. Bielenova, Nazarenko and Vishnyakova also use the same geometric circles with absolute comparisons. The disk theorem here permits substantial cancellation and explicitly treats roots on the counting circle. [Pellet (1881)](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMA_1881_2_5_1_393_0/) · [Forsgård, Novikov and Shapiro, Lemma 16 and Corollary 17](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnw118) · [Bielenova, Nazarenko and Vishnyakova, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08108)
