## Analytic argument

- The block construction gives positivity through each count polynomial's capacity bound. Each row block spans at most five distinct entries in the cyclic list, hence at most one of each column, and the sequence includes each column exactly $k$ times.
- The independent tilted row supports have the required exact expected margins, by column symmetry and the chosen scalar tilt. Rounding every nonsingleton, nonempty count down and adjusting each singleton and then the empty count gives a feasible type whose deviations are bounded solely by the number of allowed supports. Positive lower bounds for the probabilities on compact quota intervals ensure feasibility uniformly. Stirling yields logarithm $O(\log n)$ for the probability of this type, since the empirical frequencies differ from the tilted probabilities by $O(1/n)$ on a fixed finite alphabet. The tilted event probability provides the upper bound. The entropy squeeze near quota zero and the continuity of $f$ there extend the approximation to $[0,\delta]$.
- Differentiating the entropy rates cancels the inverse-weight derivatives and yields $-f'=\log Q$. The expansion $Q(\theta)=\theta^2/\alpha+O(\theta^3)$ with analytic remainder justifies positivity and strict monotonicity of $Q$ near zero. For fixed sufficiently small $q>0$, the contribution with $k/n\ge\delta$ is exponentially small even after the linear number of terms. The unique maximizer on the remaining interval is interior and has positive value. Uniform exponential weights then concentrate at that quota, so the log derivative tends to it.
- For a hypothetical unbounded real-rooted subsequence, all roots are strictly negative and finite. The weak-limit measures have mass $\beta$ on the compact interval $[0,1]$. For each fixed $q>0$, $p_q$ is continuous on that entire interval, so the same weak subsequence yields the log-derivative limit for every $q$. Around each fixed positive $q$, the denominator has a uniform positive lower bound for real $q$ and a sufficiently small complex disk yields a uniform analytic expansion. Contributions from endpoint atoms are constants, so the nonconstant small-$q$ inverse forces positive mass in $(0,1)$. This gives $0<T(q)<\beta$ and $T'(q)>0$. The identity theorem applies to $Q\circ T$ on the whole positive axis because $Q$ is analytic at every strictly interior quota, regardless of global monotonicity. As $q\to\infty$ any strictly interior limiting value of $T$ would force $Q(T(q))$ to stay finite, whereas the identity makes it diverge. The limit cannot be zero by monotonicity. At the other endpoint the small-$q$ inverse tends to zero, so $T$ attains $\theta_*$.
