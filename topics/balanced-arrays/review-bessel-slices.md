## Root transfer and powers at zero

The minimum quota is $h=\lceil s/2\rceil$. The reduced coefficients have positive constant term, and a term at quota $h+j$ uses $L+2j$ occupied rows, with $L=2h+s$. Thus the labeled polynomial is $q^h(n)_L$ times the transform with multiplier $(n-L)_{2j}$ for $n\ge L$. In particular $(h,L)$ is $(1,3)$, $(1,4)$, $(2,7)$ for two, four and six singletons. An empty slice below its minimum has no asserted root property.

Writing the transform as $Q_N(x)=x^N S_N(-x^{-2})$ shows precisely how positive zeros produce all the negative zeros of the reduced polynomial. The leading coefficient in $Q_N$ stays nonzero under product approximation. Reserving $N-1$ nonzero linear factors, in pairs if needed, reduces the maximal multiplicity to one by the logarithmic-derivative argument. For even $N$, zero cannot be a simple root of the even polynomial. For odd $N$, the root at zero is simple and is omitted when mapping to the reduced polynomial. Parity and the degree count account for all roots of $S_N$. Rolle's theorem with $Q_{N+1}'=(N+1)Q_N$ places the rightmost reduced root in the longer row when both rows have roots. In equal degree the old roots alternate to the left of the new roots, and in the degree-increase case the new row has both outer roots.

## Four singletons and weighted two singletons

The normally convergent product derivative series and the alternating bound for $F$ justify the summation. For positive independent weights, the coefficient of the minimum-quota distinct-color case is $v_av_bw_{cd}$ for the pair $ab|cd$. In the derivative identity, the prime may be read as differentiation with respect to the Bessel argument. ## Six singletons

The power $W_3(z)=zU_3(-z^2)$ has just a simple zero at zero because $U_3(0)=3/2$. The cubic coefficient $p_3(u)$ in the auxiliary polynomial is strictly negative for $u\ge1$, and $P(u,-1/\sqrt u)<0$ there from the displayed terms. The smallest positive zero $a_1$ of $F(-u)$ exceeds one by the alternating-series grouping. On each gap $(a_j,a_{j+1})$, the ratio $\sqrt u R$ is strictly increasing from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$ as follows by differentiating the normally convergent paired partial fractions in the variable $z=\sqrt u$. Thus the two interior test points occur in the stated order, and the signs force three distinct roots. At $a_1$ the cleared polynomial identity yields a strictly negative value since $G(-a_1)$ is positive. No root occurs at any pole because $p_3(a_j)G(-a_j)^3\ne0$.

For odd $s=3$, the asymptotic comparison is with $B(3A^2-B^2)$. Its zeros at $B/A=0,\pm\sqrt3$ are simple because the ratio is strictly increasing and has simple poles where $B$ is nonzero. Its positive count is one before the first zero of $A$ and three in every complete gap. At a large positive zero of $A$ the leading sine is bounded away from zero. On the near-real part of the comparison circle the real part differs from the radius by $O(1/r)$, and off that part the hyperbolic-sine term supplies the exponential lower bound. The right-half-plane Bessel asymptotic bound and parity therefore give strict Rouché domination around the full circle. For radius $\sqrt{a_j}$ the real sign count, its reflection and the simple origin zero already reach the comparator's complete count inside the circle. Letting these radii grow excludes every nonreal zero and every excess multiplicity. This uses the global counts directly and does not need the additional local disks. The order and genus-zero argument then transfers the six-singleton slice to all finite sizes. ## Supplement on consecutive interlacing of the paired sums

Reviewed 2026-11-25 after receipt of the Appell argument. **Passed for consecutive nonempty summed rows, with the rightmost root in the longer row when both have roots.** The earlier review of the sum covered root location and simplicity. The added argument supplies the consecutive comparison.

The coefficient of $x^{M-2j}$ in $g_M$ is $(M)_{2j}[u^j]b$, so $xg_M'$ gives exactly the occupancy factor $M-2j$. The derivative identity follows from $(M-2j)(M)_{2j}=M(M-1)_{2j}$, with the term of exponent zero omitted under differentiation, and the same calculation applies to $f_M$. Thus $H_M'=M(H_{M-1}+g_{M-1})$. At a positive zero $t$ of $H_M$ the previous audit gives $g_M(t)\ne0$. Differentiating $H_M/g_M=f_M/g_M+x(\log|g_M|)'$ and subtracting $(\log|g_M|)'$ gives the displayed expression for $M H_{M-1}(t)/g_M(t)$. The derivative of $f_M/g_M$ is nonpositive by the nonpositive residues of $D_1/D_2$ and the decreasing substitution $u=x^{-2}$. The remaining term is strictly negative because $t>0$ and the nonconstant $g_M$ is real-rooted. The derivative $H_M'(t)/g_M(t)$ is strictly negative by the strict quotient derivative in $u$ and the same substitution. This proves equality of the signs of $H_{M-1}(t)$ and $H_M'(t)$, and excludes any shared positive root.

These signs alternate at consecutive positive roots and are positive at the largest one because the leading coefficient of $H_M$ is $a(0)+M b(0)>0$. For even $M=2d$, the $d-1$ interior gaps account for every positive old root. The signs at the largest new root and positive infinity are both positive, so the one remaining simple old root cannot be to the right. It must lie to the left of the first positive new root. The additional root of $H_M$ at zero when $M$ is odd is simple from the positive top quota coefficient and is discarded on passing to quota roots. The increasing map $x\mapsto-x^{-2}$ preserves the resulting orientation. The nonempty cases $M=0,1$ have no reduced roots, and the next transition has one new root and no old roots. The higher-pair result is conditional on the stated alternation of its entire functions.
