## The uniform absolute error at the midpoint

The zero-hole indicators in different rows have nonpositive covariance. At a fixed hole quota $H$ with $H\le2N$, where $N=n-i\sim\alpha d$, their expected sum is at least $n(1-2N/n)^d\asymp n$. Chebyshev bounds the probability of no zero-hole rows by $O(n^{-1})$. The discarded probability $\Pr(H>2N)$ is also $O(n^{-1})$ from the midpoint mean and variance bounds. Hence $P(t)/F(t)=O(n^{-1})$ for every proper cap in this regime.

To rule out the uniform absolute-error comparison, one must also bound $\min_{|z|=t}|F(z)|/F(t)$ away from one. The same midpoint estimates give $m=N+O(1)$ and $\mathbb E(H-m)^2=O(1)$ for a mode $m$. A fixed window about $m$ consequently has probability at least $1/2$, so the modal mass $p_m$ is bounded below. Both neighboring quotas exist for large $n$, and the raw binomial curvature there satisfies

$$
\Gamma_m=\left((1+1/m)(1+1/(n-m))\right)^d\le C_\alpha.
$$

The two outward ratios are at most one and their product is $\Gamma_m^{-1}$, so $p_{m+1}\ge p_m/C_\alpha$. The two-copy variance identity gives $V\ge p_m p_{m+1}\ge\delta_\alpha>0$. Evaluating the Bernoulli factors at the negative midpoint gives $|F(-t)|/F(t)\le e^{-2V}\le e^{-2\delta_\alpha}$. Meanwhile the maximum normalized absolute perturbation is exactly $1-P(t)/F(t)$ because all coefficient differences have the same sign and the bound is attained at positive $t$. The uniform comparison therefore fails eventually. This conclusion concerns the uniform absolute bound used against the minimum of the reference modulus.

## Configuration counting and the endpoint

For a profile of row sizes $(s_i)$, $L!/(\prod_i s_i!)$ counts assignments of distinguishable stubs to row sets, with $N$ stubs for each of the $d$ columns and $L=dN$. Summation over the allowed profiles gives $L![z^L]E_{h,R}(z)^n$. On binary configurations, every array is counted $(N!)^d$ times. For fixed profile, uniform permutations into labeled slots induce the uniform law on stub assignments because every assignment has the same factor $\prod_i s_i!$ of slot orders.

The expected collision-pair count is at most $d\binom N2 (R-1)/(L-1)$, so a proportion at least one half has $O_R(N+1)$ pairs. For the indicated repair, rows containing the repeated column $c$ exclude at most $RN$ slots. Columns represented in the colliding row exclude at most another $RN$ slots. Thus $d>2R$ guarantees an available exchange. The target row contains no copy of $c$, and the returning column is absent from the colliding row. The swap creates no pair and removes at least one. A deterministic repair is invertible given its ordered history of swaps, so any binary output has at most $\sum_{j\le O_R(N+1)}L^{2j}$ preimages among the inputs with few pairs. This yields the stated uniformly subexponential loss for $N=O(n/d)$ and $d\asymp\sqrt n$.

For $h<\lambda<R$, the exact mean tilt at $L/n$ has all $R-h+1$ probabilities bounded below along the sequence. Floor the counts in sizes $h+2,\ldots,R$. The consecutive sizes $h,h+1$ can absorb both the count and total discrepancies with bounded changes. Their counts stay positive for large $n$. The resulting multinomial type has probability $\exp[-O_R(\log n)]$, giving the exact-total lower bound. The upper bound follows by tilting, and the unlimited saddle follows by letting $R$ increase at fixed interior $\lambda$.

If $L/n\to h$ along feasible totals, let $e=L-nh\ge0$. Then $e=o(n)$, so a profile with $e$ rows of size $h+1$ and $n-e$ rows of size $h$ gives lower exponential coefficient rate $-\log(h!)$ with upper cutoff $R=h+1$. The coefficient upper bound at fixed $z>0$ tends as $z\downarrow0$ to the same rate. Together with the factorials this gives $\psi_h(h)=h\log h-h-\log(h!)$. The minimum quota $\lceil nh/d\rceil$ is feasible and has $L-nh<d=o(n)$. Totals below $nh$ yield zero coefficient, so feasibility matters at the endpoint. This rate is also the continuous limit of the interior saddle, since $\mu_h(z)-h=z/(h+1)+O(z^2)$ at zero.

## Concavity, concentration, and the dilation scope

For $z>0$, the identities $\mu_h=z+h p_h$ and $z p_h'=(h-\mu_h)p_h$ give $0<V_h=z+h(h-\mu_h)p_h<z<\mu_h$ for fixed $h\ge1$. Therefore $\psi_h''(\lambda)=1/\lambda-1/V_h<0$. The three chosen quotas give equally spaced interior limiting values $h+1,h+2,h+3$, so affine corrections from any one shared multiplier and dilation leave a strictly negative second difference on scale $n$.

For the positive partitions, the factor $L!/((N!)^d d^L)$ is a multinomial atom and at most one. It costs only $O(d\log(N+1))=o(n)$ in the lower bound for a quota nearest the desired mean. This establishes both positive-pressure limits, using preliminary $h=0$ for the unrestricted one. The derivative of the normalized log partition in $\log z$ is $(d/n)\mathbb EH$. Convexity and nearby fixed secants identify the derivative limits, and monotonicity extends them to $z_n\to z>0$. For the capped law, normalized log-concavity survives reversal and has contiguous positive support. Comparing its outward ratios to those at the mode yields the same binomial-dependent bounds as in the unrestricted case. The shifted lower endpoint simply removes unavailable steps. For large $d$, the second moment about the mode is at most $19(1+m/d)$. Writing $\mu=\mathbb EH$, the inequality $\sqrt{19(1+m/d)}\le5+m/2$ gives $m\le2(\mu+5)$ and hence $V\le38(1+\mu/d)$. In particular the variances at fixed charts are bounded.

After dividing by the forced power $y^{m_0}$, the capped scaled mean tends to $\mu_h(z)-h>0$. In the unrestricted Bernoulli factorization, the count of factors with root modulus at most the positive radius differs from the unrestricted mean by at most twice its variance. Deleting the $m_0$ smallest moduli changes that count by truncating at zero after subtraction of $m_0$. The truncated variance is at most the full variance. Thus for a finite positive dilation limit $\beta$ the truncated scaled mean is $\max(\beta z-h,0)$. Monotonicity gives the zero limit for $\beta\to0$. The ratio $\mu_h(z)/z$ strictly decreases because $(p_h/z)'=p_h(h-\mu_h-1)/z^2<0$. Matching positive scaled means at two distinct charts is therefore impossible. For $\beta\to\infty$, monotone comparison with each fixed dilation gives unrestricted mean $\mu_F\gg n/d$. Removing $m_0=O(n/d)$ factors leaves the truncated mean asymptotic to $\mu_F$, and its standard deviation is $o(\mu_F)$ by the variance bound.

In all cases with a mean separation $\Delta$, the two standard deviations divided by $|\Delta|$ tend to zero. On $0\le t\le3\pi/|\Delta|$ each centered characteristic factor is $1+o(1)$ uniformly by the first absolute moment bound. The ratio admits a continuous phase lift whose endpoints differ by $3\pi+o(1)$ in magnitude. Thus it reaches the opposite argument to any chosen nonzero complex scalar. Both values are nonzero on the arc, and the strict scalar Rouché inequality fails there. If a sequence of dilations allowed the comparison on both charts at arbitrarily large heights, a convergent subsequence in the compactified range $[0,\infty]$ would fall into one of the cases above, a contradiction. This establishes the claimed obstruction for a single global dilation with the stated truncation and independent scalars on the two fixed circles.
