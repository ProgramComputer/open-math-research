# Independent referee report on uniform eventual real-rootedness

**Verdict.** I accept the written theorem that a single integer $N$ suffices for all $d\ge2$ and all $1\le r\le d$. I found no substantive gap in the new argument, using the cited fixed-quadratic and large-dimension theorems as inputs. The original conjecture asks for real-rootedness at every row count. The argument gives simple negative roots for every $n\ge N$ in dimensions at least two and reduces the original conjecture to an existentially finite set of possible exceptions. The argument leaves the numerical threshold and the status of those possible exceptions open.

I compared the anonymous argument with Per Alexandersson’s issue and catalog entry. In both, the coefficient at $q^k$ counts labeled $n\times d$ binary matrices with every column sum equal to $k$ and every row sum at most $r$. Both sources quantify over $n\ge0$ and $1\le r\le d$. The conjecture permits multiple roots and includes constants. In one column the polynomial is $(1+q)^n$, so the theorem’s simplicity clause is correctly restricted to $d\ge2$. I checked the new proofs and the applicability of the prior estimates cited below. I treated the two earlier all-root theorems as inputs. This review was independent of the earlier referee reports and computations. ## Angular signs and assembly

For the analytic angular bound, the fixed-size stability disk works for all $s$, using complements in the real-part bound and the even fourth-order coefficient when useful. Factoring the common quadratic and cubic contributions out of the complex row-size mixture leaves zero first-order fluctuation. The squared fluctuation of the coefficients is $O(V/d^2)=O(a/d)$ because they are Lipschitz with constant $O(1/d)$ and $a\ge V/d$. The log remainder is therefore bounded by $Ca(Q_4+Q_2M_y^3)$. The real Gaussian comparison at precision $A=n\mathrm{Re}\,a(t)\asymp na$ uses cancellation in the cubic moment on the zero-sum hyperplane and a $\chi^2$ estimate for the radial correction. The errors vanish under $na/d\to\infty$ even when that ratio grows arbitrarily slowly. Under the same condition the full Gaussian is admissible after the fixed chart cutoff. Its normalized factor is $(2\pi na)^{-(d-1)/2}/\sqrt d$, from the fixed-product torus lattice. This lattice differs from the projected quotient in the fixed-size problem.

The all-size modulus bound removes the height loss outside the common-angle charts. Applying the all-tilts variance bound to the lower tails of $S$ and $d-S$ gives exponential control of sizes with atypically small $a_S$. The fixed-size exponential chord bound then averages to $\exp(-caD_z)$, including bounded $aD_z$ by the pair estimate. At $h\le h_0$, the geometric deficit tail gives the analogues at every label, and at an internal phase level $nh\gtrsim d$. When the principal pair need not dominate, retained labels lie at width $O((nV)^{-1/2})$ or $O((nh)^{-1/2})$. Subtracting the appropriate odd multiple of the principal phase cancels the linear drift. Their parity is common and their combined amplitude controls both the summed local errors and the discarded Gaussian lattice tail. This includes a number of significant labels growing with $d$.

A hypothetical sequence of bad rows with $n\to\infty$ must satisfy $d\to\infty$, $n/d^2\to0$ and $d/n\to0$ by the two prior all-root bounds. The full cap can be removed independently. A bounded limit of $r/b$ is handled by the geometric sign proof. If $r/b\to\infty$, then $D/d\to\infty$, so the ratios $k/d$ and $(D-k)/d$ cannot both remain bounded.
