# Independent referee report

This blind review was agent-assisted and covered the proposed fixed-profile asymptotic and its localization proof. The statement under review is as follows. For each dimension $d$, choose independent uniform $s_i$-subsets of $[d]$, where $\sum_i s_i=dk$, $k/d\to\beta\in(0,\infty)$, and $\max_i s_i=o(d)$. With $\Lambda=\sum_i s_i(d-s_i)/(d(d-1))$, the probability of column sums identically $k$ is

$$
\sqrt d\,(2\pi\Lambda)^{-(d-1)/2}\exp(-1/(12\beta))(1+o(1)).
$$

**Verdict.** The argument establishes this statement for every sequence of profiles satisfying the hypotheses above. I found no mathematical gap or missing profile hypothesis. My one requested clarification is to describe the retained chart as a specific domain with asymptotically full Gaussian probability, as detailed below. The review covers the written mathematics.

## Absolute bounds and the angular chart

The large-$sD/d$ part of the single-row estimate follows by coefficient extraction at $r=s/(d-s)$. The binomial normalization costs $O(\sqrt s)$. When the mean modulus is at least $1/2$, angular integration pays this factor back. For smaller mean modulus the decay on the coefficient circle is exponential in $s$. The disjoint-pair argument gives the needed bound down to zero dispersion. Exchangeability makes the selected $s$-subset uniform. The squared chords have sum of means $2sD/(d-1)$. The mixed second moment is bounded by dropping disjointness in the numerator, and the diagonal term is bounded using the squared chord upper bound of four. The application of Cauchy–Schwarz to this second moment gives the asserted linear loss at the origin and a fixed loss on the remaining bounded $sD/d$ interval.

For the coarse tail with $D>L$, the nearest point of the $d$-point angular grid increases the optimized chord sum by $O(1/d)$, costing a bounded factor since $k=O(d)$. The product bound then integrates to at most a polynomial factor times $e^{-c kL}(C/\sqrt k)^d$. Increasing fixed $L$ offsets the Gaussian-normalized $\exp(O(d))$ factor. In the bounded-dispersion region the expansion around the complex mean has no linear term and gives a total logarithmic error $O_L(k s_{\max}/d)=o(d)$. It therefore preserves the coefficient $1/2$ to the precision needed for a fixed exceptional angle. The one-coordinate chord Laplace integral has its precise Gaussian normalization to relative error $O(1/k)$. The exceptional angle then gains $\exp(-c k\eta^2)$ after allowing for the grid and flag unions. I also checked the optional shrinking-angle bound. The retained degree-two term yields the exact coefficient $\Lambda$ in the leading dispersion penalty, with total overhead $O_L(1+k(s_{\max}/d)^2)$. The stated shrinking condition dominates that overhead and the grid and flag unions.

Pinned angular coordinates push forward onto the zero-sum quotient lattice. The Gram matrix of the projected coordinate basis is $I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf{T}/d$ in dimension $d-1$. Its determinant is $1/d$, so the factor $\sqrt d$ in the Haar-normalized Gaussian is correct. The centered Fourier integrand is invariant under lattice changes because the sum of all prescribed counts is $dk$. The short lift is injective on the chart after centering because the pairwise differences belong to an interval of length strictly less than $2\pi$.

The retained chart can be defined as the image of $D\le L_0$ and all angles within $\delta$ of the circular mean, with $L_0>1/\beta$ chosen first. Since $\min_\tau\sum_j|z_j-e^{i\tau}|^2=2D/(1+|\overline z|)$, the image lies in $Q_2\le(\pi^2/2)L_0$ and $M_y\le2\delta$. Choose $\delta$ after this bound is fixed to meet the uniform integrability estimates. Conversely, if $y$ is centered with $Q_2\le L_1$ for $1/\beta<L_1<L_0$ and $M_y\le\delta/3$, then $D\le\sum_j|e^{iy_j}-1|^2\le Q_2$. The circular mean lies within the arc spanning the coordinates, so each angular distance from it is below $\delta$. This core has Gaussian probability tending to one. Applying the local expansion and uniform integrability on the actual retained chart completes the argument. The domain inclusions bound Gaussian probability directly.

## Fixed-profile correction

The right-half-plane nonvanishing argument gives an analytic logarithm on a disk of radius proportional to $1/M_y$. The positive-variable elementary symmetric inequality bounds its real part by $C a_s|z|^2Q_2$, using the cancellation of the linear term. Borel–Carathéodory and Cauchy's estimate yield the stated remainder $O(a_sQ_2M_y^3)$ at $z=i$, with constants independent of the profile.

I re-expanded the moments by their distinct-index multiplicities. In the fourth raw moment, the coefficients of $Q_4$ and $Q_2^2$ are $p_1-7p_2+12p_3-6p_4$ and $3(p_2-2p_3+p_4)$, respectively. Subtracting $3a_s^2Q_2^2$ gives the asserted cumulant. I independently checked the second through fourth cumulants and the simplified formula for $B_s/a_s$ by exact subset enumeration for every row size in dimensions $4$ through $10$, using a nonconstant centered integer vector in each dimension. The resulting 56 instances agreed. The analytic estimates give $\Gamma/\Lambda\to1$, $A/\Lambda\to1$, and $dB'/\Lambda\to-1$ uniformly over the given sparse row profiles. The two quartic limits are $1/(8\beta)$ and $-1/(8\beta)$, and the projected Gaussian cubic reduces to the sum of the third Hermite polynomials. The limiting variance of $\Gamma Q_3$ is $6/\beta$. Its characteristic function at $-1/6$ gives the proposed factor.

The remainders converge to zero in Gaussian probability. For uniform integrability, independence of the Gaussian sample mean from the centered vector justifies the fourth-power comparison on the truncated event. The one-dimensional expectation is $1+O(1/\Lambda)$ since one can absorb the truncated quartic term into the quadratic exponent and integrate a weighted fourth moment. The Gaussian union bound for $M_Y$ then controls the other exponential. On the interval from $\sqrt{K\log(d+1)/\Lambda}$ to the fixed small cutoff, absorbing $C\Lambda L M_Y^3$ into $\Lambda M_Y^2/8$ leaves a Gaussian tail summable even with the factor $d$ from the union bound. These two bounds hold at fixed powers above one, including for the correction restricted to the actual chart.

As a normalization check, for singleton rows the exact multinomial probability is $(dk)!/(d^{dk}(k!)^d)$. Stirling's expansion has polynomial prefactor $\sqrt d(2\pi k)^{-(d-1)/2}$ and logarithmic correction $1/(12dk)-d/(12k)+o(1)$. This agrees with the result since $\Lambda=k$ for that profile.
