# Independent review of the remote row-mesh obstruction

**Verdict.** The argument is valid for every fixed target row count $m\ge16$ in $U(2,4)$ and every sufficiently large source row count $N$, with the cutoff allowed to depend on $m$. The backward diagonal coefficient transfer fails to preserve real-rootedness even on inputs of the full source degree whose multiplicative mesh is at least that of the actual source polynomial.

Let $M=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$. Take $N$ large enough that $P_N$ has $M$ simple negative roots, and write $P_N(x)=\prod_{i=0}^{M-1}(1+\lambda_i x)$ with $0<\lambda_0<\cdots<\lambda_{M-1}$. Reciprocation reverses the order of the absolute root values and preserves their minimum consecutive ratio. If this ratio is $Q_N$, then $\lambda_i\le\lambda_{M-1}Q_N^{-(M-1-i)}$. As $\lambda_{M-1}\le a(N,1)\le N^4$ and $\prod_i\lambda_i=a(N,M)\ge1$, this gives $1\le Q_N\le N^{8/(M-1)}\to1$.

The degree-$M$ polynomial $B_N(x)=\prod_{i=0}^{M-1}(1+w_i x)$ with $w_i=Q_N^i$ has exactly the required mesh. Write $E=\sum_iw_i$. For fixed $L$ and sufficiently large $N$, the ratio $E/\max_iw_i$ is at least $\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}Q_N^{-j}$, which tends to $L$. Hence $\max_iw_i/E\to0$. Sampling $k$ indices independently with probabilities $w_i/E$, the probability of no collision is $k!e_k(w)/E^k$. The collision probability is at most $\binom{k}{2}\sum_i(w_i/E)^2\le\binom{k}{2}\max_iw_i/E$. Thus the required coefficient asymptotic holds for each fixed $k$. The same argument works for any prescribed $S_N\ge Q_N$ with $S_N\to1$ in place of $Q_N$. In particular $Q_N^2$ gives strictly larger mesh.

For each fixed $k$, the arrays using mutually disjoint row sets for the four columns give the lower bound $(N)_{4k}/(k!)^4$ for $a(N,k)$, and unrestricted column choices give the upper bound $\binom Nk^4$. These bounds establish $a(N,k)\sim N^{4k}/(k!)^4$. Every source coefficient on $[0,M]$ is positive, so the diagonal map sending $P_N$ to $P_m$ is uniquely determined. Its multipliers are $a(m,k)/a(N,k)$, with trailing zeros above degree $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. After applying it to $B_N$ and substituting $x\mapsto N^4x/E$, the $k$th coefficient tends to $(k!)^3a(m,k)$. The output degrees are fixed, so this is convergence uniformly on compact subsets. A disk centered at a nonreal root of the limiting polynomial and separated from the real axis gives persistence of a nonreal root by Rouché's theorem, even if the limiting root is multiple. The positive scaling preserves nonreality.

The nonreality of the limiting polynomial for every $m\ge16$ is the existing cubic-factorial classification. As spot checks, I independently counted the $m=16$ coefficients by convolving the allowed row supports on four integer column margins and obtained six real roots in degree eight by rational Sturm arithmetic. I also reconstructed the symbolic fourth Newton sum using nonempty rows and the choice of their labels. All fourteen coefficients in the variable $m-76$ are strictly negative, independently confirming the nonreal tail for $m\ge76$. This review uses the existing exact classification for the remaining finite targets.

The term “backward diagonal transfer” avoids suggesting an invertible linear map. The obstruction concerns preservation on the stated input class. The geometric witness differs from $P_N$, so it leaves the deterministic implication about the original $P_N$ and $P_m$ open. The proof also gives no preservation claim for a fixed mesh bound greater than one or when the target grows with the source.
