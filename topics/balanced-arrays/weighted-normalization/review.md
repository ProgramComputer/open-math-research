## Distinguished-column normalization

With $2t$ unpinned distinguished cells, every one is exclusive in an antipodal pair. Each other column has equally many exclusive cells of the two colors and admits the same number of opposite-color refinements on each side, as determined by the underlying cell monomial. After fixing its uncolored pairs, every position outside the distinguished column is covered by the disjoint pair family. The disjoint row groups and their lower thresholds enforce both original interval bounds. Lemma 10 therefore compares the central and upper color counts by $(t+1)/t$. Reversing the colors gives the lower count, and this factor is the ratio of the relevant central binomial coefficients. This proves CAPP, including weighted fibers, after restoring each monomial's weight and dividing by its refinement multiplicity. An interval of possible distinguished quotas follows by interpolating feasible fractional circulations and applying integrality with the intermediate integer quota fixed. Theorem 11 applies with ambient order $n$. An identically zero one-column sequence satisfies the desired local log-concavity inequalities directly. [Kahn and Neiman, Lemma 10 and Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=11)

## Rectangular support and global comparison

Hoffman's circulation cuts give precisely the two displayed families. For an internal cut with columns $J$, the entering lower supply is $q(J)$. Either excluding or including each row in the cut incurs respectively $|C_i\cap J|$ or $U_i$ in outgoing upper capacity, yielding the right-hand bound. For a cut containing both source and sink, denote its outside columns by $J$. An outside row contributes $L_i$ to incoming lower capacity and $|C_i\setminus J|$ to outgoing upper capacity. Taking the strongest inequality over outside rows gives the left-hand bound. The source-only cut has zero incoming lower capacity, and the sink-only cut is covered by the capacity of the return arc. Integer capacity bounds give integral feasibility. As every constraint bounds a sum of a subset of quotas, any profile coordinatewise between two feasible comparable endpoints also satisfies every cut. Zero-weight cells are omitted from the network. [Hoffman's circulation theorem](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~reiner/Classes/Schrijver2004.pdf)

For the mixed step, Kahn and Neiman's orientation inequality applies with identical symmetric bounds at ordinary rows, a forced outward auxiliary vertex in both counts, and the other auxiliary vertex changing its forced direction. Fixed monomial weights restore the comparison for all nonnegative real cell weights. The normalization cancels exactly across the four mixed terms. The same-coordinate step follows from the distinguished-column result. The full box between the two feasible endpoint profiles contains each intermediate base and its increment, so every ratio used along the path has positive numerator and denominator. Pairing the two parallel common-quota paths gives the diagonal inequality. The positive diagonal support is an interval by the same cuts, so zero endpoint terms require no division.

## Roots and example

After removal of the smallest feasible power, the polynomial has strictly positive coefficients. At every internal index the normalization gives the displayed second-quotient lower bound, with the second inequality following from $k(n-k)\le n^2/4$. The factor-four hypothesis makes each adjacent term less than half the center in magnitude. Log-concavity makes the terms on each tail decrease outward, and their alternating pairs are nonnegative relative to the center's sign. Consecutive geometric radii strictly increase since the coefficient ratios strictly decrease. ## Prior results and exposition

Kahn and Neiman's [separate CAPP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0243) is the original general source for the implication to ultra-log-concavity. Their competing-urn paper contains the exact hypergraph comparison directly used here. The cut characterization is an application of Hoffman's theorem. A focused search for weighted binary contingency tables, interval degree constraints and normalized column-margin log-concavity found no directly matching statement that combines the global rectangular ratio comparison and the diagonal conclusion. This is a scoped literature check. The nearby tree-coordinate log-concavity in the [cell-bounded contingency-table FPRAS](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/An-FPRAS-for-Cell-Bounded-Contingency-Tables-September-24-2026/build/sections/introduction.tex) concerns a coordinate of its tree-flow completion construction.

For readability, the theorem's first sentence could say directly that each one-coordinate restriction of $B$ is log-concave. The threshold conclusion is easiest to parse if it refers to simple negative nonzero roots and allows origin multiplicity zero.
