## Support and orientation

For a positive crossed product at sizes $s<t$, pair red arrays at margin $a$ with blue arrays at margin $b$ after adding $b-a$ outward red auxiliaries in each column, on separate auxiliary vertices. If the red has $h_j$ exclusive real cells in column $j$, each coloring has exactly $(h_j+b-a)!$ refinements there. The cell monomial, row degree bounds and factorials remain fixed when the two marked outdegree and indegree requirements exchange. The crossed red bound at the marked vertex is the larger one. All real unmarked vertices have identical bounds for the two directions, and each auxiliary remains outward in both counts. Kahn–Neiman’s comparison therefore makes the swapped product positive. The marked interval permits both requested sizes on each side.

At each margin the two endpoint arrays give fractional flows at intermediate marked loads, and fixing the marked row arc to an integer load preserves integral feasibility. At homogeneous column margins, arbitrary column permutations give feasibility of every mask of any such size. This use of permutations preserves support when all cell weights are positive and does not require their magnitudes to be exchangeable. Every residual ratio used in the proof has positive numerator and denominator. When the crossed product is zero, nonnegativity gives the result directly. Replacing zero cell weights by positive values and taking a limit covers all remaining boundary cases of the polynomial inequality.

## Factor and coupling

For $T=S\cup\lbrace i\rbrace$, apply the normalization theorem to the two residual bases $v_a-e_i\le v_b-e_i$ and their positive increments to $v_a,v_b$. On taking reciprocals it gives $B(v_a-e_i)/B(v_a)\le B(v_b-e_i)/B(v_b)$. The marked weight cancels in the ratio between the two quota levels. The residual binomial ratio is exactly $\binom{n-1}{k-1}/\binom{n-1}{k}=k/(n-k)$. Thus each added marked cell increases $f_b/f_a$ by a factor at least $\lambda=b(n-a)/(a(n-b))$.

The coupling at $a$ applies to the actual product weights. Fixing common and exclusive cells makes the pair weight constant, and the refinement factors are independent of the orientation. Row-specific upper bounds of at most three give one of the four allowed graph constraints at each unmarked vertex independently. The nested coupling then orders the means of $f_b(S)/(\lambda^{|S|}f_a(S))$, which are the normalized size ratios in the manuscript. Iteration gives precisely the power $\lambda^{t-s}$, including nonadjacent margins and sizes. The marked row itself may have any permitted size interval.

The universal factor is attained for unrestricted unit-weight rows in dimensions at most three. With $n=3$, $d=1$, $a=1$, $b=2$, $s=0$, and $t=1$, write $w$ for the marked weight and $y_1,y_2$ for the other weights. The difference between the two sides is $w(y_1-y_2)^2$. Its mixed monomial has a negative coefficient, so this larger bound is a pointwise inequality for nonnegative weights, distinct from the earlier coefficientwise guarantee. The theorem covers all finite sizes under its hypotheses and only interior column margins. Above cap three the cited graph reduction leaves open the degree-six interval of outdegrees two through four. In the limitation note, “forming two disjoint opposite-direction pairs and leaving two incidences free” would specify the count $2\binom{i}{2}\binom{6-i}{2}$ unambiguously.
