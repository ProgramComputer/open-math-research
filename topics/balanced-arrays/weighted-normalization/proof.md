# Binomial normalization for weighted binary matrices

Let $A(\boldsymbol q)$ be the total weight of labeled binary matrices with $n\ge1$ rows and $d\ge1$ columns, fixed column sums $\boldsymbol q$ and a prescribed integer interval $[L_i,U_i]\subseteq[0,d]$ for the size of each row $i$. Each occupied cell contributes its own arbitrary nonnegative real weight. Define $B(\boldsymbol q)=A(\boldsymbol q)/\prod_j\binom n{q_j}$ on the integer box $[0,n]^d$.

**Theorem.** Each one-coordinate restriction of $B$ is log-concave. For any feasible comparable bases $\boldsymbol q\le\boldsymbol p$ with feasible increments in column $j$, the ratio $B(\boldsymbol q+e_j)/B(\boldsymbol q)$ is at least $B(\boldsymbol p+e_j)/B(\boldsymbol p)$. Consequently the normalized common-quota sequence $A(k\mathbf1)/\binom nk^d$ is log-concave, with interval positive support when it is nonzero.

In particular, if $(1+2/n)^{2d}>4$, every nonzero root of the nonzero weighted common-quota polynomial is simple and negative. Its multiplicity at the origin equals the smallest feasible quota. Row lower bounds can exclude the constant term. The unrestricted unit-weight matrix model attains equality in the normalized ratio comparisons. The unweighted common-cap theorem was already known in this repository. [Earlier orientation proof](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/106ccf3/topics/balanced-arrays/orientation-normalization.md)

## One column and the conditional antipodal-pairs property

Fix the quotas of all columns except one. Use the occupied row set of that column as a Boolean random variable, with unnormalized mass given by the total fiber weight. To establish the conditional antipodal-pairs property, pin some rows of the distinguished column to zero or one, leaving $2t$ free. For $t\ge1$ compare ordered red and blue realizations having complementary free bits and agreeing at every pinned bit. Sum over all red free sets of size $t$, or of size $t+1$, and in each case over the two weighted array fibers.

Fix the common ones and exclusive cells of the two arrays. Every array pair in this class has the same weight. In every other column, the numbers of red-only and blue-only positions coincide. Refine a coloring by pairing them bijectively. If there are $h_j$ of each kind in column $j$, each coloring has exactly $\prod_j h_j!$ refinements, independent of how the distinguished cells are colored. Fix the resulting uncolored pairs. Let $W$ consist of all exclusive cells. There are $2t$ uncovered positions in the distinguished column, and every other position belongs to a disjoint two-element pair. In row $i$ group all its exclusive positions into $H_i$. If $c_i$ is the common load there and $h_i=|H_i|$, then both row intervals hold exactly when each color occupies at least $\max(0,L_i-c_i,c_i+h_i-U_i)$ slots of $H_i$. Empty groups with impossible bounds give zero counts and can be discarded.

These row groups and two-element pairs meet the hypotheses of Kahn and Neiman’s Lemma 10. Its central comparison for the red size in $W$ is precisely the comparison at $t$ versus $t+1$ free distinguished positions. Each other pair contributes one red slot. The middle count is at least $(t+1)/t$ times the upper count. Color reversal gives the identical lower count at $t-1$. Divide by the fixed refinement multiplicities, multiply by the common cell weights, and sum. Since $\binom{2t}t/\binom{2t}{t-1}=(t+1)/t$, this is the normalized antipodal-pairs inequality under every pinning with positive total mass. This argument adapts the two-color construction in the cited paper. [Kahn and Neiman, Lemma 10 and Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=11)

For each one-column marginal, possible integer sizes form an interval. To see this, consider flows from a source to columns, then to rows on positive-weight cells with capacity one, and through row arcs to a sink with lower and upper capacities $L_i,U_i$. Close the network by an unrestricted return arc. Fix all other column supplies. A convex combination of flows at the two end sizes gives a feasible fractional flow at any intermediate integer size. Integral circulation with integer bounds gives an integral flow there. The conditional antipodal-pairs theorem now gives ultra-log-concavity of full order $n$ for this column size. Identically zero fibers satisfy the local inequalities directly. [Kahn and Neiman, Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=12)

## Comparable profiles

With available neighbors $C_i$ at row $i$, the support of the quota vector is characterized by the two cut inequalities below for every column subset $J$.

$$
\sum_i\max(0,L_i-|C_i\setminus J|)
\ \le\sum_{j\in J}q_j\ \le\sum_i\min(U_i,|C_i\cap J|).
$$

For the circulation just described, use a return arc of capacity greater than the sum of all upper bounds. The cut inequality requires that the sum of lower bounds entering a set not exceed the sum of upper bounds leaving it. If neither source nor sink belongs to the set, take its columns to be $J$ and minimize over its rows. This gives the upper bound. If both belong, apply the cut to the complement of a set of columns $J$ and a set of rows. Maximization over these rows gives the lower bound. A cut with the sink inside and the source outside has the unrestricted return arc leaving it. A cut with only the source inside has zero total incoming lower bounds. Integral circulation proves sufficiency. In particular every profile between two comparable positive profiles has positive weight, even for forbidden cells.

For distinct coordinates the unnormalized counts satisfy $A(q)A(q+e_i+e_j)\le A(q+e_i)A(q+e_j)$ under exactly these weights and intervals. The proof fixes the two-color cell data, adds two dummy rows, and applies Kahn and Neiman’s orientation inequality with symmetric lower degree requirements on every real row. The binomial denominators agree on the two sides. Combining this mixed comparison with the one-column theorem makes the normalized extension ratio nonincreasing along every coordinatewise path on its positive support. The cut characterization supplies the needed intermediate profiles between $q$ and $p+e_j$. Applying this ratio comparison to pairs of parallel unit steps on the paths from common quota $k-1$ to $k$ and from $k$ to $k+1$ proves the diagonal inequality. [Weighted mixed comparison](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/106ccf3/topics/balanced-arrays/orientation-normalization.md)

On the interior of the positive diagonal support, the raw second quotient is at least $[(1+1/k)(1+1/(n-k))]^d\ge (1+2/n)^{2d}$. After removing the common power of the variable, a strictly positive coefficient polynomial whose internal second quotients exceed four has simple negative roots. At the increasing geometric radii $\sqrt{a_{k-1}/a_{k+1}}$ the two adjacent terms each have magnitude less than half the middle one. The remaining terms on either side pair to nonnegative signed tails, giving alternating signs and the full root count. Supports of size one or two follow directly.

## A marginal with nonreal zeros

Take four rows with respective allowed intervals $[0,1],[1,3],[2,2],[2,2]$, three columns and all weights one. Fix the second column sum to two and the third to three. Extracting the coefficient of $x^2y^3$ in $(1+t+x+y)((1+t)(1+x)(1+y)-1)(tx+ty+xy)^2$ gives $1+11t+14t^2+5t^3$. Its cubic discriminant is $-695$, so it has a nonreal conjugate pair. This example separates the column marginal theorem from a stronger real-rootedness statement. Alexandersson’s conjecture concerns equal quotas in all columns and a common upper row bound.
