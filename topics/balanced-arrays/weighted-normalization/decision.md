# Decision point for the balanced-arrays proof

The weighted profile theorem supplies a sharp lower bound on adjacent coefficient ratios. A proof of the full conjecture by demanding a ratio above four at every index is impossible, even with unit weights and a proper row cap. In the already settled case of 12 rows, three columns and cap two, the internal coefficients at indices two, three and four are $271590$, $8717280$ and $73236240$. The raw second quotient at index three is

$$
\frac{8717280^2}{271590\cdot73236240}=\frac{87225632}{22830843}<4 .
$$

Counting labeled rows and using independent inclusion–exclusion on full rows give the same nine coefficients. Eight exact sign changes at negative dyadic points certify eight distinct negative roots. This replay identifies the limitation of a sufficient condition. [Reproducible checks](check-separation.py) · [Complete coefficients and sign probes](separation-output.txt) · [Known corank-one theorem](../no-full-row.md)

For the original conjecture, the next question is whether the neighboring even terms at the geometric coefficient radii compensate for a central second quotient below four, uniformly over the remaining feasible parameters. The reviewed million-row proof uses seven central terms and both lower and upper bounds on neighboring curvatures. A uniform comparison or a counterexample to that geometric sign pattern would decide whether to continue using those radii. Such a counterexample would rule out those probes and would still require a separate exact root count to disprove real-rootedness. [Finite sign argument](../marked-row-likelihood/finite.md)

There is a separate decision for heterogeneous weights. The one-column normalization holds for all row intervals. The inclusion coupling used to turn a comparison of marked masks into a likelihood comparison of their sizes currently reaches upper cap three. With cap four the first untreated local constraint is a vertex of degree six with permitted outdegrees two, three and four. Pairing two incoming and two outgoing incidences has respective multiplicities $12,18,12$, so it cannot be summed with a common normalization. Proving inclusion for the actual constrained orientations or finding a certified Hall obstruction would distinguish the possibilities. This auxiliary extension is unnecessary for applying the exchangeable-row inequality to Alexandersson’s original unit-weight model. ## Comparison with other screened open problems

At the 26 November 2026 checkpoint, the project had three open conjecture issues. The comparison uses each original conjecture and the strongest result recorded in its issue.

| Conjecture | Next mathematical test | What success would establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-cycle permutation excedances | In Alexandersson’s insertion recurrence, bound the lagged term at the old roots when the cycle weight is one in both consecutive rows. Tests with different weights already have exact obstructions. | An inductive sign inequality at all the old roots would propagate consecutive interlacing through the remaining sizes. The existing proof already covers every threshold for $n\ge10^{20}$, every size at threshold three, and the two-cycle range. [Tracking issue](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/4) · [Insertion inequality](../../long-cycle-excedances/insertion-lag.md) |
| Balanced arrays | Prove signed compensation from neighboring coefficients for the actual equal-quota, unit-weight arrays, or exhibit an exact failure of the geometric sign test. | A uniform positive sign would resolve the remaining root question. A failure would decide against that choice of probes. The example above excludes a proof by strict factor-four separation at every index. [Tracking issue](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2) |
| Ordered set partitions by large blocks | Control the outermost roots as both the threshold and size vary, with enough separation to compare consecutive sizes. | This would bridge the joint-limit phase argument toward a uniform theorem. Fixed thresholds already have eventual interlacing; thresholds two through four are settled at every size. [Tracking issue](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/1) · [Ordered model in SymCat](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#openProblemsSetPartitions) |

**Decision.** Give the next proof attempt to the shared-weight insertion inequality for long-cycle excedances. Its success would close the remaining induction, and evaluating it at the actual old roots gives a direct test of the proposed mechanism. For balanced arrays, retain the weighted theorem and the sharp obstruction above. Revisit the original problem when an upper bound for neighboring curvatures can be combined with the lower bounds uniformly, or an exact sign failure points to better probes. The six-edge coupling problem remains a separate follow-up for heterogeneous arrays. For ordered large blocks, revisit the joint-limit route when there is a uniform estimate for the last root, including its ordering across consecutive sizes. [Long-cycle conjecture and recurrence](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#openProblemsPermutations)
