# Provenance and scope

Per Alexandersson asks whether every common-quota polynomial for labeled binary arrays with a common row cap and unit weights has only real roots. The weighted, row-specific theorem here supplies a curvature bound and an explicit sufficient condition. Its nonreal marginal example concerns a different polynomial. [Original statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Existing tracking issue](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2)

Jeff Kahn and Michael Neiman developed the conditional antipodal-pairs criterion in *A strong log-concavity property for measures on Boolean algebras*, first posted on 1 July 2009. In *Conditional negative association for competing urns*, first posted on 5 January 2010, Lemma 8 gives the orientation inequality, Lemma 10 the normalized central comparison for two families of blocks, and Theorem 11 quotes their antipodal-pairs criterion for ultra-log-concavity. The numbering follows the public preprint. Their proof of equation (15) treats competing urns with an individual choice of one urn per ball. For the present matrix model a row can select several columns. Refining the other columns by opposite-color matchings gives exactly the block system of their Lemma 10. The central comparison and the implication to ultra-log-concavity are theirs. The mixed-coordinate inequality is a direct specialization of their Lemma 8. [Antipodal-pairs paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2011.02.007) · [Competing-urns paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20395) · [Lemmas 8, 10 and Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9)

Jonathan Leake and Maryam Mohammadi Yekta’s *Log-concavity and Approximate Counting for Totally Unimodular Polytopes* first appeared on 30 September 2026. Theorem 3.1 gives an unnormalized coefficientwise midpoint inequality for bounded unimodular fibers. Slack arcs encode the row intervals here. The theorem does not assert the full binomial normalization for the single-column numerical weights. David Wagner's subgraph-counting results give zero-free sectors under assumptions on vertex polynomials, and real-rooted total-edge counts for certain degree intervals. Brändén and Huh describe the equivalence between full-order ultra-log-concavity and Lorentzianity of the bivariate homogenization in Example 2.26. [Leake–Mohammadi Yekta, v2, 24 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39917v2#S3.SS1) · [Wagner, Theorem 3.2 and Example 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1659#page=8) · [Brändén–Huh, Example 2.26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.03719#page=22)

The repository previously proved the full binomial normalization for unweighted exchangeable rows, the mixed inequality for weighted rows, and a coefficientwise factorial normalization for arbitrary weights. The joint result here uses an antipodal comparison for the entire distinguished column to recover its binomial factor at numerical nonnegative weights. For the marked-row corollary we use the previously reviewed inclusion coupling up to cap three, derived from Swee Hong Chan’s Lemma 3.1.
