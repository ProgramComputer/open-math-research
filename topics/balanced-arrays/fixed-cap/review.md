# Independent referee report on the fixed-cap uniform theorem

**Verdict.** I find the written proof sound, taking the cited preliminary theorems as inputs. It proves that for each fixed cap $r$ there is one threshold $N_r$ that works for every dimension $d\ge\max(r,2)$. It proves simplicity as well as negativity in that range. I found no substantive gap in the six supplied files. This is a partial theorem for Alexandersson’s conjecture. The conjecture requires real-rootedness for every $n\ge0$ and every $1\le r\le d$. The proof leaves an unspecified finite set of possible exceptions at each fixed cap and gives no interlacing conclusion. This review was agent-assisted. I read the six supplied files, checked the original statements independently, and checked the scope of the cited preliminary results. I did not consult other referee reports. [Original issue](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005) · [Author’s conjecture page](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

## Scope and uniformity

The counted objects, the row and column labels, and the exponent of $q$ agree with the original statement. For every $0\le k\le D=\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$, a feasible array can be constructed by taking $dk$ consecutive positions in the cyclic list of row labels and using consecutive blocks of $k$ positions for the $d$ columns. Each column uses distinct labels and every row is used at most $\lceil dk/n\rceil$ times. This gives the positive coefficients through degree $D$ and justifies taking logs and the sign at infinity. Degree zero and degree one are immediate. The $d=r=1$ polynomial $(1+q)^n$ separately gives the original claim in dimension one.

If the uniform threshold failed, there would be exceptions with $n\to\infty$ at one cap. The fixed-pair theorem is stated for every proper cap and every fixed $d\ge2$. The unrestricted multiplier proof works for all $n$ when $d=r\ge2$ and establishes simplicity there. Thus the exceptions must have $d\to\infty$. The rank-dependent result covers $d\ge C_r\sqrt{n/\log n}$ once $n$ is large, so on the remaining sequence $d^2/n\to0$. This uses each prerequisite only in its stated range. At bounded $n$, the orientation inequality gives strict coefficient separation for $d\ge n$. Consequently each cap has finitely many possible counterexamples, and the caps of distinct counterexamples would tend to infinity. ## Coefficient estimates at the ends

For the lower estimate, the total is $M=di\asymp d^2$ and the margin maxima are at most $r$ and $i\asymp d$. Hence their product is $o(M^{2/3})$. The normalized column variance is zero and the normalized row variance is uniformly bounded by $r/(1-i/n)$, so the extra hypothesis of Greenhill, McKay and Wang’s Corollary 5.2 holds uniformly. Any permitted row profile with total $di$ is feasible. Assign consecutive blocks of lengths $s_j$ to the rows in a cyclic list of the $d$ column labels. The profile average can therefore run over all profiles in the scalar coefficient. On the conditional event the identity $R=M(1-\mu)+U$ gives the displayed cancellation, and the one-row deletion bound gives $\mathbb E[U\mid T_n=M]/M=O(\mu)$. This avoids an error from conditioning on an event of vanishing probability. The exact adjacent ratios of $K_i$ give a **positive** $d/(2k)$ shift to $\log t_k$ and a vanishing central second difference, as needed. As a separate check, at $r=1$ the exact coefficient $n!/((n-dk)!(k!)^d)$ gives the same positive shift. Here $w_k=k/(n-dk)$ and the factorial ratio involving $n-dk$ contributes $-d\log(n-dk)+o(1)$ on the lower window. [Greenhill, McKay and Wang, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 5.2](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/irregular-final.pdf#page=33)

For the upper estimate, the homogeneous transverse covariance parameter is $\Lambda_*=i(d-r)/(d-1)$. The difference from any allowed profile is at most $re/[d(d-1)]$, so the difference of logarithms times $d$ is $O(e/M)=o(1)$ even at exact saturation. The fixed-profile estimate has the correct $\sqrt d$ factor. The projected period basis has squared covolume $(2\pi)^{2(d-1)}/d$. Independently, at $r=1$ the exact balance probability for a profile of total $M=dk$ is $M!/(d^M(k!)^d)$, which gives $\sqrt d(2\pi k)^{-(d-1)/2}(1+o(1))$ under $k/d\to\infty$. In the proof for general fixed $r$, the Gaussian cubic moment bound uses cancellation of the covariance row sums. The exterior bound has a negative exponent of order $M/d$ that dominates the dimension factor. At the last few quotas, the scalar log-concavity relative to $\binom{n+e-1}{e}$ yields the stated positive, diverging curvature. The ratio-expectation argument correctly shifts the law of $j$ upward and takes the expectation of a decreasing function, including at the support changes.

## Moving saddles and sign tests

The product constraint leaves exactly the common column exponents in the integral, with sign $(-1)^k$. The two phase limits are $-\nu\phi$ and $T\pi$. They suffice to select strictly ordered first hits for all internal levels. The phase determinant contributes $-\nu\arg\lambda_\phi$. In the lower window it leads to the quota shift $y-k=1/2+o(1)$, and on the upper window it gives $y-k=o(1)$. Together with $\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}(d\log w_x)=b/V_x$, these give precisely the two radius matches required to combine phase and geometric nodes.

In the interior the two assumptions $n\mu/d^2\to\infty$ and $n(r-\mu)/d^2\to\infty$ yield $nV/d^2\to\infty$. The common-arc exterior estimate loses one Gaussian factor through elimination of a coordinate and integration of its free anchor. Its corresponding logarithmic loss is negligible against $n\mu/d$. On the short arcs, the convolution bound preserves the leading transverse volume up to a constant and $\exp(O(d\tau^2+d^2/(n\mu)))$. This precision matters when $nV/d^2$ diverges slowly. The nonprincipal sum is a Gaussian sum over the discrete phases, so no additional factor $d$ is needed. The principal cutoff tends to zero and the complement of a fixed small cube is covered by the coordinate tail estimate. The local Gaussian calculation then has relative error $o(1)$ in this moving-radius regime. The Euclidean hyperplane density of normalized constrained-torus Haar measure is $(2\pi)^{-(d-1)}/\sqrt d$, as follows from the free-coordinate graph $p_d=-\sum_{j<d}p_j$ whose Gram determinant is $d$. This agrees with the leading term used in the main argument.

The near-end sign bound has both outward tails starting with even offsets and decreasing magnitudes. At positive finite edge scale the limiting discrete Gaussian alternating sum is strictly positive by Poisson summation. At the two switches of radius type, the log spacing of adjacent geometric nodes tends to one, so the $o(1)$ phase-radius replacement preserves order. Every possible bad sequence has a subsequence with the two scaled endpoint distances converging in $[0,\infty]$. The zero, finite-positive and double-infinite cases cover it, with the selected nodes valid in each case. The $D$ disjoint sign-change intervals therefore exhaust the degree.

## Formal coverage and editorial suggestions

The package supplies no Lean or Rocq theorem for the uniform fixed-cap result. The cited Lean record describes supporting lemmas for other balanced-array results. I found no formal instantiation that checks the moving-radius estimates, the fixed-profile local limit, the mixed radius ordering, or the uniform conclusion. The status of this stated result is **⬜ None**. [Supporting Lean record](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/ba7ddfd7efb37cac33769f4fdfed749e1b5bf58a/topics/balanced-arrays/lean/README.md)

I suggest including the two short cyclic-fill constructions at their first use, to make positivity and fixed-profile feasibility explicit in this package. I would also explicitly identify the fixed small cube as lying inside the common-arc chart by taking each deviation at most half the common-arc tolerance. These are editorial improvements.

## Editorial follow-up

The proof and lower-end estimate now give the cyclic fill explicitly, both for positivity at every quota and for feasibility of every row profile used in the coefficient comparison. The interior sign argument now takes the local cube radius at most half the common-arc tolerance. The manuscript includes both clarifications.
