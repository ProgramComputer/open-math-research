# Independent agent-assisted referee report

## Verdict and scope

I accept the proposed quadratic row theorem and its logarithmic-cap corollary as written mathematical results, subject to minor presentation clarifications below. I found no substantive mathematical gap in the anonymous submission. I checked its dependencies against the pinned statements, and checked the application of the external margin enumeration against the authors’ paper.

Per Alexandersson asks whether the common-column-sum generating polynomial for labeled binary arrays with a uniform row cap is real-rooted for every $`d\ge1`$, $`1\le r\le d`$ and $`n\ge0`$. The manuscript uses exactly that polynomial and proves the stronger conclusion of $`\lfloor nr/d\rfloor`$ simple negative roots in the regime $`d\ge2`$ and $`n\ge C d^2`$ for an absolute existential $`C`$. The separate $`d=1`$ formula agrees with the original definition. The corollary also proves simplicity and negativity for every dimension $`d\ge2`$ when $`n`$ is sufficiently large and $`r\le c\log n`$ for an absolute $`c>0`$. These are partial results for the original conjecture. They establish no consecutive interlacing and leave parameters outside the covered ranges open. [Symmetric Functions Catalog statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Author’s GitHub issue](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005)

## Main argument

The pinned fixed-cap theorem excludes bounded $`d`$ on that sequence, and the binomial multiplier result covers $`r=d\ge2`$, including simplicity. - The two positive trigonometric sums lie in a sector of width strictly below $`\pi`$ from angle zero through $`\pi/d`$. The limiting phase as the real tilt goes to zero is $`-\nu\pi/d`$, since the numerator starts at size one. At infinite tilt the phases of the numerator and denominator of $`\lambda`$ agree, since $`G(r)>0`$, so the limiting phase is $`T\pi`$. Continuity gives strictly ordered first hitting points, without a monotonicity hypothesis. The centered characteristic error is $`O(V/d^2)`$ in argument. With $`V\le C\sqrt d\min(\mu,h)`$ this gives (1), which implies $`y=O(d)`$ when $`k=O(d)`$ and $`y/d\to\infty`$ when $`k/d\to\infty`$. The latter implication uses $`\min(y,T-y)\le y`$, so it also applies near the upper edge.
- In the transition $`k/d\to c_0\in(0,\infty)`$, $`\mu=O(d^2/n)`$ at the phase node. Reweighting by $`G`$ leaves expected distance $`O(\mu)`$ from size one. The centered cubic angular error is $`O(n\mu/d^3)=o(1)`$, and the $`G`$-weighted angle contributes $`\nu\phi+O(\mu)`$ after subtracting the denominator angle. This yields the positive shift $`y(v_k)-k=1/2+o(1)`$. Integrating the inverse-tilt derivative $`d^2/(nV_x)`$ proves (2) with its stated sign.
- The orientation comparison supplies strict coefficient log-concavity on the support. At $`t_k`$ the two adjacent terms have normalized size $`\Gamma_k^{-1/2}`$, and the outer alternating tails have nonnegative sums, so the range $`k/d\to0`$ gives the required sign. At positive finite $`k/d`$ the normalized two-sided limits are $`e^{-j^2/(2c_0)}`$. Both tails are bounded by their first terms times geometric-series factors, using outward ratio monotonicity and the limiting ratios $`e^{-(2L+1)/(2c_0)}`$ after a fixed distance $`L`$. This justifies the summation limit. Poisson summation gives a strictly positive limit for every fixed $`c_0>0`$. At the switch the logarithmic gap between the two geometric points tends to one, while replacing the second with its phase point changes the logarithm by $`o(1)`$. The endpoint signs and subsequence trichotomy then imply all $`D`$ simple negative roots for sufficiently large $`n/d^2`$. ## Lower-quota margin comparison

For $`i=k+O(1)`$ one has $`M=di\asymp d^2`$ and $`\mu=M/n\to0`$. For caps at least $`L=\lceil\log d\rceil`$, the union bound is on occupied $`(L+1)`$-subsets of a row. Its expectation is at most $`M\mu^L/(L+1)!`$, which tends to zero uniformly in the cap. The analogous uncapped single-row tail is small enough even after multiplying relative mean error by $`d`$. This verifies the comparison with the unrestricted binomial coefficient ratios.

For smaller caps, Greenhill, McKay and Wang’s Theorem 1.3 requires the product of maximum margin entries to be at least one and $`o(M^{2/3})`$. Here it is at most $`ri=O(d\log d)`$. The extra condition of Corollary 5.2 is $`(1+\mu_2)(1+\nu_2)=O(M^{1/3})`$ for its normalized centered margin moments. Here $`\nu_2=0`$ and $`\mu_2=R/[M(1-p)]\le r/(1-p)`$, with $`p=i/n`$. The exponent is therefore $`-1/2+R/[2M(1-p)]+o(1)`$ with error uniform in the profile. Cyclic assignments realize each profile. The formula’s product and quotient of binomial factors agree with the given $`K_i`$ and tilted profile weights. [Greenhill, McKay and Wang, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 5.2](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/irregular-final.pdf#page=33)

For the conditioning step the zero and one atoms give the same Fourier envelope under both the original and normalized reweighted laws. Hence any mixture of $`n-1`$ of these laws has point masses $`O(M^{-1/2})`$. Combining that bound with the product telescope costs only $`Z^n-1=O(\mu)`$ relative to the local central probability, which is of order $`M^{-1/2}`$. This controls profiles with larger $`U`$ without assuming that $`U/M=o(1)`$ holds deterministically. The exact ratio
$`K_i/K_{i-1}`$ has the stated logarithmic expansion $`-(d-1)/(2i)+O(d/n+d/i^2)`$. When the sign is reversed in the centered first difference for $`\log t_k`$, its contribution is $`+d/(2k)+o(1)`$. The second difference tends to zero. The tilted entropy contributes its stated first derivative, while its second derivative tends to $`-1/c_0`$ on each fixed quota window. The local probabilities change by a factor tending to one between adjacent quotas. These facts give both coefficient limits as claimed.

## Angular estimates

- The normalized integral uses the translated determinant-one torus, with Euclidean covolume $`\sqrt d(2\pi)^{d-1}`$. The monomial average is zero unless all column exponents agree, in which case the product constraint gives $`(-1)^k`$. The Gaussian determinant factor in both new notes agrees with this normalization.
- In the interior range, the modal-atom argument gives a positive variance lower bound depending only on the fixed cutoff $`h_0`$. The success/failure comparison gives $`a\ge V/d`$, and $`\mu\ge1/10`$ gives $`a\ge 1/(11d)`$. The log-concave difference law has survival ratio decreasing in the initial index, so iteration from the Chebyshev scale produces the stated moment and tail bounds. The mixture of centered discrete uniforms retains a fixed fraction of the second moment at widths $`O_{h_0}(\sqrt{1+V})`$. This gives the far radial gap. The fourth-moment bound yields the near-zero derivative gap and therefore the comparison with $`\pi/d`$. For nonprincipal odd phases the loss $`nV(t^2-t_*^2)`$ absorbs the transverse factor $`O(d(1+V)t^2)`$, and the remaining bound sums to $`o(1)`$ because $`nV/d^2\to\infty`$.
- In both new angular estimates the disjoint-pair construction gives a modulus loss at a fixed size from the first two moments of the squared chord sum. Averaging gives the stated loss in $`aD_z`$. In the chart complement, integrating one share of the chord penalty in relative coordinates supplies the full $`(na)^{-(d-1)/2}`$ scale. The fixed-pin map is onto and pushes normalized Haar measure to product Haar measure. The other share gives an exponential factor $`e^{-cna}`$, which dominates the $`e^{O(d)}`$ comparison cost and the principal radial loss. This avoids a logarithmic loss in the row threshold. The principal branch of the quadratic coefficient lies in the right half-plane along the segment from $`0`$ to $`t_*`$. It is the same branch as the continued argument used in the phase-node definition.
- I checked the second through fourth transverse moments against the conditional inclusion probabilities. The quartic bound separates $`aQ_4`$ from $`a^2Q_2^2`$. The former has a bounded exponential moment on the local ball after projecting independent Gaussians to the zero-sum hyperplane. The latter has exponent $`O(U^2/n)`$ for chi-square $`U`$ and has bounded exponential moment on the ball, by splitting at a sufficiently large multiple of $`d`$. For the signed cubic, Wick expansion cancels the linear covariance sum, so the relevant norm is $`O(\sqrt d(na)^{-3/2})`$. The integrated errors are thus bounded by terms of order $`\sqrt{d/(na)}`$, $`d/(na)`$ and $`d^2/n`$, all tending to zero. The same argument supports the interior note’s fixed large $`C_*`$ consequence with an error made small by increasing $`C_*`$. The main theorem needs only the diverging-ratio form.
- The inherited small-mean theorem’s moving-tilt estimates are applicable uniformly for a varying cap. The uniform size moments follow from the factorial tail. They give the required cubic and quartic control with scale $`\mu/d`$. The empty row and singletons give the off-arc suppression, which, after integrating the other coordinates, only requires $`n\mu/d^2\to\infty`$. Within the arc the one-dimensional convolution majorant and the diagonal gap sum the nonprincipal contributions. Wick’s estimate controls the principal cubic there as well. The second height condition in the original fixed-cap localization is automatic here since $`r-\mu\ge9/10`$ and $`n/d^2\to\infty`$.
