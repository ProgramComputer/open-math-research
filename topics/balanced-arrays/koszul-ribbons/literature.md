# Koszul array diagonal and ribbon positivity

Screen date: 2026-11-26. Supporting algebra for [balanced-array issue #2](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2).

## Algebra and scope

Over any field $`K`$, take $`B_j=K[x_{1j},\ldots,x_{nj}]/(x_{1j}^2,\ldots,x_{nj}^2)`$. The Segre product $`B=B_1\circ\cdots\circ B_d`$ has basis the balanced labeled binary arrays, graded by quota. Its multiplication is union or zero on repeated cells. Killing a subset of quota-one monomial generators realizes the row cap: kill the quota-one arrays already violating a row cap. Every larger array violating a row cap has such a quota-one divisor, obtained by including all of its cells of that row in the divisor and choosing one arbitrary available cell in each remaining column. Conversely a valid array admits no such divisor. Thus the quotient $`A`$ has the claimed basis and multiplication. The same argument works for row-dependent upper caps. A lower row quota alone does not give such an algebra by this argument, since taking subsets need not preserve it.

The quota-one array monomials minimally generate $`B`$ because any nonempty balanced support partitions into layers with one cell in each column. Colons by a generator are monomial: multiplication by it is injective on surviving monomial supports. If a support $`M`$ is in $`(S):u`$ because $`Mu=0`$, then it shares a cell with $`u`$, and a quota-one divisor of $`M`$ containing that cell lies in the colon. Otherwise some generator $`g\in S`$ divides $`Mu`$. Select a quota-one divisor $`v`$ of $`M`$ containing every cell of $`g`$ absent from $`u`$ and complete any other columns arbitrarily. Then $`g`$ divides $`vu`$, so $`v`$ belongs to the colon. This proves strong Koszulness with respect to the named generators. Quotienting by an ideal $`J`$ generated by a subset of those generators preserves it: the colon in the quotient by a surviving generator is the image of the colon by its lift of the corresponding ideal plus $`J`$. These arguments apply over any field and do not use dividing by multiplicities. In particular $`A`$ is strongly Koszul.

Herzog–Hibi–Restuccia, *Strongly Koszul algebras*, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, supply the terminology, linear resolutions and implication to Koszulness. Proposition 2.3(b) states Segre closure for rings of homogeneous semigroups and explicitly leaves arbitrary strongly Koszul algebras open. The present square-zero rings have nilpotents and are not in their affine semigroup-ring setting. Ordinary Segre Koszulness is older and appears in Sam–VandeBogert Proposition 4.18(3), citing Polishchuk–Positselski and Backelin. The elementary quota-one colon proof gives the stronger property needed for the quotient description. Failla Proposition 3.2 records the linear resolution of ideals generated by subsets of a strongly Koszul basis. Its Segre application studies a squarefree Veronese **subring of a polynomial ring**, distinct from the square-zero quotients here. [HHR DOI](https://doi.org/10.7146/math.scand.a-14287), [HHR paper](https://www.mscand.dk/article/download/14287/12284), [Failla](https://www.opuscula.agh.edu.pl/vol37/6/art/opuscula_math_3744.pdf), [Sam–VandeBogert](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10408).

## Exact ribbon statement

VandeBogert, *Ribbon Schur functors*, Algebra & Number Theory 19 (2025), 771–834, DOI 10.2140/ant.2025.19.771, **Definition 4.16** defines the ribbon module as a kernel of adjacent multiplication maps. **Definition A.7**, p. 829, gives its cochain complex with summands $`A_{\beta_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{\beta_j}`$ for coarsenings $`\beta`$ of a composition $`\alpha`$, in cohomological degree $`\ell(\alpha)-\ell(\beta)`$. **Theorem A.12**, p. 830, gives exactness in positive degrees for every composition when the quadratic algebra is Koszul. These numbers and the exactness assertion were checked in the version of record. The corresponding degree-zero kernel is $`S_A^\alpha`$. Hence its multicharacter is

$$
\mathrm{ch} S_A^\alpha
=\sum_{\beta\text{ coarsens }\alpha}(-1)^{\ell(\alpha)-\ell(\beta)}
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\beta)}h_{\beta_i}
=s_{\mathrm{ribbon}(\alpha)}[h],
\qquad h_j=a_j(\mathbf w).
$$

The last identity is the ribbon Jacobi–Trudi expansion. Here all multiplication maps are homogeneous for the cell multidegree as well as for quota, so Euler characteristic holds one weight space at a time with no positivity or division assumption on the field. VandeBogert allows associative quadratic algebras; commutativity is unnecessary for this construction. The field case satisfies the underlying finite-dimensional graded-piece hypotheses immediately. Koszul can be read as diagonal $`\mathrm{Tor}^A(K,K)`$ by the published Observation A.9, but the object realizing a general ribbon is the degree-zero refinement kernel, not the full ordinary Tor group. For the all-ones composition the refinement strand specializes to the usual linear bar strand. [VandeBogert DOI](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2025.19.771), [published PDF](https://msp.org/ant/2025/19-4/ant-v19-n4-p05-p.pdf), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14086).

Sam–VandeBogert, *From total positivity to pure free resolutions*, arXiv:2408.10408, **Proposition 4.23** compares the ribbon module with their generalized Schur construction under the stronger hypothesis that the Koszul algebra is $`G`$-totally positive. **Remark 4.24** explicitly says every Koszul algebra is partially $`G`$-totally positive in the ribbon sense. Their existence theorem for general skew Schur modules is not needed here, and its semisimplicity and Jacobi–Trudi structure assumptions should not be imported into the simpler refinement-complex proof. [Primary preprint, §4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10408#page=18).

## Boundary of the implication

The commutative ring $`K[c,x,y,z]/(c^2,x^2,y^2,z^2,cx,cy,cz)`$ is strongly Koszul by the same elementary variable-colon rule for a quadratic monomial quotient. Its surviving monomials are independent vertex sets of the claw $`K_{1,3}`$, and its Hilbert series is $`1+4t+3t^2+t^3`$. The cubic discriminant is $`-31`$, so it has a nonreal conjugate pair. For a concrete failure of full Jacobi–Trudi positivity, the straight partition $`(2,2,2,2)`$ gives determinant $`-5`$ under $`h=(1,4,3,1,0,\ldots)`$. This example separates the ribbon consequence from full total positivity or real-rootedness.
