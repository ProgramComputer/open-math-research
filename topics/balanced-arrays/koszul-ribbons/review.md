# Independent end-to-end review of the balanced-array ribbon proof

**Verdict:** Accepted. I found no mathematical gap in the written proof. This is a review of the supporting theorem about the coefficient sequence, with no claim of a proof of the original real-rootedness conjecture.

## Checks

1. **Generalized row families and the presentation.** Downward closure gives a monomial basis of admissible binary supports and lets every balanced support factor into admissible one-per-column tuples. The unrestricted diagonal is the Segre product of the square-zero column rings. An inadmissible balanced support contains a forbidden single-row support, whose cells have distinct columns and can all be selected in one tuple inside the support. Hence the cap ideal in the unrestricted diagonal is generated by precisely the inadmissible tuples. To verify the quadratic presentation, a repeated cell occurs in two tuple factors and produces a quadratic zero relation. Among products without repetitions, any different assignment of a support to the same number of tuples is obtained by independently permuting the entries of each column, using pairwise swaps. These quadratic swaps preserve the support and cannot link two different supports. Eliminating the forbidden degree-one variables from this presentation leaves quadrics in the surviving generators. This does not use that the families are identical, matroidal, or defined by a cardinality cap.

2. **Colons and the filtration.** For an ideal generated by a subset of tuple basis elements, ideal membership of surviving supports is equivalent to containing a generating tuple. Multiplication by a fixed admissible tuple takes every surviving support injectively to its union, so no cancellation enlarges the colon beyond its indicated monomial basis. In each of the three cases, the proposed divisor can be completed to one cell in each column and its complement remains admissible. In the forbidden-row case the full offending row is preserved in the smaller product, since all cells of the first support from that row were selected. In the surviving-product case the selected divisor recovers all cells of the ideal generator that are absent from the colon generator. Strong Koszulness and the Koszul filtration follow from these colons. The ideals and multiplications are homogeneous in quota and in the full cell multidegree, with the usual degree shift when recording cyclic quotient multiplication. The zero-generator case gives the base field directly. [Herzog–Hibi–Restuccia, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.7146/math.scand.a-14287)

3. **Ribbon coefficients.** The cited refinement cochain complex starts with the tensor product indexed by the full composition. Deleting one cut between adjacent parts gives the corresponding multiplication map to a coarser composition. Its degree-zero kernel is the ribbon module and the positive-degree cohomology vanishes for the quadratic Koszul algebra. The alternating weight-space dimensions agree with the ribbon Jacobi–Trudi expansion. Each map preserves the full cell multidegree, and the weight decomposition here can be regarded as a formal multigrading over any field, including finite fields. No semisimplicity of a group action or characteristic-zero character theory is required. [VandeBogert, Definition 4.16, Definition A.7 and Theorem A.12](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2025.19.771)

4. **Binomial basis in the row count.** For identical uniform caps, a selected set of row labels supports a subcomplex selected by exact row support, since every nonzero multiplication preserves the weight of the tensor. The terms, maps, and hence the kernel on exactly that row set are carried by relabeling to the corresponding full-support complex on that many rows. This yields a nonnegative integer count for each possible exact row-support size and those counts are independent of the ambient number of rows. The total cell multidegree, including multiplicity from the tensor factors, is $`d|\alpha|`$, so the support-size bound is valid.

5. **Scope and counterexample to the stronger implication.** The ribbon determinant statement is a proper subset of the Toeplitz inequalities needed for general real-rootedness. The claw ring in the proof is a quadratic monomial quotient with variable-generated colons. Its Hilbert polynomial is $`1+4t+3t^2+t^3`$, whose discriminant is $`-31`$. This verifies the claimed limit of strong Koszulness as an abstract hypothesis. The claw ring is separate from the balanced-array model and the proof makes no assertion about real-rootedness, general skew-shape positivity, or ribbon matroids. The original conjecture remains the one for uniform caps with unit cell weights. [Original statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)
