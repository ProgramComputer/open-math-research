# Strongly Koszul balanced-array algebras and ribbon positivity

For each labeled row $`v\in[n]`$, let $`\mathcal F_v`$ be a downward-closed family of subsets of the column set $`[d]`$, containing the empty set. Let $`a_j(\mathbf w)`$ count binary arrays with every column sum $`j`$ and row support in its prescribed family, with the product of the independent occupied cell weights $`w_{vi}`$. Over every field the corresponding quota-graded algebra is strongly Koszul. For any ribbon $`R`$ the specialization $`h_j\mapsto a_j(\mathbf w)`$ makes $`s_R`$ a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients. If all rows have the same uniform cap $`r`$, at unit cell weights each such ribbon specialization has a nonnegative integer expansion in the basis $`\binom ns`$ of polynomials in the row count.

These statements concern the coefficient sequence of labeled balanced arrays. The original uniform-matroid balanced-array conjecture asks for real-rootedness when the families are uniform caps and the weights are one. [Original conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

## The quota-graded algebra

Fix a field $`K`$. In the commutative polynomial ring on variables $`x_{vi}`$, set every cell square to zero and set $`\prod_{i\in U}x_{vi}=0`$ for every $`U\notin\mathcal F_v`$. The quotient has a basis consisting of admissible square-free cell supports. The linear span $`A`$ of supports with equal column counts is a connected graded subalgebra, graded by common column count, or quota. Its multigrading records the exponent of each cell independently. Multiplication is union of the cell supports when they are disjoint and their union is admissible. Otherwise multiplication gives zero.

A tuple is a support with one cell in each column. Every positive-quota balanced support contains an admissible tuple. Pick one of its cells per column. Removing those cells preserves admissibility by heredity and lowers the quota by one. Iteration gives a factorization; thus the admissible tuples generate $`A`$ and form its degree-one basis. The formal multigraded character of $`A_j`$ is $`a_j(\mathbf w)`$, and $`a_0=1`$. If no tuple is admissible then $`A=K`$ and the conclusions follow directly.

The unrestricted balanced algebra is the graded Segre product of $`K[x_{1i},\ldots,x_{ni}]/(x_{1i}^2,\ldots,x_{ni}^2)`$ over the columns $`i`$. The kernel of the quotient to $`A`$ is the ideal generated by inadmissible tuples: any inadmissible balanced support contains a forbidden set of cells in one row, and those cells can be extended to a tuple inside the support because they lie in distinct columns. Conversely every support containing an inadmissible tuple is inadmissible. This also shows the presentation is quadratic. In the unrestricted tuple presentation, a product with a repeated cell is killed by the quadratic product of the two tuples that share that cell. Two ordered products without repeated cells that represent the same support differ by independent permutations of the cells within each column. Swapping the cells of one column between two tuples is a quadratic relation. Together these quadrics identify exactly the products with the same surviving support. Killing inadmissible tuple generators leaves a quadratic presentation on the surviving generators by setting the inadmissible variables to zero in these relations.

## Colons and strong Koszulness

Let $`I`$ be the ideal generated by any subset of the tuple basis, and take a tuple $`v`$ outside that subset. A basis monomial belongs to $`I`$ exactly when it contains a generating tuple. Its complement supplies the quotient monomial by heredity. Multiplication by $`v`$ is injective on its nonzero basis images, since subtracting the cells of $`v`$ recovers the original support. Hence $`(I:v)`$ is spanned by the basis monomials $`m`$ with $`mv=0`$ or $`mv\in I`$. The unit does not belong to the colon.

For each such $`m`$ choose a tuple divisor $`w`$ inside its support as follows.

- If $`m`$ overlaps $`v`$, include one shared cell in $`w`$. Then $`wv=0`$.
- If the supports are disjoint and $`mv=0`$, some row of the union is forbidden. Include in $`w`$ all cells of $`m`$ from that row. They occupy distinct columns. Complete to a tuple using cells of $`m`$ in the remaining columns. The same forbidden row survives in the union with $`v`$, hence $`wv=0`$.
- If $`mv`$ survives and contains a generating tuple $`u`$ of $`I`$, include in $`w`$ every cell of $`u`$ missing from $`v`$. These cells are in $`m`$ and in distinct columns. After completing the tuple, $`wv`$ is admissible as a subarray of $`mv`$ and contains $`u`$. Thus $`wv\in I`$.

In all cases $`w`$ is admissible and its complement in $`m`$ has common quota one less, so it divides $`m`$ as an element of $`A`$. The colon is generated by the tuples it contains. This proves strong Koszulness with respect to the named degree-one basis. It also gives a Koszul filtration: the family of ideals generated by subsets of the basis contains zero and the positive-degree ideal, and deleting any one generator $`v`$ from a nonzero member $`L`$ gives a smaller member $`J`$ with $`L/J`$ cyclic and $`J:L=J:v`$ in the family. In particular $`A`$ is Koszul. [Herzog–Hibi–Restuccia, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.7146/math.scand.a-14287) · [Authors' article](https://www.mscand.dk/article/download/14287/12284)

## Ribbon modules and cell weights

Write a ribbon by its composition $`\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\ell)`$ of positive row lengths, using the order in VandeBogert's refinement complex. A coarsening $`\beta`$ merges adjacent parts of $`\alpha`$. For a quadratic Koszul algebra the refinement cochain complex has a summand $`A_{\beta_1}\otimes_K\cdots\otimes_K A_{\beta_j}`$ in cohomological degree $`\ell-j`$ for each such coarsening of length $`j`$. Its differential is the signed sum of adjacent multiplication maps. VandeBogert proves exactness in positive degrees for every composition, and the degree-zero kernel is the ribbon Schur module $`S_A^\alpha`$. [VandeBogert, *Ribbon Schur functors*, Definition 4.16, Definition A.7 and Theorem A.12, pp. 829–830 for the refinement complex and exactness](https://msp.org/ant/2025/19-4/ant-v19-n4-p05-p.pdf) · [Version of record](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2025.19.771)

Here the differential preserves the cell multidegree. Taking the Euler characteristic separately in each cell multidegree expresses the formal multigraded character of the degree-zero kernel as

$$
\mathrm{ch}\thinspace  S_A^\alpha
=\sum_{\beta\text{ coarsens }\alpha}(-1)^{\ell-\ell(\beta)}
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\beta)} a_{\beta_i}(\mathbf w).
$$

By the ribbon Jacobi–Trudi identity the right side is the indicated specialization of the ribbon Schur function. The coefficients on the left are dimensions of vector spaces, proving the assertion in every characteristic. [Stanley, *Enumerative Combinatorics*, Vol. 2, §7.16](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/ec/)

## Uniform caps and the row count

Take $`\mathcal F_v=\{U:|U|\le r\}`$ for every row and fix $`d,r,\alpha`$. Group the character by the union $`S`$ of occupied row labels in its cell multidegree. In a tensor summand the support of that multidegree is the union of the supports of the factors. A nonzero multiplication preserves it. The part of the complex with exact row support $`S`$ only uses cells on those rows. It is identical, after relabeling, to the full-support part on a set of $`s=|S|`$ rows with the same cap. Thus the total kernel dimension for each such support is a nonnegative integer $`c_s`$ independent of $`n`$ and of the choice of $`S`$. At unit weights this gives

$$
s_{\mathrm{ribbon}(\alpha)}[a(n)]=\sum_{s=0}^{d|\alpha|}c_s\binom ns .
$$

The bound on $`s`$ holds because the sum of the quotas of every tensor summand is $`|\alpha|`$ and its total cell degree is $`d|\alpha|`$.

## Scope of the conclusion

Ribbon positivity concerns the connected skew shapes with no two-by-two square. General straight and skew minors include shapes outside that class. Strong Koszulness alone cannot imply full Toeplitz positivity: the commutative algebra with variables $`c,x,y,z`$, all squares zero and $`cx=cy=cz=0`$, is strongly Koszul by the variable-colon criterion for quadratic monomial ideals. Its Hilbert series is $`1+4t+3t^2+t^3`$, whose discriminant is $`-31`$. This example is an algebraic limitation, not a balanced-array counterexample. The result is distinct from the Narayana theorem for ribbon matroids.
