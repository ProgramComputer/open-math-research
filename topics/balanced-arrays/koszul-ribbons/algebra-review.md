# Independent review of the strong Koszul property for balanced arrays

**Verdict.** The degree-one colon argument is valid. The admissible tuple generators give the strong Koszul property and a Koszul filtration. The cap algebra is also the stated quotient of the Segre product. The proof works over any field.

Let the ambient cell algebra be the commutative polynomial algebra modulo the cell squares and every square-free monomial on $`r+1`$ distinct cells of a single row. Its surviving basis is exactly the capped square-free arrays. The balanced monomials form a graded subalgebra, graded by common quota. In any nonempty valid balanced monomial, choosing a cell in each column gives an admissible degree-one divisor. The complement remains balanced and valid by the upper-cap condition. Iterating proves generation, and the tuple monomials are a basis of degree one.

## The colon

Let $`v`$ be a tuple and let $`I`$ be generated by a subset of the other tuples. A basis monomial lies in $`I`$ exactly when its cell support contains a generating tuple. In that event its complementary support gives the quotient monomial. Multiplication by $`v`$ is injective on surviving basis images, since deleting the cells of $`v`$ recovers the original monomial. Therefore $`(I:v)`$ is the span of the basis monomials whose products are zero or lie in $`I`$. The unit does not belong to this colon, since $`v`$ is outside the degree-one span of $`I`$.

Let $`m`$ be a basis monomial in the colon. If $`m`$ overlaps $`v`$, choose a tuple divisor $`w`$ of $`m`$ containing a shared cell. Then $`wv=0`$. If $`m`$ and $`v`$ are disjoint but their product overflows a row, choose $`r+1-\mathrm{occ}(v,\mathrm{row})`$ of the cells of $`m`$ in that row. They are in distinct columns. Completing them with a cell from each other column of $`m`$ gives a valid tuple divisor $`w`$, and that row still overflows in $`wv`$. If $`mv`$ survives and contains a generator $`u`$ of $`I`$, choose the cell of $`u`$ from $`m`$ in every column where it differs from the cell of $`v`$, then complete to a tuple divisor $`w`$ of $`m`$. The product $`wv`$ is a valid subarray of $`mv`$ and contains $`u`$, so $`wv\in I`$. These cases give a tuple divisor of every colon basis monomial. The tuple divisors need only belong to some subset of the full tuple basis. In fact $`v`$ itself always lies in the colon because $`v^2=0`$.

## Filtration and Segre quotient

The family of ideals generated by subsets of the tuple basis contains zero and the positive-degree maximal ideal. For a nonzero member $`L`$, delete a generator $`v`$ to obtain a strict subideal $`J`$. Then $`L/J`$ is generated by the class of $`v`$, and $`J:L=J:v`$ belongs to the family by the colon argument. These are the filtration conditions. Koszulness also follows directly from

$$
0\longrightarrow R/(J:v)(-1)\xrightarrow{\cdot v}R/J\longrightarrow R/L\longrightarrow0.
$$

In the long exact Tor sequence against the field, induct on homological degree and, within one degree, on the number of tuple generators of $`L`$. For a degree other than the homological degree, the terms on either side of $`\mathrm{Tor}_i(R/L,\mathbb Q)`$ vanish by these two inductions. The start in homological degree zero follows because every $`R/L`$ is cyclic in degree zero, and the empty-generator case is the free module $`R`$. Taking $`L`$ to be the positive-degree ideal proves that the field has a linear resolution.

In the uncapped balanced algebra, each column factor is the commutative algebra in its cell variables modulo their individual squares. The uncapped algebra is their graded Segre product. Any invalid balanced monomial has $`r+1`$ cells in some row, all in distinct columns. Those cells can be extended within the monomial to a bad degree-one tuple. Conversely, every surviving multiple of a bad tuple violates the cap. The kernel of the cap quotient is therefore generated by exactly the bad degree-one tuples. When no admissible tuple exists, including zero rows or cap zero, the capped ring is the field and the assertion is immediate.

I independently enumerated small arrays for $`0\le n\le3`$, $`1\le d\le3`$, and every cap. This checked 2,710 annihilator cases and 59,188 singleton-colon cases, along with 206 bad Segre basis terms. The singleton tests iterate over the monomial membership alternatives used in the proof. Arbitrary ideals generated by linear combinations and consequences for ribbons are outside this review.
