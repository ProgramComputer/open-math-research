# Priority screen: an absolute polynomial row cutoff for balanced arrays

Screen date: 2026-11-26.

## Exact statement screened

Let $`a_k`$ count labelled binary $`n\times d`$ matrices with every column sum $`k`$ and every row sum at most $`r`$. The candidate is a single absolute constant $`C`$ such that for every $`d\ge2`$, $`1\le r<d`$, and $`n\ge C d^4\log(d+1)`$, the original unweighted polynomial $`\sum_k a_kq^k`$ has $`\lfloor nr/d\rfloor`$ simple negative roots. One may append full cap $`r=d`$ via its separate known binomial-power case. The candidate uses an existential absolute constant in an explicit dimension-dependent rate. It does not give a numerical constant, apply to all row counts, or require consecutive-row interlacing. It is enough to prove the uniform bound for sufficiently large $`d`$, since a finite set of smaller proper pairs can be absorbed into the absolute constant using the fixed-pair theorem. The author asks for real-rootedness at every $`n,r,d`$. [SymCat original statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays), [author issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005).

## Prior results and comparison

- The author's open issue #2005 describes an unreviewed eventual-root argument for $`U(2,4)`$ without a numerical cutoff. The independent open issue #165 contains bounded checks and approach notes. Neither supplies a growing-dimension cutoff. [Author issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005), [independent issue #165](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/165).
- The project's published fixed-pair theorem gives an existential threshold $`N(d,r)`$ and additionally proves strict consecutive-row interlacing. Its previous proof has no stated polynomial dimension bound independent of rank. [Fixed-pair manuscript at ecd6479](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/ecd64798ac94b6fd81613d1ca6b98ec0d459cb3e/topics/balanced-arrays/fixed-rank-eventual.tex).
- The reviewed and merged #91 gives an existential $`N_r`$ uniform over all dimensions for every **fixed** cap. It captures dimensions growing arbitrarily fast at fixed cap. The proposed result contributes a uniform rate valid simultaneously for growing caps in very tall arrays. Neither quantifier supplies the other parameter region. [Publication #91, merge f7e10f8](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/91), [review and proof PR #90](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/90).
- The merged #89 concerns enough columns relative to rows. It proves the full root count for $`n\ge N_\varepsilon`$, $`d\ge C_\varepsilon\sqrt{nr/\log n}`$ and either $`r\le n^{1/5-\varepsilon}`$ or $`d\ge n^{3/4+\varepsilon}`$. When $`n\ge C d^4\log(d+1)`$ and $`d\to\infty`$, its lower dimension bound fails even at $`r=1`$. The two theorems address different parameter regions. [Publication #89](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/89).
- The project has all-row corank one and the underlying open question remains tracked at #2. Row saturation at fixed low rank is also a fixed-margin enumeration problem, which is a distinct use of the statistic. [Corank-one publication #50](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/50), [project issue #2](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2).

The nearest asymptotic references are classical fixed-margin counts. GMW Theorem 1.3 requires $`st=o(S^{2/3})`$ for maximum degrees $`s,t`$ and total $`S`$, which can fail in the very tall range even at fixed cap near saturation. Canfield–McKay Theorem 1(c) allows extreme aspects when both margins are exactly regular. CGM Theorem 1 and LW Theorem 1.1 require additional dimension, density, or degree-deviation restrictions. These references control counts with prescribed margins and do not claim the required uniform zeros of the capped generating polynomial. [GMW](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/irregular-final.pdf), [Canfield–McKay](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/EJC/Volume_12/PDF/v12i1r29.pdf#page=3), [CGM](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/CGM.pdf), [published LW](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rsa.21105).

No exact external polynomial cutoff or full resolution surfaced in this screen. This is evidence about accessible sources rather than a definitive priority claim. The interior saddle formula is supporting work only. A theorem at this cutoff also needs the endpoint estimates and a matching count of sign changes.
