# Blind referee report on the balanced-array row bound

**Verdict.** Accept the written argument for the stated partial theorem, subject to minor editorial clarifications below. I found no substantive gap. The proof gives a single absolute constant $`C>0`$ such that, for integers $`d\ge2`$, $`1\le r\le d`$, and $`n\ge Cd^4\log(d+1)`$, the balanced-array polynomial has exactly $`\lfloor nr/d\rfloor`$ simple negative zeros. For $`d=1`$ it gives the separate identity $`P_n^{(1,1)}(q)=(1+q)^n`$.

This is an agent-assisted blind review of the main argument and all four submitted supplements. I also checked the conjecture's two primary statements and the cited prior results used to pass from the estimates to the theorem.

## Scope and formal verification

Alexandersson's two statements count labeled zero-one matrices with a common column sum $`k`$ and a row-size upper bound $`r`$. They ask for real-rootedness for every $`n\ge0`$ and $`1\le r\le d`$. The submission concerns the same polynomial and proves the required real-rootedness, with simplicity, throughout the stated tall range for $`d\ge2`$. The range below the cutoff remains in the original conjecture. The constant is existential, so the proof supplies no numerical cutoff for a finite check at a given $`d`$. The collection of uncovered parameters over all $`d`$ is still infinite. [Alexandersson's conjecture list](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Original GitHub issue](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005)

Formal verification for the stated cutoff theorem is **⬜ None**. The submission contains Markdown proofs and no Lean or Rocq theorem or build to assess. My review is of the written mathematics.

## Checks

- **Use of prior results.** The fixed-cap theorem gives precisely the uniform eventual simplicity needed to eliminate any bounded-$`d`$ sequence, since such a sequence involves finitely many caps. The full-cap argument applies the finite Schur–Szegő multiplier to the actual coefficients $`\binom nk^d`$. Its first application gives the Jacobi symbol with simple negative zeros. Later applications preserve simplicity by the interior argument for an invertible linear preserver. The orientation result supplies log-concavity of $`a_k/\binom nk^d`$ for the same labeled arrays. Strict ordinary log-concavity follows from the binomial factor, including the lower bound $`\Gamma_k\ge(1+1/k)^d`$ needed close to zero. The orientation comparison in its citation applies to multigraphs and has the coordinatewise bounds required by the dummy-row and fixed-skeleton reductions. - **Fourier normalization and dense localization.** On the product-negative torus the character for a common exponent $`k`$ has value $`(-1)^k`$, and all other exponent vectors integrate to zero. The real zero-sum hyperplane covering this torus has lattice generated by $`2\pi(e_j-e_d)`$, giving covolume $`\sqrt d(2\pi)^{d-1}`$. In the conditioned count the pinned quotient is different. The orthogonal projections of the pinned unit basis have Gram determinant $`1/d`$, so Haar measure there has density $`\sqrt d/(2\pi)^{d-1}`$ with respect to hyperplane volume. Both prefactors in the submission follow. I checked the pairing estimate via the first and second moments of the sum of squared chords, then checked the interior diagonal gap using the symmetric interval mixture. The adjacent-mass test gives the necessary lower variance bound. Truncating the mixture at $`O(\sqrt{d\log d})`$ loses $`o(V)`$ of its variance by the sub-Gaussian bound. Its low-frequency comparison distinguishes $`\pi/d`$ from $`3\pi/d`$, and its larger-angle comparison exceeds the possible $`O(V/d^2)`$ transverse perturbation. The within-chart expansion gives $`n\bar a/d^2`$ in the exponent outside the radius-$`d^{-1}`$ balls, with $`\bar a\ge1/(11d)`$. For the fixed positive deficit regime used in the main proof the total exterior cost is bounded by $`\exp(O(d\log n)-c_\eta n/d^3)`$. This vanishes for a sufficiently large absolute cutoff after fixing $`\eta=h_0`$. The supplementary $`d^6\log(d+1)`$ range for deficits as low as $`d^{-3}`$ is consistent with the weaker variance lower bound there and is unnecessary for the main cutoff. The conditional subset moment identities through fourth order also passed 140 exact rational checks for dimensions 4 through 8.

- **The two thin angular regimes.** Near saturation, geometric deficit domination is uniform even at $`r=d-1`$, since $`G(r-j)/G(r)\le1+j`$ and $`G(r)\ge1/d`$. The decrease of the real transverse coefficient away from the principal phase costs at most $`\exp(O(1+dh\min(\tau^2,1)))`$ in Gaussian volume. The radial gap of order $`nh(\tau^2-\pi^2/d^2)`$ absorbs that cost, with no prefactor increasing in $`d`$ in the phase sum. Thus an arbitrarily slow divergence $`nh/d^2\to\infty`$ suffices. At the lower end, the empty support and singleton masses localize all the lifted angles to a fixed arc about zero. Retaining half a flagged angle's penalty and integrating the remaining Gaussian factors leaves a relative exponent at most $`-c n\mu/d+O(d+n\mu/d^2+\log d)`$, which tends to $`-\infty`$. Inside a sufficiently small zero arc all averaged phases are in the small-phase range of the cited convolution proof. The submitted bound on $`\mathbb E[S^\ell|L|^m]`$ replaces the bounds involving $`r`$ in the cited proofs. It also gives $`O(\mu/d^j)`$ for fixed $`j`$-coordinate inclusion weights and their phase derivatives. These bounds justify the required Taylor coefficients, quartic separable majorant, Gaussian convolution volume and centered Wick estimate with absolute constants. A sufficiently small fixed cube about each principal phase lies inside the zero arc, and the flagged-coordinate convolution tail controls the difference. - **Upper coefficient estimates and exact saturation.** With total deficit $`e=O(d^2)`$, at least $`n-e`$ rows have size $`r`$. The profile's quadratic precision satisfies $`\Lambda\ge(n-e)/d`$. Also $`\Lambda-\Lambda_*=\sum_j s_j(r-s_j)/(d(d-1))`$, giving the required $`d\log(\Lambda/\Lambda_*)=O(d^3/n)`$. The conditional Gaussian formula therefore averages with uniform relative error, including $`e=0`$. In the positive finite overlap the scalar tilt has $`nh\asymp d^2`$, the tilted local limit has lattice span one from the adjacent masses, and $`E''=-b/V\to-1/c`$ while $`E'''=O(1/d)`$. These estimates produce the curvature and geometric radius claimed. For the final $`o(d)`$ quotas, the decreasing forward ratio after normalization by $`\binom{n+e-1}{e}`$ follows from log-concavity of the $`n-1`$-row counts and the likelihood-ratio ordering in $`j`$. The resulting step-$`d`$ lower bound diverges since $`d\le e=o(d^2)`$ and $`e\ll n`$. It includes a neighbor at $`e-d=0`$. The lower coefficient estimate uses the unrestricted-column collision expectation $`O(d^4/n)`$, which tends to zero uniformly in the cap.

- **Phase levels, overlaps and exhaustion of the degree.** The sector of opening $`r\pi/d<\pi`$ makes both arguments at the principal phase continuous. It gives limits $`-\nu\pi/d`$ and $`T\pi`$ for $`\Psi`$, and continuity orders its first crossings without a monotonicity hypothesis. The variance bounds give the claimed localization of the phase levels in the three quota regimes. At the lower overlap the $`G`$-weighted size distribution concentrates near one, giving $`\nu\arg\lambda_{\pi/d}=1\cdot\nu\pi/d+o(1)`$ while the centered characteristic contributes $`o(1)`$ to the phase. This yields the $`+1/2`$ shift in the tilted quota. The analogous expansion about size $`r`$ gives $`o(1)`$ shift at the upper overlap. Since $`d(\log w_x)'=b/V_x\to1/c`$ on the bounded windows, the signs can be transferred between the phase and geometric radii. For the overlap limit, normalized neighboring terms have equal limit at distance one, and their successive logarithmic differences converge to the discrete Gaussian differences. Strict log-concavity makes the tails summable uniformly by bounding each outward ratio by its value at a fixed large distance. The alternating Gaussian sum is strictly positive by Poisson summation. For the two diverging endpoint curvatures the direct alternating-series bound is positive, and in the middle the saddle phase gives exactly the required parity sign. At the joins the geometric logarithmic spacing converges to one and the phase replacement changes a logarithm by $`o(1)`$. These signs at ordered radii, together with feasibility of all quotas through $`D`$, yield $`D`$ distinct roots by the intermediate value theorem. The subsequence contradiction establishes an absolute $`C`$, since each possible offending sequence has a further subsequence in one of the checked regimes.

## Editorial suggestions

1. In the nearly empty supplement, write the cubic explicitly as $`-\mathrm{i}\,c(t)\sum_j y_j^3/6`$. The bound on the imaginary part applies to $`c(t)`$ in this convention. The real part of the displayed cubic is consequently $`O((\mu/d)|t|\sum_j|y_j|^3)`$. Spell out that the initial singleton zero arc is chosen inside the small-phase range, and give the two-sided comparison of each principal region with fixed cubes. This makes the import of the fixed-cap proof easier to check.
2. In the main reduction, state explicitly that the fixed positive deficit $`\eta=h_0`$ is chosen before the absolute cutoff constant. An explicit one-line derivation of the success/failure variance bound would help. For a coordinate indicator $`X_1`$, $`V=d\,\mathrm{Cov}(S,X_1)=d p(1-p)(\mathbb E[S\mid X_1=1]-\mathbb E[S\mid X_1=0])`$, where $`p=\mu/d`$ and the difference is at most one by the nested truncations.
3. When describing the consequence, retain “existential” next to the cutoff. The manuscript establishes a uniform asymptotic restriction on possible counterexamples, so the open all-parameter conjecture should remain open.

## Editorial follow-up

The final text names the complex cubic coefficient and compares the principal clusters with fixed centered cubes inside the zero-arc argument. It fixes the near-saturation threshold before choosing the absolute cutoff and gives the variance comparison directly in the main proof. The landing page and manuscript state that the cutoff constant is existential and that the original conjecture remains open.
