# Blind review of the balanced-array theorem

Date 2026-11-26. Independent, agent-assisted review of the anonymized submission and its mathematical dependencies.

## Verdict and scope

The proof establishes that for every real $\epsilon>0$ there is an integer $N(\epsilon)$ such that for every integer $n\ge N(\epsilon)$, every integer $d\ge\epsilon n$, and every integer $1\le r\le d$, the balanced-array polynomial has exactly $\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$ distinct negative zeros. The cutoff is existential and is uniform in $d$ and $r$ for fixed $\epsilon$. I found no substantive gap in this theorem.

I checked the polynomial against the original “Balanced arrays of uniform matroids” statement. Both use labeled binary matrices with all column sums equal to $k$, each row sum at most $r$, and monomial $q^k$. The conjecture asks for all $n\ge0$ and $1\le r\le d$. The dense theorem leaves the universal claim open, including the fixed pair $(r,d)=(2,4)$ as $n$ grows and the general range $d/n\to0$. The companion orientation argument also proves the stated explicit sufficient conditions whenever their inequalities hold and gives real-rootedness directly for degrees zero and one. The source asks for real-rootedness alone. [Original conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

## Checks on the combinatorial and enumerative inputs

- The support is exactly $0,\ldots,\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$. Total capacity gives necessity. Assigning successive groups of $k$ rows in a cyclic list makes rows distinct within a column and uses each row at most $\lceil dk/n\rceil$ times, proving sufficiency.
- Kahn and Neiman's Lemma 8 applies to a multigraph and compares componentwise outdegree and indegree lower bounds. I checked the conversion from two capped arrays to paired exclusive cells and the orientation count. In each column, the same occupied sets and paired exclusive cells give the same augmented quotas on each side of the comparison. Each pair of arrays has the same $\prod_j m_j!$ pairing multiplicity for its given common and exclusive data, so the weighted sum of fixed-data comparisons preserves the sign. The own-coordinate cap change and cross-coordinate dummy-cell flip both satisfy the lower-bound inequalities at every vertex. The adjacent comparisons multiply along feasible paths and yield the required diagonal normalized log-concavity. [Kahn and Neiman, Lemma 8, §4, preprint p. 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20395)
- A failed sign node has $\Gamma_k\le4$ by the basic separation argument. The normalized bound gives an upper bound for $d/n$ along any sequence of failures, and the assumed lower bound is $\epsilon$. With these bounds, $\Gamma_k\le4$ rules out both $k/n\to0$ and $k/n\to1$ by the lower bounds $d/(k+1)$ and $d/(n-k+1)$ on $\log\Gamma_k$. Hence every such sequence has a subsequence with a positive finite limiting aspect ratio and a limiting density in $(0,1)$. The cap ratios also have a convergent subsequence.
- Canfield, Greenhill and McKay's Theorem 1 uses positive integer margins with equal totals. With its dimensions $(m,n_{\mathrm{CGM}})=(n,d)$ and density $\lambda=k/n$, the regular rows have margins $dk/n+O(d^{1/2+\eta})$ and all columns have margin $k$. All margins are positive for large $n$ under the resulting compact density bound. With $A=\lambda(1-\lambda)/2$ bounded below and the dimensions comparable, both asymptotic growth hypotheses hold for each fixed permitted $\eta>0$. The left side of the logarithmic density condition is bounded, so one can fix the two positive constants in that condition and the error with sum below $1/2$, then choose the deviation exponent sufficiently small for the theorem. Its $C$ is zero and its $R$ is $\sum_i(s_i-dk/n)^2$. Substitution gives the factor $\binom nk^d/\binom{nd}{dk}$, the row factor $\prod_i\binom d{s_i}$, and exponent $-1/2+R/[2nd(k/n)(1-k/n)]+o(1)$. The error can be used uniformly on the regular profiles, since a violating selection would be a sequence satisfying the same margin-deviation and compact dimension and density hypotheses. The fixed-margin theorem does not impose a separate slack hypothesis. [Canfield, Greenhill and McKay, Theorem 1, §1, author manuscript pp. 2–3](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/CGM.pdf#page=2) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2007.03.009)

## Summing over capped margins

The actual-array tail estimate is a relative error in the enumeration of $a_k$. Fixing all but two rows leaves independent assignments in the singly occupied columns before imposing the two caps. Both caps restrict the absolute row gap to a centered interval. Consequently its conditional upper tail is at most the untruncated binomial tail, uniformly in the rows that were fixed. The row mean is $dk/n$, so one large row deviation forces a large gap from another row. The union bound gives relative error $O(n^2e^{-d^{2\eta}/2})=o(1)$.

The comparison after inserting the fixed-margin theorem is a different distribution. Independent tilted rows, conditional on the exact total $dk$, give each labeled row profile mass proportional to $\prod_i\binom d{s_i}$. The conditioning probability is at least $1/(nd+1)$ by convolution log-concavity, linear interpolation of the log mass, and Jensen at the integer mean. The binomial comparison at the same mean is valid in convex order. The two mass functions differ in sign at most twice with the tilted law carrying more mass on the intervening interval. Subtracting a secant of a test convex function proves the comparison using equality of total mass and mean. Hence the centered tilted variable has the stated uniform Hoeffding tail, including the deterministic case as a separate endpoint.

I checked that this bound gives a uniform positive exponential moment for $(S-\mu)^2/d$. The exponential bound for deviations of its empirical mean $W$ from $V/d$, and the stretched-exponential row-maximum bound, survive division by the polynomial lower bound on the conditioning probability. Cauchy–Schwarz is applicable even to the weighted tails because $\mathbb E e^{cW}$ is uniformly bounded for each fixed $c>0$. The resulting conditional, exponentially weighted sum proves the claimed formula (1) with an $o(1)$ in the exponent. At an exactly saturated quota the tilt, row profile and exact-total event are deterministic, so this step remains valid with $V=0$. The two binomial log-curvatures in $\log K_k$ both tend to $\delta/[x_0(1-x_0)]$ and cancel. These checks apply at each feasible fixed quota shift with the same compact limiting density.

## Slack limits and root count

- With $(r-dk/n)/\sqrt d\to0$, the deficit law is a tilted truncated geometric law with a multiplicative coefficient adjustment. Expansion against its geometric moments gives $V=h(1+h)(1+O((1+h)^2/d))$ for positive deficit mean $h=o(\sqrt d)$. Its variance is zero at $h=0$. The entropy representation and log-concave central-mass bounds give only bounded error in the logarithm after subtracting the displayed variance prefactor. For unbounded $h=o(\sqrt d)$, the log-prefactor second difference is bounded and the entropy curvature has size $n(d/n)^2/[h(1+h)]\to\infty$. For bounded $h$, the central part of the triangular integral supplies order $n$, against an $O(\log n)$ log-prefactor contribution. Continuity of the entropy gives the same lower bound if the upper adjacent quota saturates exactly. The bound $h\ge d/n$ at an internal quota ensures that no infeasible adjacent quota enters any of these formulas. Thus the log-curvature of the true counts diverges after using (1).
- With positive finite limiting scaled slack, the tilt is bracketed by fixed trial tilts on the central-limit scale. Their conditional Gaussian means vary strictly through zero and their variances converge. Infinite scaled slack gives the untruncated Gaussian. The variance limit $v>0$ holds on any bounded interval of real quota shifts by the sequential argument, and both support ends are arbitrarily many integer steps away. The local limit calculation has effective control on all of its frequency ranges. The lower variance and upper fourth moment give the small-frequency Gaussian bound, the compact range away from zero follows from convergence to a continuous limiting distribution, and unimodality plus the maximum atom bound controls all remaining lattice frequencies. Fourier inversion applies because the sum is centered at an integer at each integer quota. Thus all required local probability factors have the common asymptotic equivalent. The entropy second derivative, the vanishing curvature of $\log K_k$, and the common limit of the variance corrections give $\log\Gamma_{k+j}\to\delta/v$ at every fixed integer offset $j$.
- At the ordered node $-t_k$, concavity bounds each relative magnitude at offset $j$ by $e^{-c_0j^2/2}$. The exact product in the argument gives the limit $e^{-(\delta/v)j^2/2}$. Dominated convergence followed by Gaussian Poisson summation at frequency $1/2$ gives a strictly positive limiting signed value. This is a contradiction for the chosen sequence of failures. The zero-scaled-slack case also contradicts failure, by $\Gamma_k\to\infty$. The argument by subsequences suffices for a single threshold $N(\epsilon)$, since any failure of the quantified assertion would supply failures with $n$ arbitrarily large. The ordered successful nodes, the positive constant term and the leading sign give one root per successive interval. Degree then gives simplicity, and constant and linear cases follow directly.
