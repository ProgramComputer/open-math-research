## Centered recursion

The reference model is the cap-truncated binomial at parameter $z(p)$ with carried mean $dp$. Its saturation probability conditional on a missing bit is the top mass of the $(d-1)$-trial truncated binomial at the same tilt. Multiplying the $d$-trial top mass by $(1-r/d)/(1-p)$ and using the mean identity gives $1-e^{-L^*(p)}$. Differentiation yields $V/d=p(1-z)-(z-p)(r-dp)$ and hence $(L^*)'(p)=B/[v(v-B)]$ with the stated $B,v$. The variance upper bound $V\le dz(1-z)$ follows because the subtracted term of the mean identity is increasing in the log odds. It makes $z-p$ nondecreasing, so the rectangular bounds from the two endpoint tilts are valid.

Each descent removes one row and lowers individual quotas by zero or one. The phase rectangle in the checker contains every resulting quota vector and mean through the terminal depth. The stronger capacity check at the lowest height also covers the additional eight scalar steps used in the terminal lower bound. At terminal depth the actual insertion loss at any residual vector lies between the two balanced endpoint losses by coordinatewise orientation monotonicity. The same interval contains every relevant reference loss. Its bounded-away-from-zero survival gives the stated initial log-error estimate.

For a layer of marked supports excluding column $i$, swapping a marked and unmarked column compares two one-unit insertions into the same residual margin vector. The two reference terms therefore cancel exactly. Induction and the binomial normalization give the stated two-sided ratio bound on the remaining factors. Under the matching swap of subsets, the events in which exactly one of two specified positions is vacant compare with the same ratio. The common event in which both are vacant preserves both directions. Thus every vacancy weight normalized by its layer mean lies between $1/K$ and $K$. The average of the odds in these additions therefore gives the exact consecutive-size ratio after dividing by $(d-1-j_0)/(j_0+1)$. At a fixed third column, choosing a $00$ row with a one leaves at least $b_j-y-1$ partners of type $11$ missing that column. The inverse choice has at most $(y+1)^2$ pairs.
