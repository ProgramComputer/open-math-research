# Prior-work check for uniform comparison below the row cap

Checked 26 November 2026. The proposed theorem concerns the original labeled $n\times d$ binary arrays with common column sum $k$ and row sums at most $r$. For $2\le d\le n$ and every cap $1\le r\le d$, it bounds the number of zeros left uncertified by $CD\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}/d$, with $D=\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$ and an absolute constant $C$. For $D>0$, the certified fraction of simple negative zeros tends to one whenever $d/\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}\to\infty$, uniformly over the caps. This strengthens the preliminary absolute loss bound $O(n^{3/2}\sqrt{\log(n+1)}/d)$, which could be vacuous when $D$ was small. The proposed proof individually compares the roots below its cutoff with those of $F_{n,d}(q)=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk^d q^k$ and gives all $D$ roots when $D\le d/2$ by coefficient separation. It leaves the all-parameters conjecture open. [Per Alexandersson's original conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Author's issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005) · [Project question #2](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2)

The prior public fixed-parameter claim at $(r,d)=(2,4)$ and the finite checks in solved-conjectures #165 concern other scopes. The current project theorem verifies all roots for $d\ge Cn/\log n$, with an existential absolute $C$ and row cutoff. Its implication in that range does not identify roots individually against $F_{n,d}$. The fixed-dimension eventual results and the geometric-center obstruction likewise do not supply this uniform comparison in the intermediate growing-dimension range. The unrestricted row case $r=d$ is classical from finite composition. ## Classical inputs

Katkova, Shapiro, and Vishnyakova, *C. R. Math.* 349 (2011), 35–38, **Theorem 6**, §1, printed p. 37, prove that the Schur–Szegő product of same-degree polynomials with real roots all of one sign has logarithmic mesh at least the maximum of their two meshes. The mesh is the minimum ratio of successive absolute root values, rather than the difference of their logarithms. The authors explicitly remark that the stronger product inequality is false already for quadratics. Finite composition multiplies the coefficients after each is divided by its same-degree binomial coefficient, then restores one copy of that binomial coefficient. Thus $F_{n,d}=F_{n,d-1}*F_{n,2}$ for $d\ge3$. The negative zeros and the ratio mesh of $F_{n,2}$ give negative simple zeros for every $F_{n,d}$ with $d\ge2$ and the same mesh lower bound. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2010.11.031) · [Version of record, p. 37](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mathematique/item/10.1016/j.crma.2010.11.031.pdf#page=3)

For the Legendre polynomial $L_n$, the Jacobi binomial expansion at $\alpha=\beta=0$ gives

$$
F_{n,2}(q)=(1-q)^nL_n\left(\frac{1+q}{1-q}\right).
$$

Equivalently $F_{n,2}(q)$ is the terminating hypergeometric series with parameters $(-n,-n;1;q)$, and Pfaff's transform sends it to the Legendre series. The $n$ zeros of $L_n$ lie in $(-1,1)$ and are simple. In log-radius coordinates $q=-e^u$, the Legendre argument is $-\tanh(u/2)$. The Legendre equation gives $g''+n(n+1)\mathrm{sech}^2(u/2)g/4=0$ for $g(u)=L_n(-\tanh(u/2))$. Between consecutive zeros, integration by parts and Dirichlet Wirtinger give $\Delta u\ge2\pi/\sqrt{n(n+1)}$. This lower-bounds the log of the ratio mesh of $F_{n,d}$ for all $d\ge2$. [DLMF 18.5.8, binomial identity](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E8) · [DLMF 18.5.7, Legendre series](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E7) · [DLMF 15.8.1, Pfaff transform](https://dlmf.nist.gov/15.8.E1) · [DLMF 18.16(ii), zeros](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.16.ii) · [DLMF Table 18.8.1, row 7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.8.T1)

The plausible attack is to compare on disjoint disks around the unrestricted roots. Independent uniform $k$-subsets in each column give a binomial row marginal and a cap-excess bound for the coefficient perturbation. The quantitative question is whether that bound, together with the tilted mass of nearby degrees and the logarithmic separation, beats cancellation of $F_{n,d}$ on each disk boundary. A uniform count with $o(D)$ unlocated roots in the stated regime would pass the current review point. Mesh preservation and coefficient concentration separately give no root count for the capped polynomial.
