# Independent blind review of the subcap theorem

**Verdict.** The three supplied documents establish the stated uniform estimate and the stated localization of simple negative zeros. I found no mathematical gap in either claim. This is a partial result on the original conjecture. The review was agent-assisted and used the anonymous proof, its two anonymous supporting documents, and the cited primary sources.

## Claim and scope

The primary source asks for real-rootedness of the polynomial that counts labeled $n\times d$ binary matrices with a common column sum $k$, weighted by $q^k$, and with row sums at most $r$. Its quantifiers are $n\ge0$ and $1\le r\le d$. The proposed proof uses exactly those coefficients. The cyclic construction in the orientation proof realizes each $k$ from zero through $D=\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$ because a block of at most $n$ consecutive row labels has no repetitions and each row is used at most $\lceil dk/n\rceil$ times. For $n=0$ the empty array gives the constant polynomial. [SymCat conjecture statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

For $2\le d\le n$ the manuscript proves that at most $CD\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}/d$ zeros, counted with multiplicity, lie outside the set of simple negative zeros, with one absolute $C$ for every cap. It allows a positive deficit and gives no real-rootedness conclusion for the remaining zeros. The full-root cases $D\le d/2$ and $d>n$ follow from the coefficient result already used as an input. The elementary case $d=1$, which is outside the theorem's quantified range, has $P(q)=(1+q)^n$. The universal conjecture remains open under the submitted argument.

## Checks of the proof

- For the unrestricted polynomial, the Legendre expansion gives the stated Möbius transformation. Under $q=-e^y$, it yields the displayed second-order equation with potential bounded above by $n(n+1)/4$. The Dirichlet inequality between two consecutive zeros gives the claimed logarithmic separation. The Schur–Szegő identity uses binomial-normalized coefficients, and the cited mesh theorem preserves the lower bound under this operation. All the polynomials have degree $n$, positive coefficients and constant term one, so the real zeros are strictly negative and the mesh bound rules out multiplicity. [Katkova, Shapiro and Vishnyakova, Theorems 4 and 6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2010.11.031)
- The circle product estimate has a single centered factor of magnitude at least $\epsilon/3$ after normalization by $F(t)$, where $\epsilon=1/(4n^2)$. Each other factor is bounded below by $\tanh(|\log(\rho_j/t)|/2)$, and the spacing gives $|\log(\rho_j/t)|\ge1/n$. Within log-distance one, each index contributes a uniformly positive summand to the Bernoulli variance, so the number of such indices is $O(\mathrm{Var}(K))$. Farther out, each logarithmic loss is bounded by an absolute multiple of its variance summand. The resulting exponent is $O((1+\mathrm{Var}(K))\log(n+1))$. The same spacing gives disjoint disks and ensures that exactly $i-1$ or $i$ radii lie below the tilted radius $t$. The Bernoulli step-function comparison gives the claimed expectation bound, since $\min(p,1-p)\le2p(1-p)$.
- The cap-sensitive variance estimate holds uniformly over $t$ and mode $m$, including a mode at an endpoint. On available sides, division by the first outward ratio gives the two displayed ratio bounds. For $L=1+\lceil\sqrt{(m+1)/d}\rceil$ the right-hand exponent is at least $dL/(m+1+L)\ge1/(2L)$ beyond distance $L$. On the available left side it is at least $dL/m\ge1/L$. To bound a one-sided tail at distance $(h+1)L$, shift every term back by $hL$ and compare it to the corresponding term starting at distance at least $L$. Summing the resulting geometric bound in blocks of length $L$ gives second moment $O(L^2)=O(1+m/d)$. This also covers the endpoint cases by summing the available side. The global variance estimate in the support file follows separately by monotone likelihood comparison against the indicated one-sided Gaussian weights.
- The variance and expectation bounds imply $|m-i|=O(1+m/d)$. In the range $\alpha\le1/2$ with $A$ chosen sufficiently large, $d\ge2A\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}$ lets one absorb the $m/d$ term. The bound $m=O(i+1)$ then gives $\mathrm{Var}(K)=O(D/d)$ and $|\mathbb E K-i|=O(D/d)$ when $D>d/2$. For $s=\alpha D$, the deviation of $K$ can be tested at $s/4$ above its mean. Bernstein gives an upper bound $\exp(-cs^2/D)$ using variance $O(D)$ and $s\le D$. For every $k\le D-s/2$, the row law is binomial and its upper tail can be tested at the fixed gap $ds/(2n)$. Its variance and this gap are both at most $dD/n$, giving $\exp(-c'ds^2/(nD))$. The upper bound on the union over all $n$ rows requires only the row marginals. As $d\le n$, the first exponent is at least as strong up to constants, and $ds^2/(nD)=A^2(D/d)\log(n+1)$. Since $D/d>1/2$, one absolute choice of $A$ handles the polynomial prefactor and yields strict Rouché dominance on every indicated circle. Conjugation and the multiplicity count ensure a simple negative zero in each disk.
- I checked the normalized coefficient inequality used when $D\le d/2$ and $d>n$. The multigraph lemma applies to the stipulated integer lower bounds. Fixing the common and exclusive entries and pairing the exclusive red and blue cells assigns the same multiplicity to every array pair with those common and exclusive entries. The dummy vertices in the mixed comparison preserve the fixed augmented quotas on both sides and have the required forced orientations. The own-coordinate and mixed ratios then multiply along the two comparable diagonal paths, giving the normalized inequality. For $D\le d/2$, each internal $k<D$ gives a strict ratio greater than $(1+2/d)^d\ge4$. For $d>n\ge1$, the global lower bound $(1+2/n)^{2d}$ exceeds four. The geometric-center sign argument consequently counts the full degree in these cases. [Kahn and Neiman, Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9)
- For $D>d/2$ and $\alpha>1/2$, the trivial bound is absorbed by increasing $C$. Otherwise the disk count leaves a loss bounded by $\alpha D+1$. Since $\alpha D>A\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}/2$, the rounding term is absorbed into the same uniform bound. This verifies the stated asymptotic uniformly in $r$.

## Follow-up review of the separating-circle corollary

**Verdict.** The proposed corollary holds for each integer $1\le i\le(1-\alpha)D$ in the disk-localization regime. Since $\alpha>0$ and $D\le n$, one has $i<n$. At $T_i=\sqrt{\rho_i\rho_{i+1}}$ the minimum absolute log-distance to an unrestricted root radius is half the log-gap, at least $\pi/\sqrt{n(n+1)}>1/n$. The reverse triangle inequality and the same close- and far-factor estimates therefore give $|F(z)|/F(T_i)\ge\exp[-C \mathrm{Var}(K)\log(n+1)]$ on $|z|=T_i$, without a centered factor. The Bernoulli step-function estimate now gives $|\mathbb E K-i|\le2\mathrm{Var}(K)$ because exactly $i$ root radii lie below $T_i$. All cap-error bounds then apply at $t=T_i$ with the same absolute constants, taking $A$ sufficiently large for both circle estimates. Rouché gives exactly $i$ zeros of $P$ inside, with multiplicity, and the strict inequality excludes boundary zeros.

For $1\le j\le i$ the outer radius of localization disk $j$ is at most $\rho_i(1+1/(4n^2))$, which is strictly less than $T_i$ by $\log(T_i/\rho_i)\ge\pi/\sqrt{n(n+1)}>\log(1+1/(4n^2))$. Those $i$ disks account for the entire count inside $T_i$. Every nonreal or multiple zero thus has modulus strictly greater than $T_i$. This is an additional conclusion about the location of the unresolved zeros.
