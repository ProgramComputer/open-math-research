# Independent referee report on the rank-dependent dimension theorem

**Review date** 2026-11-26

**Review type** Independent, agent-assisted mathematical review of the anonymized packet.

**Verdict** The argument proves the stated partial theorem for the original labeled balanced-array polynomial. I found no substantive gap. The theorem gives exactly $\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$ simple negative zeros for all sufficiently large $n$ in its two quantified ranges, with constants depending only on $\varepsilon$. It leaves the general real-rootedness conjecture open. In particular it gives no conclusion for the original fixed pair $(r,d)=(2,4)$, and there is a gap in the growing regimes, for example $r=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor$ and $d=\lceil n^{2/3}\rceil$. I found no Lean or Rocq statement, proof, or build instructions for this theorem in the packet. The checked coverage here is a written mathematical argument. [Original conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

## Statement and quantifiers

The primary statement is real-rootedness for all $n\ge 0$ and $1\le r\le d$, with one term $q^k$ per labeled zero-one matrix of common column sum $k$ and row sums at most $r$. The proof uses those counts throughout. The cyclic construction gives positive coefficients at every index from zero to the exact degree, and the support is bounded by the sum of the row caps. The stronger simple-negative conclusion is valid in the claimed eventual range. For each fixed $r$ one may apply the first alternative with any fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/5$ and absorb $\sqrt r$ into the threshold. The two displayed sample families meet their respective alternatives and have $d/\sqrt{nr/\log n}$ tending to infinity.

The negation of the stated existence of $C_\varepsilon,N_\varepsilon$ supplies exceptions with $n\ge j$ and $d/\sqrt{nr/\log n}\ge j$. One alternative holds on an infinite subsequence. The first conclusion of the logarithmic dimension theorem then gives bounded $d\log n/n$. I checked the coefficient and sign proof of that conclusion in the accompanying notes. This use has no dependency on that note's final discussion of fixed dimension. Thus $r/d\to0$. On a failed node the orientation bound gives $k+1\ge d/\log4$, hence $b=d^2/n=O(r)$. The window width is negligible compared with $d$, so the whole feasible part of the window has density $p\le r/d=o(1)$ and mean row size $\mu\ge c b$. These are the bounds used below.

## Orientation and fixed margins

I checked the two orientation comparisons used in the proof. Pairing the exclusive cells of each column gives the same multiplicity for the two compared counts at fixed common and exclusive data. In the own-coordinate comparison the red constraints dominate those of blue before the swaps in the sense needed by Lemma 8. In the mixed comparison the equal real-row caps give equal red and blue lower bounds, and one forced dummy changes color while the other remains red. The augmented quotas and pairing multiplicities agree on the two sides. Dividing by the binomial normalizations gives the diagonal log-concavity bound. With $s$ reduced quotas left after fixing one row, the same mixed inequality compares two distinct unreduced columns at the lower base vector and gives $B_sB_{s+2}\le B_{s+1}^2$. Deleting a 1 in a residual column shows the positive support of $B_s$ extends to the right once nonempty, so the likelihood-ratio argument has an interval of positivity. [Kahn and Neiman, Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9)

For small rank, with $S=dk$, Greenhill–McKay–Wang Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 5.2 apply uniformly to capped profiles. The two maxima have product at most $rk$ and their bound becomes
$n^{-1/6}r^{5/6}(r/b)^{1/2}=O(n^{-5\varepsilon/6}\sqrt{\log n})$ after division by $S^{2/3}$. The normalized row second central moment equals $\sum_j(s_j-\mu)^2/[S(1-p)]$ and is at most $2r$, while the normalized column moment is zero. In particular $r^3/S=O(r^2\log n/n)=o(1)$ verifies the extra moment condition. The exponent consequently has the claimed constant $-1/2$ and positive row-variance correction. Hoeffding's bound with $M=O(r\log n)$ takes a polynomial-size deviation cutoff to an error smaller than every inverse power. The conditional mean event costs at most $nr+1$. The bounded exponential moment used with Cauchy–Schwarz controls the weighted exceptional contribution. [Greenhill, McKay and Wang, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 5.2](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/irregular-final.pdf#page=33)

For large dimension, both actual and scalar row laws have binomial mean $dp$. The actual row mass has a log-concave likelihood ratio proportional to $B_s(p/(1-p))^{-s}$ through its support, and the scalar ratio is geometric through the cap. The likelihood-ratio crossing argument subtracts an affine secant and proves domination for every convex function. Applying it to the positive and negative exponential tests yields the Bernstein bound in both laws. Therefore the regularity width $\mu^{1/2+\eta}$ gives a superpolynomially small exceptional mass even at small density, since $\mu\ge c n^{1/2+2\varepsilon}$. The width is smaller than the required $\mu^{1/2+\kappa}$ for fixed $0<\eta<\kappa$. With the smaller part of size $d$, the first aspect term dominates. Substituting $p\ge cd/n$ into the normalized aspect bound gives $O(n^{3/2-5\kappa}/d^{2-10\kappa})$. Its largest admissible power of $n$ is $-2\varepsilon+(5/2+10\varepsilon)\kappa<0$ for sufficiently small $\kappa$. The logarithmic growth condition follows from $d^2/n\gg\log^K n$ for each fixed $K$. The normalization follows by multiplying the joint fixed-total binomial probability by $\binom{nd}{di}$, leaving one inverse binomial factor. The two part variances in the correction are $n^{-1}\sum_j(s_j-\mu)^2$ and zero. I used the available publisher-indexed theorem text and error expression and cross-checked the conditions and normalization with the authors' preprint, whose aspect condition is equivalent at fixed $\kappa$. The large-dimension average uses exponential arguments of order $n^{-1/3}$ or smaller for $Y=(S-\mu)^2/\mu$. They are inside the permitted constant times $p$ range. On the bounded support, half of the Bernstein exponent absorbs $tY$ uniformly for those arguments. The remaining exponent controls the second moment with an integrable envelope and justifies the quadratic bound on the log moment generating function. Conditional Cauchy–Schwarz and the actual relative tail bound then justify summing the margin estimate. At saturation there is only the constant row profile. ## Variance, endpoints and signs

For $V\le 1$, the mode carries an absolute positive mass by Chebyshev. Log-concavity bounds the second and third absolute moments about the mode by a constant times the combined neighboring mass. The independent-copy identity gives $V\ge b_m(1-b_m)$, where $b_m$ is the modal mass, and $V$ is at most the second moment about the mode. These imply the third centered moment bound $O(V)$ and the neighboring mass lower bound of order $V$. The modal pairs with distance one give a Fourier envelope $e^{-cVt^2}$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$. The telescoped powers have integrated error $O(1/(nV))$, giving the claimed relative local limit bound, and the high-variance lattice argument covers $V\ge1$. These estimates and the cumulant identities give the Lipschitz bound on $\sqrt{1+V}$ used up to either deterministic endpoint.

In the low-variance case $V_k<b n^{-1/16}$, the Lipschitz variation over unit quota distance is $O(bn^{-1/16})$ because $b\ge 1/log n$. The curvature on the middle half of the triangular difference is therefore at least a constant times $n^{1/16}$. The entropy term extends continuously and is concave on the closed feasible interval, so the rest of the triangle can only increase this lower bound even when its outer point is exactly saturated. The lattice probability cost is $O(\log n)$ by Jensen and the entropy bound. In the complementary case relative variance variation over the whole window is polynomially small even after multiplication by $n^{1/16}$. By continuity it excludes either deterministic endpoint from that window. Here the local limit estimate varies polynomially little on the log scale, since $n\min(1,V)$ is polynomially large. The derivative bound on the correction and the elementary cancellation in the adjacent binomial ratios then give the uniform additive error on $\log\Gamma_{k+j}$.

Finally, $\log\Gamma_i>0$ makes the nodes strictly ordered. If $\Gamma_i>4$ the two neighbors use less than the central magnitude, and every subsequent alternating tail starts with the central sign and has decreasing magnitudes. At a hypothetical failed node the exact offset products yield the Gaussian comparison in the entire logarithmic window and give the required outer Gaussian bound from the orientation inequality. In the retained window the additive error summed over all offsets is $O(J^3 n^{-\kappa})$. Outside it, $bJ^2/r$ grows faster than $(\log n)^3$, so the tails also admit a fixed inverse-power bound. The positive Poisson sum is at least $\sqrt\pi\exp(-\pi^2r/(2b))=n^{-o(1)}$ since $b\log n/r\to\infty$. It dominates the absolute error. This excludes every failed node on the assumed sequence of exceptions and proves the existential uniform bound.

One optional clarity edit is to state explicitly that the high-variance window excludes the continuous real endpoint $nr/d$ even if it lies strictly beyond the last integer coefficient. All coefficient estimates themselves apply only at integer quotas. The existing argument handles this case.
