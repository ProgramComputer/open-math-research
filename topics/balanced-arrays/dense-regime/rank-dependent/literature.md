# Source and literature record for the rank-dependent dimension bound

Per Alexandersson’s balanced-array conjecture asks whether the polynomial counting labeled binary $n\times d$ arrays with common column sum $k$ and row cap $r$, by $q^k$, is real-rooted for every $n\ge0$ and $1\le r\le d$. The rank-dependent theorem settles all sufficiently large row counts when $d\ge C_\varepsilon\sqrt{nr/\log n}$ and one of the two stated hypotheses on $r,d$ holds. It gives a new growing-dimension range for fixed caps down to order $\sqrt{n/\log n}$. The general claim remains open. [Primary statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Proof](proof.md)

The fixed-margin inputs are Greenhill, McKay and Wang, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 5.2 in §5 for the small-rank case, and Liebenau and Wormald, Theorem 1.1, equations (3)–(5), and Remark 1.2 in §1.1 of the published paper for the large-dimension case. Both inputs count labeled matrices with prescribed margins. They do not establish root locations. The argument also uses the repository’s normalized log-concavity reduction to Kahn and Neiman’s graph orientation inequality, Lemma 8 of their preprint. [Greenhill–McKay–Wang, version of record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2005.03.005) · [Liebenau–Wormald, version of record](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.21105) · [Kahn–Neiman, Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9) · [Repository reduction](../../orientation-normalization.md)


The earlier logarithmic-dimension theorem gives all roots for $d\ge Cn/\log n$ at large $n$ across all caps. The below-cap comparison gives a uniform fraction of simple negative roots once $d$ grows faster than $\sqrt{n\log(n+1)}$. The new claim uses the rank bound to reach lower dimensions while certifying the entire degree in its stated ranges. [Logarithmic theorem](../logarithmic-window.md) · [Below-cap comparison](../below-cap/README.md)

## Other enumeration results and priority check

- Canfield–McKay, Theorem 1(c), treats both sides exactly regular. With short side $m$, long side $N$, and degree $t$ on the long side, it requires $2\le m=O((t(m-t)N)^{1/4-\eta})$ for fixed sufficiently small $\eta>0$ as $N\to\infty$. Transposition gives $(m,N,t)=(d,n,r)$ at exact saturation when $d\mid nr$. At fixed rank even saturation in the present range falls outside that aspect bound. [Published paper, Theorem 1](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/EJC/Volume_12/PDF/v12i1r29.pdf#page=3).
- Barvinok–Hartigan's binary bipartite formula, §2.5, Eq. (2.5.4), requires fixed $\delta>0$, both aspect ratios at least $\delta$, and all maximum-entropy entries in $[\delta,1-\delta]$. Symmetry forces the maximizing entries in row $i$ to equal $s_i/d\le r/d=o(1)$. Both conditions fail here. [Primary text, §2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.0356#page=9).
- Liebenau–Wormald's published Theorem 1.1 Eq. (3) requires $n/d^{3/2-5\kappa}+d/n^{3/2-5\kappa}=o(p^{1/2-5\kappa})$ for fixed $0<\kappa<1/10$, failing already at $d\asymp\sqrt n$. [Published theorem](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rsa.21105).

Per Alexandersson asks for real zeros of the polynomial for every $n\ge0$ and $1\le r\le d$, with exactly the equal-column and capped-row counts above. [SymCat](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays), [author's issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005).
