# Priority check for balanced arrays with sublinear column counts

Search date 26 November 2026. The strengthened target under review asks whether the original labeled balanced-array polynomial has only simple negative zeros eventually for every row cap $1\le r\le d$ along every sequence with $d\log(n)/n\to\infty$. Its new regime satisfies $d/n\to0$. The coefficients count binary matrices with every column sum equal to the exponent and row sums bounded by $r$. The current reviewed result requires a fixed positive lower bound on $d/n$. [Original conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Author’s issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005) · [Published linearly-many-columns theorem](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/23d24d4a3f0ff6009ca4d1c197aa3b0f3235083d/topics/balanced-arrays/dense-regime/README.md)

The author’s eventual claim is for the fixed pair $(r,d)=(2,4)$. The exact finite evidence in solved-conjectures #165 includes a corank-one screen with Newton-inequality checks and bounded experiments for other caps. Neither contains the strengthened growing-parameter result. The fixed-margin theorems of Canfield–Greenhill–McKay and Liebenau–Wormald estimate the number of matrices with prescribed margin vectors. They supply the enumeration used before summing over capped row profiles. Their conclusions concern counts and do not address zeros of the balanced-array polynomial. [Prior investigation #165](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/165) · [Canfield–Greenhill–McKay, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2007.03.009) · [Liebenau–Wormald, published Theorem 1.1, equations (3)–(5) and Remark 1.2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rsa.21105#rsa21105-mthst-0001)
