# Independent review of the shifted-ray package

**Verdict.** The shift and root orientation are correct, and both synthetic obstructions check out. The abstract one-step sign argument is valid along nonzero paths. The statement needs an explicit qualification for rays that vanish identically.

For a column polynomial $P$, the ray at offset $u$ is $\sum_k[x^{u+k\mathbf1}]P\,q^k$. Reindexing gives $F_{u+\mathbf1}=q^{-1}F_u$. If an increment followed by coordinate sorting gives $v+s\mathbf1$ with $\min v=0$, the new ray is $q^{-s}F_v$. Multiplying the old and new ray by $q^s$ gives the ordered pair $q^sF_u,F_v$. The archive accordingly passes the new ray first and the shifted old ray second to its interlacing checker. With positive leading coefficients, the orientation for the ordered old-new pair $f,g$ is $f'g-fg'\ge0$ on the real axis.

For the nonvanishing abstract family in the note, the degree-one cases have the old root strictly to the right of the new root. In the wraparound case the cleared pair is $f=q(1+q)$ and $g=1+3q$, with the new root $-1/3$ between the two old roots and Wronskian $1+2q+3q^2>0$. I reconstructed the update sums $G_{(2,0,0)}=16+25q$ and $qG_{(3,0,0)}=1+1017q+2016q^2$. At $-16/25$ the latter is $109921/625$ and its derivative is $-39087/25$. Since the upward quadratic has two negative roots, this point is strictly left of both. Weak interlacing fails with either common clearing or separate monomial stripping.

For the one-step result, interlacing leaves simple poles after canceling common factors. The Wronskian condition makes the residues of $g/f$ nonnegative and any linear coefficient in its polynomial part nonpositive. Thus each adjacent ratio lies in the closed lower half-plane for $q$ in the upper half-plane. Taking the argument continuously from the positive axis, its contribution is in $[-\pi,0]$. On a nonzero path from $u-\mathbf1$ to $u$, the sum of arguments equals $-\arg q$ by shift equivariance and the positive-axis limit. The suffix from $u-\mathbf1_S$ to $u$ gives $\arg(F_{u-\mathbf1_S}/F_u)\in[0,\arg q]$. This sector has width less than $\pi$. A sum with nonnegative weights and at least one nonzero summand is therefore nonvanishing in the upper half-plane, and the cleared polynomial is real-rooted.

A sufficient local hypothesis is that $F_u$ is nonzero and each positively weighted, nonzero predecessor $u-\mathbf1_S$ lies on a positive-coordinate path from $u-\mathbf1$ to $u$ on which every ray is nonzero. Weak orientation checked only on nonzero edges does not imply this. For example, in dimension two and cap one take $F_{(a,b)}=q^{-b}f_{a-b}$, with $f_{-1}=1$, $f_1=q$, and all other $f_j=0$. The family is shift-equivariant, each nonzero ray is a monomial, and there are no adjacent nonzero rays. The update at $(0,0)$ is $1+q^2$. A zero updated ray should also be identified explicitly if all weighted terms vanish.

For the finite symmetric example, $A=1+e_1+e_2+47\sum_i x_i^2$ has all and only the nonnegative exponent vectors of total degree at most two. Every nonzero ray is a monomial whose exponent is $-\lfloor\sum_i u_i/3\rfloor$. Thus every nonzero adjacent ratio is a positive constant or a positive constant times $q^{-1}$, giving the claimed weak orientation and phase changes. Some predecessors can vanish in this finite family. Its first update is nevertheless individually real-rooted on each nonzero ray by total degree at most four in three variables, which allows at most two adjacent powers. Independent coefficient convolution gave the second equal diagonal $1+24q+147q^2$, with discriminant $-12$. This establishes failure after two updates for the auxiliary input $A$.

I independently computed the directed archive entries for every $d=2,3$, $1\le r\le d$, and $0\le n\le8$ by dynamic programming on labeled column-margin vectors without permutation orbits. The replay matched 515 coefficient lists and Sturm reports, and 1,034 nonzero coordinate-edge flags. For orientation I canceled common roots, checked that the remaining old polynomial had simple roots, and used exact Wronskian sign checks based on square-free factorization and rational Sturm arithmetic. The full screen records 6,651 rays and 18,617 edges. The expanded archive records 75,723 rays and 283,239 edges across its six stated shape ranges. Both archives report zero failures. The larger totals are archive summaries. This review independently certifies only the stated small-dimensional replay.
