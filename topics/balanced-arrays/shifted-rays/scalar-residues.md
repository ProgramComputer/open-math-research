# A scalar comparison by residues

If $H(z)=\sum_{s=0}^r h_s z^s$ has positive, log-concave coefficients and $r<d$, write $H(z)^n=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}z^i F_i(z^d)$. The nonzero sections are real-rooted and weakly interlace in descending residue order. This scalar fact applies to the labeled array counts after imposing only a total-size constraint. Imposing each column quota individually is the remaining condition. The assertion follows from the interlacing-preserving matrix criterion of [Brändén, Theorem 8.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.6601#page=22); an elementary argument is included below. The [all-ones Veronese kernel](https://doi.org/10.1017/prm.2018.76) is a related special case. No claim of priority is made.

Fix $q$ in the open upper half-plane and write $\theta=\arg q\in(0,\pi)$. Extend section values to integer indices by $f_{j+d}=q^{-1}f_j$. Consider nonzero values with successive arguments admitting a nonincreasing lift $\alpha_j$ and $\alpha_{j+d}=\alpha_j-\theta$. Multiplication by $H$ updates the lifted sequence by $g_i=\sum_s h_s f_{i-s}$. Its terms have arguments within an interval of width at most $\theta$, hence the sum never vanishes. Choose the lifted argument of $g_i$ between the extreme lifted arguments of its terms.

The union of the index windows for $g_i,g_{i+1}$ has span $r+1\le d$. For every $u<v$ in this union, $\mathrm{Im}(\overline f_v f_u)\ge0$. Extend $h$ by zero outside its interval support. Expanding $\mathrm{Im}(\overline g_{i+1} g_i)$ as a sum indexed by $u<v$ gives each such imaginary part the coefficient
$$
h_{i-u}h_{i+1-v}-h_{i-v}h_{i+1-u}\ge0 .
$$
Indeed consecutive positive ratios of $h$ decrease, and zeros beyond either endpoint preserve this inequality. The two lifted arguments were chosen inside the common interval of width $\theta<\pi$; the sign just proved forces the argument of $g_i$ to be at least that of $g_{i+1}$. The periodic decrease by $\theta$ persists. Start with $f_0=1$, $f_j=\epsilon>0$ for $1\le j<d$, and iterate. Each nonzero section has no upper-half-plane zero and successive quotients have the proper half-plane sign there. Let $\epsilon$ decrease to zero; the fixed-degree coefficient limits give the sections of $H^n$, with the usual convention for zero polynomials. The same angle argument for any two residues in a period gives pairwise weak interlacing via the real-rooted pencil criterion, including common roots by closure. For $n=0$ only the constant section is nonzero.

The span and Toeplitz determinant argument depends on a one-dimensional index. For the actual balanced arrays the coordinate-shift indices live in $\mathbb Z^d/\mathbb Z\mathbf1$. Convolving with one capped row involves a subset of a Boolean cube, which includes incomparable shifts. Comparing just the arguments on comparable pairs does not supply the displayed two-term determinant comparison for an adjacent pair of output rays.
