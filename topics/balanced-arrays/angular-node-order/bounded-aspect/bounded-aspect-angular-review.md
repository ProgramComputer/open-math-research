## Fixed row profiles and averaging

For a compact density interval, regular profiles have all $p_i(1-p_i)\ge c>0$ and total precision $\lambda=d\sum_i p_i(1-p_i)/(d-1)\asymp n$. The hypothesis $d\asymp n$ lets one fix a single $T$ with $v_i\ge1/T$ and $d\ge n/T$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Enlarging it if needed, $A-n/d\ge n/(2T)$ and the remaining hypotheses of the Bessel tail proof hold eventually. Its aligned-region decay is $\exp[-c_T n^{1/5}]$ up to polynomial factors per profile, and its diffuse-region decay is stronger. This removes all of the Fourier domain except the chart of diameter $n^{-2/5}$. In the gauge fixing the last column angle, the quadratic-form determinant is $\lambda^{d-1}/d$, so the normalization is $\sqrt d(2\pi\lambda)^{-(d-1)/2}$. The total size $dk$ cancels the common-angle phase, including on profiles with every row at the cap. [Pinned Fourier tail](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/6a91a65e940e8aa9adadb3be9fe344266eb22d59/topics/balanced-arrays/effective-sublinear/direct-row-tail.md)

In the local expansion, the row logarithm remainder has Gaussian $L^2$ norm $O(d n^{-6/5})=o(1)$ on the chart, and its real part is uniformly $O(nd n^{-12/5})=o(1)$. The quartic mean is $O(d/\lambda)=O(1)$ and the centered quartic term has $L^2$ norm $O(\sqrt d/\lambda)=o(1)$. The exponential moment needed for these replacements is taken on the chart. In the normal coordinates one inserts the independent event $|\bar Z|\le1$ at bounded cost and truncates the independent $Z_j$ at $O(n^{1/10})$. The quartic exponential then costs at most $\exp(O(d/\lambda))$. These bounds permit the same replacement of the quartic by its mean when the earlier explicit numerical lower bound on $\lambda/d$ is replaced by an arbitrary positive constant. [Pinned row cumulants](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/6a91a65e940e8aa9adadb3be9fe344266eb22d59/topics/balanced-arrays/effective-sublinear/direct-row-expansion.md)

The cubic central limit step supplies the missing positive lower bound. The leading term is the normalized sum of $Z_j^3-3Z_j$, of mean zero and variance six. For the correction, $\lVert \bar Z\rVert_4=O(d^{-1/2})$ and $\lVert \sum_j(Z_j^2-1)\rVert_4=O(\sqrt d)$, so their product has bounded $L^2$ norm. Also $\lVert d\bar Z^3\rVert_2=O(d^{-1/2})$. Thus the correction after division by $\sqrt d$ tends to zero in $L^2$. The first absolute moments of $C_d$ are bounded, so the characteristic expectations are equicontinuous in $\xi$ and their convergence to $e^{-3\xi^2}$ is uniform on every bounded interval. In particular they exceed a positive constant on the bounded interval of $\xi$ furnished by the compact density and aspect ranges. Together with the bounded quartic mean this makes the normalized probability uniformly positive on regular profiles, allowing a logarithmic comparison.

The monotone scalar coupling applies on the finite integer interval supports, including the saturated total. Its adjacent profiles have $\sum_i|p_i'-p_i|=1$. Thus the changes in $\lambda,\Gamma,A_{\ast},dB_{\ast}$ are bounded and the changes in $\xi$ and the quartic mean are $O(n^{-1})$. The determinant factor has log change $O(n^{-1/4})$ since $|B'-B|\le4n^{-1/4}$ and the compact density bound keeps $v$ positive. The Gaussian cubic expectations at the two endpoints may be compared using the same $C_d$ and its bounded first absolute moment. This proves the regular-pair estimate before averaging. [Pinned coupling and variation estimates](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/6a91a65e940e8aa9adadb3be9fe344266eb22d59/topics/balanced-arrays/effective-sublinear/direct-comparison.md)

The two irregularity measures have distinct uses. Under the scalar marginal, convex domination by the binomial and conditioning at its integer total mean give the Hoeffding tail divided by a probability at least $1/(nd+1)$. The point-mass law at saturation satisfies the same estimates. Under the actual balanced marginal the two-row argument instead bounds the probability of a large gap by $2e^{-d\eta^2/2}$. Neither estimate needs the earlier density window. On regular profiles the normalized weight is bounded below by a positive constant and above by $C e^{CB}$. Integrating the scalar Hoeffding bound yields $\mathbb E e^{2C(S/d-x)^2}\le1+O_C(d^{-1})$, so the regular weighted second moment is $O((nd+1)e^{C'n/d})$. The weighted mass of its own irregular marginal is the actual-law tail times the full expectation. On own regularity, the weighted mass of its partner's irregular marginal is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz using the scalar tail and this second moment. Hence the discarded fractions are $o(1)$ uniformly on the compact ranges. The adjacent log comparison follows, and telescoping gives all fixed feasible offsets. ## Phase and passage to the signed sum

Let $A_w(t)=\mathbb E_w[q_S e^{itS}]/\mathbb E_w[e^{itS}]$. The identity $q_j\binom dj=\binom{d-2}{j-1}$ gives $A_w(t)=we^{it}B(we^{it})/H(we^{it})$. For $0\le t\le\theta$, the sector arguments of $H$ and $B$ start at zero, so the lift of $\arg A_w(t)$ is $t+\arg B-\arg H$. This makes $n\arg H-\nu\arg A_w(\theta)$ precisely the true phase. In each Taylor expansion the small centered transforms are near their values on the real axis uniformly over the same interval of angles. Their analytic logarithms therefore give the sector lifts. [Pinned phase ordering](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/8d9a0cfdd12bf23086bcc2db05691310b9d3a126/topics/balanced-arrays/angular-node-order/README.md)

When $V/d\to v_0>0$, put $z=y/d+it$ at bounded $y$ and $t=\theta$. For the centered size transform the third moment gives logarithm $Vz^2/2+O(d^{-3/2})$. For the precision, subtract $a$ from $q_S$ inside the transform. The resulting numerator is $c z+O(d^{-3/2})$, with $c=\mathrm{Cov}(q_S,S)=O(V/d)$. This uses $|q_S-a|\le(2/d)(|S-\mu|+\sqrt V)$ and $|z(S-\mu)|=O(1)$. The centered denominator is $1+O(d^{-1})$ and $a\ge x(1-x)$, so the precision logarithm has linear coefficient $c/a$. Multiplication by $n$ and $\nu$ makes both cubic errors $o(1)$. The phase slope in $y$ is $\pi nV/d^2\to\pi v_0/s_0>0$, making bracketing and the substitution at $w_{\ast}$ valid. Direct quadratic covariance gives $c/a=(V/d)g'(x)+O(d^{-1/2})$. Thus $d\log(w_{\ast}/w_0)=\nu g'(x)/n+o(1)$. Its sign cancels the neighboring balance drift $-\nu g'(x)j/n$ after multiplying by the signed offset $j$.

For the scalar local limit in this case and in the diverging-deficit case, the row mass is log-concave with interval support, $n\mu_j=d(k+j)$ is an integer, and the variance tends to infinity at the center. The quoted Lipschitz bound for the standard deviation and a first-exit argument give relative variance stability on the fixed mean interval where it is used. The numerical lower threshold on standard deviation in the scalar estimate is eventually met, along with $\sigma\le d\le n$. Its scalar prefactor ratio is $1+o(1)$ at every fixed feasible offset. In the proportional case all fixed offsets become feasible since bounded deficit would give bounded variance. The remaining entropy drift is $-ns^2 j^2/(2V)+o(1)$ and $ns^2/V\to s_0/v_0$. Log-concavity gives geometric domination from the convergent first outward ratios on both sides. The limiting signed sum is the strictly positive Jacobi product with parameter $e^{-s_0/(2v_0)}$. [Pinned scalar local limit](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/7210ef194132ebad5d0e2ebda61c2aadccc973a4/topics/balanced-arrays/adjacent-quotas/scalar.md)

When $V/d\to0$ and $h\to\infty$, the variance lower bound gives $V\gtrsim sh\to\infty$. The same local stability holds. The centered scalar argument costs $O(nV^{3/2}/d^3)=o(1)$, and the precision contribution costs $O(V/d)=o(1)$ after multiplication by $\nu$. The latter follows by centering and applying the two-copy covariance identity with Lipschitz constants $2/d$ and $t$ for $q_S$ and $e^{itS}$. Bracketing by means $\mu\pm\epsilon$ for each fixed small $\epsilon$ is valid in the open support interval. Then $m_{\ast}-\mu=o(1)$ implies $\log(w_{\ast}/w_0)=o(1/V)$. Its contribution is $o(n/V)$ and is dominated by the negative entropy term at the two adjacent offsets. The profile ratios stay bounded, so the actual outward ratios vanish. [Pinned variance bound](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/27e3e70d6603e8a395c5fdb7ff944922bce83380/topics/balanced-arrays/engset-curvature/carried-load.md)

When $h\to h_0<\infty$, internality gives $h_0\ge1$ and $r/d\to x_0$. At fixed odds near $w_\infty$ the deficit ratios have maximum at deficit zero, stay uniformly below one, and converge at every fixed index to $x_0/[(1-x_0)w]$. This proves the geometric limit and a uniform exponential moment on a neighborhood of the limiting odds. The mean and phase brackets therefore put both $w_0$ and $w_{\ast}$ in this neighborhood and give their common limit. Writing the likelihood comparison using the deficit sum cancels the term of order $nr$, leaving an $o(n)$ log cost between these tilts. The center atom is $e^{-o(n)}$ by the integer-mean bound at $w_0$. The adjacent deficit averages differ from the mean at $w_{\ast}$ by a positive limiting gap in either direction. Uniform Chernoff estimates give $e^{-c n}$ even for an adjacent saturated total. The balance comparison includes that saturated neighbor, so both actual outward ratios again vanish. In both vanishing-ratio cases, pair signed terms from offset two outward using log-concavity.

For clarity, the sequential statement is uniform in the following qualitative sense. Fix $0<\delta<1/2$ and $1\le K<\infty$. If failures persisted at arbitrarily large heights with $x\in[\delta,1-\delta]$ and $n/d\in[1,K]$, take a subsequence with limits for both coordinates and for $V/d$. In the vanishing variance-ratio case take a further subsequence of bounded or diverging deficit. The three proven estimates exclude each possible failure subsequence. The cutoff can depend on $\delta$ and $K$.
