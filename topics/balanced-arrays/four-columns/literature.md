# Literature and priority check for the four-column balanced arrays

Checked 26 November 2026. This is a scope and literature screen for the target in [ProgramComputer/open-math-research #2](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2). The public sources examined did not supply a proof for every row count at the original four-column weights or a resolution of the full conjecture.

## Original statement and prior work

Per Alexandersson’s SymCat entry, on a page displaying 24 November 2026, and his public issue opened 24 October 2026 state the conjecture for every integer $n\ge0$ and $1\le r\le d$. Let $a_{n,k}^{(r,d)}$ count labeled $n\times d$ binary matrices whose column sums all equal $k$ and whose row sums are at most $r$. All zeros of
$$
P_n^{(r,d)}(q)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_{n,k}^{(r,d)}q^k
=\sum_{k\ge0}[x_1^k\cdots x_d^k](1+e_1(x)+\cdots+e_r(x))^nq^k
$$
are asserted to be real. Any zeros are negative because the coefficients are nonnegative and the constant term is 1. Both sources single out $U_{2,4}$, with degree $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, as the first open case. Consecutive interlacing is an additional question. The public statement is an unnumbered web conjecture with no printed page or journal version located. [SymCat, “Balanced arrays of uniform matroids”](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays) · [Alexandersson’s issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005)

- Alexandersson reports exact finite real-rootedness and consecutive interlacing for $r=2$, $4\le d\le12$, $n\le40$. He also reports a local AI-assisted argument for eventually simple negative zeros of $U_{2,4}$ using two endpoint limits and uniform complex saddle estimates. The public record states that the argument lacks an independent review, formalization, and an explicit cutoff. The argument itself was unavailable for comparison. This is a prior eventual claim at the original weights. [Alexandersson’s issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005)
- The author’s issue timeline links directly to the AI-assisted investigation of the full conjecture in solved-conjectures #165. Its public work reports exact corank-one coefficient and Newton-inequality screens and bounded higher-rank computations. Newton inequalities are necessary for real-rootedness. The researchers released their work reservation on 24 November with the issue open. [Timeline](https://api.github.com/repos/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005/timeline) · [Screens](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/165#issuecomment-7058747753) · [Release](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/165#issuecomment-7120876995)
- The repository already records an eventual theorem for each fixed rank and dimension and an all-$n$ corank-one theorem. The latter covers cap two in three columns. Within the four-column problem, each fixed slice by the number of pure pair rows has a real-rooted diagonal after multiplying coefficient $k$ by $k!$. The all-$n$ problem concerns the sum over the slices at the prescribed array weights, with the original coefficients. The full paired bivariate refinement loses real stability for $n\ge6$. The common-pair-weight deformation can have nonreal zeros for some positive weights. This leaves the specific value 1, which assigns weight 1 to each allowed row support. A theorem proved at that weight for the original unscaled polynomial would address the actual $U_{2,4}$ case. A theorem only for a factorial-rescaled full polynomial would need an explicit root-preserving transfer or recovery of the original polynomial. [Tracking issue and scope](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/issues/2) · [Factorial-weighted slices and paired stability](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/53) · [Falling-factorial method](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/56) · [Common pair weight](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/66)
- Other names for the count include equal-margin row-capped binary matrices, balanced bipartite graphs with a degree bound on one side, equal-exponent elementary-symmetric diagonals, and Pólya frequency of the coefficient rows. Prescribed-margin enumeration results give counts or asymptotics at individual margins. The classical matching formulation here sums over matching size in a graph that changes with $k$. Its matching zeros therefore do not give the zeros of $P_n^{(2,d)}$. The uniform-matroid interlacing theorem of Xie and Zhang concerns $Z$-polynomials. [Prescribed-margin enumeration](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0606496) · [Author’s matching formula](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005) · [Xie–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07636)

## Eligibility and review point

A proof for all row counts at $U_{2,4}$ would settle the first case identified by the conjecture’s author and establish Pólya frequency for every row of its coefficient family. It would constitute an infinite subfamily of the full statement. A certified nonreal zero at any dimensions and row count would disprove the full conjecture. The exact unweighted sum is the current obstacle. The plausible route in #2 is to make the fixed-parameter analytic proof effective by deriving explicit bounds in all saddle and endpoint regimes, then use exact negative-root counts for the remaining rows. The decision point is a proved finite cutoff with a feasible certificate, an exact counterexample, or a quantified obstruction that rules out a proposed route. Extending the positive grid alone would not pass this review point.

The negative search is limited to the available public record. It cannot establish priority against unpublished work, unindexed statements, or formulations without overlapping terminology.
