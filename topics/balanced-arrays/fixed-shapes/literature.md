Full row cap gives $`a_k=\prod_{j=1}^d e_k(X_j)`$ for independent column alphabets, so the ordinary Jacobi–Trudi specialization of shape $`R=\lambda/\mu`$ is exactly the Hadamard product of $`d`$ copies of Angarone–Kim–Oh–Soskin's $`\mathcal E_R`$, one per alphabet. Remark 2.1 identifies the determinant of one $`\mathcal E_R`$ with $`s_{R'}`$. Their Theorem 1.3 proves separate-alphabet Schur positivity, including for the determinant, for any number of factors when every input shape avoids a three-row by two-column rectangle. This covers every ribbon. Theorem 3.10 gives explicit ribbon expansions, with Theorems 1.4 and 4.4 treating the same ribbon in two alphabets. This uncapped overlap is also recorded in the [ribbon theorem’s literature note](../koszul-ribbons/literature.md). The hereditary row restrictions couple the alphabets and do not have this factorization. [AKOS v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08969v1).

## Fixed-shape comparison

No exact precedent for the following threshold surfaced. For $`0\le a\le b`$ and $`0\le s\le(b-a)/2`$, a monomial in $`e_ae_b`$ with $`c`$ doubled variables has coefficient ratio in $`e_{a+s}e_{b-s}`$ versus $`e_ae_b`$ equal to $`\prod_{t=0}^{s-1}(b-c-t)/(a+s-c-t)`$. This is at least its value at $`c=0`$, namely $`a!b!/[(a+s)!(b-s)!]`$.

Write $`u_i=\lambda_i-i,\ v_i=\mu_i-i,\ p_i=u_i-v_i,\ q_i=u_i-v_{\sigma(i)}`$ for a determinant permutation $`\sigma`$. Discard terms with any negative $`q_i`$. Swapping an inversion strictly balances the two affected indices, preserving their sum and nonnegativity. The old difference is the sum of the two positive gaps $`u_i-u_j`$ and $`v_{\sigma(j)}-v_{\sigma(i)}`$ for $`i<j`$, and the new absolute difference is their absolute difference. Iterate to the identity. The coefficient bounds telescope and show that the identity term in one alphabet dominates the permutation term by $`\rho_\sigma=\prod q_i!/\prod p_i!>1`$ for each feasible nonidentity permutation. In $`d`$ independent alphabets the factor becomes $`\rho_\sigma^d`$. Thus $`\sum_{\sigma\text{ odd, feasible}}\rho_\sigma^{-d}\le1`$ is sufficient, by bounding the negative terms and dropping the other even terms. This yields a threshold depending on the fixed shape, uniform in alphabet sizes. It leaves the general two-factor conjecture unchanged.

The same threshold covers the factorial-weighted expression of Conjecture 5.1 whenever $`d-s\ge D`$, since the additional factorials reduce the exponent in the permutation-to-identity comparison from $`d`$ to $`d-s`$.
