The submission uses this exact polynomial. The main theorem proves simple negative roots for every fixed $`\alpha_0>0`$ once $`n`$ is sufficiently large in $`n\ge\alpha_0d^2`$, uniformly in the cap. The corollary covers all dimensions $`d\ge2`$ for each fixed bound $`r\le M\log n`$ and sufficiently large $`n`$. Dimension one has $`P=(1+q)^n`$. This is a partial result on the conjecture. For example, the two new sufficient conditions leave $`d=\lfloor n^{2/3}\rfloor,\ r=\lfloor d/2\rfloor`$ outside their range. The original conjecture also includes all small row counts. ## Fixed-corank coefficients

The profile truncation at $`\lceil\log d\rceil`$ works under both measures. Under the actual array measure, the forward switching has exactly $`s(n-\ell)`$ choices for each row of size $`s>c`$. Each output has at most $`(d-s+1)\ell`$ reverse choices. This yields a factorial bound by iteration, even if some profile masses vanish. Under the independent tilted law, the successive weight ratio yields a separate factorial tail. Conditioning on the exact sum costs at most $`nd+1`$ by log-concavity and Jensen at the integer mean. The conditional contribution from profiles with a medium or large exceptional row is therefore superpolynomially small under the scalar profile law, while the actual arrays with such a row are controlled by switching. The cap on retained profiles makes the exponential margin correction at most polynomial. The Hoeffding estimate for the clipped sample variance survives both the conditioning and that weight.

The hypotheses of Greenhill–McKay–Wang Corollary 5.2 hold with total $`d\ell\asymp d^2`$, maximum-degree product $`O(d\log d)`$, $`\nu_2=0`$, and $`\mu_2=V_{\mathbf s}/[m(1-p)]=O(\log d)`$. Its exponent is $`-1/2+V_{\mathbf s}/[2m(1-p)]`$ and its error is $`O(d^{-1/3}\log d)`$. At a positive limiting reflected excess the limiting truncated Poisson law has span one and positive variance. Fourier inversion gives the scalar normalization, so the curvature limit follows from the saddle exponent's second derivative. The complementary log-radius correction is $`-d/(2\ell)+o(1)`$. The determinant phase shifts $`\Psi/\pi`$ by $`+V/(2m)+o(1)`$, which compensates for the negative radius correction. Equation (1) confines the first hit to bounded hole mean. The bounded-moment phase expansion then forces its mean to the same interior limit as at the geometric radius, so the derivative of order $`n/d`$ applies between those two points. At a vanishing reflected excess, the variance tends to zero and the scalar curvature diverges by the bound below. ### Reconstructing the scalar bound

For positive log-concave weights $`w_j`$ on $`0\le j\le L`$, put $`W(z)=\sum_j w_jz^j`$, $`C_e=[z^e]W(z)^n`$ and $`R_e=\binom{n+e-1}{e}`$. Given total $`e`$ under the product weights, reweight the first coordinate by $`j+1`$. Its resulting marginal is proportional to $`(j+1)w_j[z^{e-j}]W(z)^{n-1}`$. For $`n>1`$, as $`e`$ increases by one this marginal moves up in likelihood-ratio order, because the coefficients of $`W^{n-1}`$ are log-concave and the ends of the marginal's support move to the right. The ratio $`(C_{e+1}/C_e)/(R_{e+1}/R_e)`$ is the expectation under this marginal of the decreasing sequence $`w_{j+1}/w_j`$, with $`w_{L+1}=0`$. This follows by differentiating $`W^n`$ and using the conditional mean $`e/n`$ of the first coordinate before reweighting. Thus $`C_e/R_e`$ is log-concave. The $`n=1`$ case is immediate.

For feasible $`e-d,e,e+d`$, the logarithm of the curvature is therefore at least that of $`R`$. Each of the $`d`$ step terms of the latter is
$`\log(1+d/u)-\log(1+d/(u+n-1))`$
for $`u=e-d+1,\ldots,e`$. Integrating over the step length gives the lower bound $`(n-1)/(n+e+d-1)\log(1+d/e)`$ on each term. With $`w_j=\binom d{c+j}`$ this proves the claimed divergence for $`d\le e=o(d^2)`$, including a neighbor at exact saturation. The manuscript can make this dependency reproducible by including or pinning the scalar lemma.
