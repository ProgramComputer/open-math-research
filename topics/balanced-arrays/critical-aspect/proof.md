# Every positive quadratic aspect and every fixed logarithmic rank multiple

**Theorem.** Given any real $`\alpha_0>0`$ there exists an $`N_{\alpha_0}`$ such that for every $`n\ge N_{\alpha_0}`$, every $`d\ge2`$ with $`n\ge\alpha_0d^2`$, and every $`1\le r\le d`$, all $`D=\lfloor nr/d\rfloor`$ roots of the balanced-array polynomial are distinct, real and negative. The coefficient $`a_k`$ counts labeled binary $`n\times d`$ matrices with common column sum $`k`$ and row sums at most $`r`$.

**Corollary.** For every fixed $`M>0`$ and all sufficiently large $`n`$ depending on $`M`$, the same holds for every dimension $`d\ge2`$ and every admissible $`r\le M\log n`$. Thus in any infinite sequence of distinct counterexamples to the all-parameter real-rootedness conjecture, both $`d/\sqrt n`$ and $`r/\log n`$ must tend to infinity. At $`d=1`$ the polynomial is $`(1+q)^n`$. [Original conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

## What is left by the quadratic bound

The earlier absolute bound gives all the roots at $`n\ge C d^2`$, and gives all dimensions at $`r\le c_*\log n`$ for a particular absolute $`c_*>0`$ and large $`n`$. Suppose the present theorem fails for a fixed $`\alpha_0>0`$. Choose failures with $`n\to\infty`$. Passing to a subsequence, the remaining case is $`d\to\infty`$, $`n/d^2\to\alpha\in[\alpha_0,C]`$ and $`r>c_*\log n`$. Full rank is already known, so $`r<d`$. We may further assume that the corank $`d-r`$ either tends to infinity or is a fixed positive integer. Put $`T=nr/d`$, $`D=\lfloor T\rfloor`$. Then $`D/d\to\infty`$ since $`r\to\infty`$. All coefficients $`a_0,\ldots,a_D`$ are positive by the cyclic column fill. [Quadratic theorem, full-cap references and cyclic fill](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/quadratic/proof.md)

Use the phase function $`\Psi(w)`$, first hitting points $`\Psi(v_k)=k\pi`$ and geometric radii $`t_k=\sqrt{a_{k-1}/a_{k+1}}`$ from that proof. For clarity, write $`\phi=\pi/d`$, $`\nu=(d-1)/2`$, $`H(w)=\sum_{s\le r}\binom ds w^s`$, and let $`S`$ have that normalized positive tilt. Put $`\mu=\mathbb ES`$, $`V=\mathrm{Var}(S)`$, $`h=r-\mu`$, $`y=n\mu/d`$, and $`G(s)=s(d-s)/(d(d-1))`$. Then

$$
\Psi(w)=n\arg H(we^{i\phi})-\nu\arg\frac{\mathbb E[G(S)e^{i\phi S}]}{\mathbb E e^{i\phi S}},
\qquad
|\Psi(w)/\pi-y|\le C_0d+C_0\min(y,T-y)/\sqrt d .
\tag{1}
$$

The estimate and the existence and ordering of the first hitting points depend only on the tilt. The inequality implies that $`k=O(d)`$ forces $`y(v_k)=O(d)`$ and $`T-k=O(d)`$ forces $`T-y(v_k)=O(d)`$. If $`k/d\to\infty`$, then $`y(v_k)/d\to\infty`$, and the same implication holds for $`(T-k)/d`$ and $`(T-y(v_k))/d`$.

## The low column sums

When $`k/d\to0`$, normalized log-concavity gives $`\Gamma_k=a_k^2/(a_{k-1}a_{k+1})\ge(1+1/k)^d\to\infty`$, so the geometric center has sign $`(-1)^k`$. If $`k/d\to\beta\in(0,\infty)`$, the degree is sufficiently far from these indices that both distances to the endpoints diverge. For $`i=k+O(1)`$ choose independent uniform $`i`$-subsets, one per column. The expected number of forbidden $`(r+1)`$-subsets within rows is at most $`n(di/n)^{r+1}/(r+1)!=o(1)`$, since $`di/n=O(1)`$ and $`r>c_*\log n`$. Thus $`a_i=\binom ni^d(1+o(1))`$. It follows that $`\log\Gamma_{k+j}\to1/\beta`$ on every fixed translate, and

$$
\log t_k=d\log(k/(n-k))+d/(2k)+o(1).
\tag{2}
$$

We check the phase shift at the first hitting point. Since $`y=O(d)`$ there and $`n/d^2\to\alpha`$, we have $`\mu=O(1)`$. This forces $`u=dw=O(1)`$ uniformly along that sequence. If $`u\to\infty`$, the weight at any fixed size $`s+1`$ dominates the sum through $`s`$, since $`\binom di/d^i\to1/i!`$ for each fixed $`i`$, and $`r\to\infty`$. That contradicts the bounded mean. For bounded $`u`$, truncating the binomial law at $`r`$ changes each fixed-order raw moment of positive order by $`o(d^{-A})`$ for any fixed $`A`$. The error is also $`o(\mu d^{-A})`$ uniformly at vanishing mean, since the deleted tail is bounded by $`\sum_{s>r}s^j u^s/s!`$ and the size-one mass is at least $`u e^{-u}`$. These bounds apply equally to the $`G`$-weighted mean after division by $`\mathbb E G\ge\mathbb P(S=1)/d`$.

Consequently $`V=\mu(1+o(1))`$ and the $`G`$-weighted mean minus $`\mu`$ is $`1+o(1)`$ uniformly here. In the unrestricted binomial law the latter difference equals $`1-2p`$, with $`p=w/(1+w)=O(1/d)`$: weighting $`S`$ by $`S(d-S)`$ leaves the law of $`1+\mathrm{Bin}(d-2,p)`$. The centered third absolute moments of both the original and weighted laws are uniformly bounded. Taylor at $`\phi`$ now gives $`\Psi(v_k)/\pi=y(v_k)-1/2+o(1)`$ since $`n\phi^3=o(1)`$ and $`d\phi^3=o(1)`$. Hence $`y(v_k)=k+1/2+o(1)`$. If $`w_x`$ denotes the tilt with $`y=x`$, on bounded translates of $`k`$ we have $`\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}(d\log w_x)=d^2/(n V_x)\to1/\beta`$. At $`x=k`$ the same deleted-tail comparison identifies $`d\log w_k=d\log(k/(n-k))+o(1)`$. Equation (2) gives $`d\log v_k=\log t_k+o(1)`$.

At $`x=t_k`$ or $`v_k^d`$, the centered alternating sum divided by $`a_k x^k`$ and signed by $`(-1)^k`$ tends to $`\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^j e^{-j^2/(2\beta)}>0`$. The two immediate relative magnitudes are set by the center, the fixed-offset others by their consecutive curvatures, and the tails are bounded by geometric ratios using global log-concavity. Positivity is Gaussian Poisson summation. ## Remaining angular signs and the bounded-corank endpoint

Whenever $`\mu\to\infty`$ and $`d-\mu\to\infty`$ at a first hitting point, $`d\mathbb E G(S)\to\infty`$. Indeed $`V\le\mu(1-\mu/d)`$ gives $`d\mathbb E G(S)\ge\mu(d-\mu)/d`$. The critical-aspect angular lemma then gives $`(-1)^kP(-v_k^d)>0`$. Its fourth-order logarithmic term has an additional cancellation: the coefficient of $`(\sum_i y_i^2)^2`$ has size $`O(\mathbb E G(S)/d)`$ at the relevant phases. This supplies a vanishing integrated error at fixed aspect even though an absolute fourth-moment error could stay of order one. [Angular lemma](critical-aspect-angular-nodes.md) · [Fourth-order identity](fourth-transverse-cumulant.md)

If $`d-r\to\infty`$, use geometric radii for $`k\le d`$ and first hitting radii $`v_k^d`$ thereafter. At a sequence of indices with $`k/d\to\infty`$, (1) gives $`\mu\to\infty`$, and $`d-\mu\ge d-r\to\infty`$ independently of quota. The angular lemma applies all the way to the final internal index. All sequences of indices with $`k/d`$ bounded are covered by the preceding low-quota arguments.

If $`d-r=c`$ is fixed, use geometric radii also for $`D-k\le d`$. When $`k/d,(D-k)/d\to\infty`$, (1) gives $`\mu\to\infty`$ and $`h\to\infty`$, so once again $`d-\mu=c+h\to\infty`$. For sequences with $`(D-k)/d`$ bounded, reflect the array into holes. Rows now have at least $`c`$ holes and the common hole quota is $`n-k`$. The complementary coefficient lemma proves a strict geometric-center sign when $`(D-k)/d\to0`$. For $`(D-k)/d\to\beta\in(0,\infty)`$ it gives an adjacent log curvature tending to $`1/(\alpha V_*)>0`$, where $`V_*`$ is the variance of a Poisson law conditioned to be at least $`c`$ with tilt chosen for the limiting mean $`c+\beta/\alpha`$. The matching phase point satisfies $`d\log v_k=\log t_k+o(1)`$ and both centers have the required sign. To see that this applies to the *first* hitting point, (1) gives $`T-y=O(d)`$ there, hence bounded hole mean. The bounded-moment phase expansion in the complementary lemma gives $`y=k+O(1)`$ at every such crossing: its remainder estimates are uniform for $`c\le m\le M`$ at fixed $`c,M`$, even as $`m\downarrow c`$, because the size law has a uniform factorial tail and $`\mathbb E G\asymp1/d`$. Thus $`m\to c+\beta/\alpha>c`$. The lemma's positive derivative and expansion apply in that neighborhood. Equivalently, both radii have the same signed correction $`-d/(2(n-k))+o(1)`$ relative to the tilt at quota $`k`$. [Complementary coefficients and phase](fixed-corank-critical-upper.md)

At each join the two neighboring geometric log radii differ by half the sum of the adjacent log curvatures. The limit is $`1`$ at the lower join and is $`1/(\alpha V_*)>0`$ at the bounded-corank upper join. Replacing one by its designated phase radius changes the logarithm by $`o(1)`$. Together with strict log-concavity and the ordering of the first hitting points this gives one strictly ordered family. The endpoint signs are $`1`$ at zero and $`(-1)^D`$ at positive infinity. Any hypothetical interior sign failure yields a subsequence with $`k/d`$ and $`(D-k)/d`$ convergent in $`[0,\infty]`$, and all cases above contradict it. Alternation gives $`D`$ distinct simple negative roots, contradicting the sequence of failures.

## Logarithmic ranks

For a given $`M>0`$ use $`\varepsilon=1/10`$ and its dimension constant $`B`$ in the rank-dependent bound. Choose $`\alpha_0`$ with $`0<\alpha_0<1/(B^2M)`$. At $`n\ge\alpha_0d^2`$ the theorem applies once $`n`$ is sufficiently large for this $`\alpha_0`$. Otherwise $`d^2>n/\alpha_0>B^2 M n\ge B^2 n r/\log n`$ for $`r\le M\log n`$. Also $`r\le n^{1/10}`$ eventually in that range. The rank-dependent theorem covers this case. Finally, distinct counterexample triples force $`n\to\infty`$ since $`d\ge n`$, $`d\ge2`$ is already covered by coefficient separation. Applying the results for every fixed $`M`$ and $`\alpha_0`$ proves both divergence statements. All row thresholds are existential.
