\begin{abstract}
For each fixed positive $\alpha$, the theorem places all zeros
of the balanced-array polynomial on the strictly negative real axis,
with multiplicity one, whenever $n\ge\alpha d^2$ and $n$ is sufficiently
large depending on $\alpha$. The dimension and the row cap may vary.
Combined with the complementary rank-dependent dimension bound, the
result covers every fixed logarithmic cap constant in every dimension
at large row counts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement}
The \href{https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm\#conjBalancedArrays}{balanced-array conjecture of Per Alexandersson}
asks for real-rootedness throughout the parameter range. Write
$P_n^{(r,d)}(q)=\sum_k a_kq^k$, where $a_k$ counts labeled binary
$n\times d$ arrays with every column sum $k$ and every row sum at most $r$.

\begin{theorem}
For every $\alpha>0$ there is $N_\alpha$ such that for every $n\ge N_\alpha$,
every $d\ge2$ with $n\ge\alpha d^2$, and every $1\le r\le d$, the polynomial
$P_n^{(r,d)}$ has exactly $\lfloor nr/d\rfloor$ simple, strictly negative zeros.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}
For every $M>0$ there is $N_M$ such that for every $n\ge N_M$, the same
conclusion holds in every dimension $d\ge2$ for $1\le r\le\min(d,M\log n)$.
\end{corollary}
The thresholds are existential. At $d=1$, the polynomial is $(1+q)^n$.
The general conjecture remains open. In any infinite sequence of distinct
counterexamples to real-rootedness, $d/\sqrt n$ and $r/\log n$ both tend to
infinity. The row counts must tend to infinity by
\href{https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/quadratic/proof.md}{the bounded-row reduction in the quadratic proof}.
