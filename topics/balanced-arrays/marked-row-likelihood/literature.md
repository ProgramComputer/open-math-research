# Sources, prior comparisons and scope

On 26 November 2026 the primary SymCat page, under “Graphs, matroids and arrays,” states real-rootedness for every labeled binary array polynomial with common column sum, row cap $`1\le r\le d`$ and height $`n\ge0`$. The page reports tests at rank two, dimensions 4 through 12 and heights through 40. Its text was verified from the original HTML. The test in this package is sufficient and finite. A proof for the entire conjecture must include triples outside this test. [Original statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

Jeff Kahn and Michael Neiman’s *Conditional negative association for competing urns*, first posted in 2010, is the source of the orientation comparison used here. Lemma 8 in §4 compares orientation counts when in- and outdegree requirements are redistributed. Lemma 10 and its proof already exploit the multiplicity of pairings. Their Theorem 4 compares occupancy ratios of independently placed balls under changes to constraints on other urns. The current application fixes labeled column subsets of distinct rows and compares the size of a particular row when every column margin rises by one. [Preprint, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0610#page=9) · [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20395)

The repository already uses auxiliary rows to equalize two column margins, and already has coefficientwise comparisons that count exclusive-cell pairings. The factor here comes from giving a dummy-to-marked pairing a weight chosen so that each blue-only marked cell incurs the same average penalty. Restricting overlap on a high-probability event gives a sharper factor for unweighted arrays. The earlier finite result treated 506,565 roots at the million-row parameters, and the first proposed complete calculation in PR #184 uses a centered recursion and a two-class switching comparison. The two finite arguments share the earlier signs and mixed-phase estimates. [Earlier orientation comparison](../orientation-normalization.md) · [Pairing factors and earlier sources](../quota-minors/cellwise-balancing-prior.md) · [Reviewed finite bound](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/183) · [First proposed finite proof](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/184)

Swee Hong Chan’s *Normalized matching property for competing urn models* appeared on arXiv on 8 September 2025. Its Lemma 3.1 concerns balanced middle ranks of orientations when specified vertices must have at least one incident edge of each direction. This gives the separate small-cap inclusion theorem after the exact-degree refinements. Theorems 1.4 and 1.5 concern conditioning on nonempty urns or on exact occupancies. Section 7.1 discusses the harder interval setting. For cap four the unrestricted paired array can impose the interval $`[2,4]`$ at a degree-six vertex, outside the direct reduction. The general size likelihood theorem avoids requiring an inclusion coupling. [Chan, §§2–4 and 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06273v1)

The exact-odds refinement adds a pairing at the marked vertex and retains one unpaired marked cell. After conditioning on both marked masks, this cell compares two independent uniform subsets of the other rows. Vandermonde’s identity gives the unrestricted binomial odds exactly. For row-specific weights or intervals the unmarked rows can fail to be exchangeable; the stated exact-odds theorem assumes this symmetry. A separate fixed-seed pilot at heights three through five found no violation of the stronger odds among 4,084 nonzero adjacent comparisons with variable intervals and 1,382 with equal caps and variable weights. These are exploratory counts. The universal weighted result established in the written argument is the coefficientwise bound.
