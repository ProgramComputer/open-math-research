# Priority screen: five-thirds row cutoff and small rank or corank

Screen date: 2026-11-26.

The exact candidates concern the original unweighted $`P_n^{(r,d)}(q)`$, whose coefficient of $`q^k`$ counts labelled binary $`n\times d`$ arrays with equal column quota $`k`$ and row cap $`r`$. The first candidate is an absolute $`C`$ guaranteeing $`\lfloor nr/d\rfloor`$ simple negative roots for every $`d\ge2`$, $`1\le r\le d`$, and $`n\ge Cd^{5/3}`$. The second is an absolute $`c>0`$ and $`N`$ guaranteeing the same property for all $`n\ge N`$ and $`d\ge\max(2,r)`$ whenever $`\min(r,d-r)\le c n^{1/5}`$. This includes $`o(n^{1/5})`$ rank and corank sequences. The author asks for real-rootedness for all parameters and does not require simplicity. [Primary SymCat statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays), [author's open issue #2005](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005).

The current published quadratic theorem #95 (merge 4fb624b) uses $`Cd^2`$ and gives the logarithmic rank corollary. The stronger result, merged to research in #96 and published in #97 at 605cc467a5be93a8408519da331e46f1008df96e, proves every fixed positive quadratic aspect $`n\ge\alpha d^2`$ at large $`n`$, and every fixed logarithmic cap multiple $`r\le M\log n`$ regardless of dimension. The earlier #89 theorem has a dimension threshold $`d\ge C_\varepsilon\sqrt{nr/\log n}`$ and rank branch $`r\le n^{1/5-\varepsilon}`$, with a separate large-dimension alternative. Its $`1/5-\varepsilon`$ exponent in one sufficient rank condition does not give the proposed two-sided $`n^{1/5}`$ all-dimension corollary. The all-row corank-one theorem is a prior exact case, while #91 has one existential row threshold for each fixed cap and all dimensions. [Quadratic publication #95](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/95), [fixed quadratic aspects, published package](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/tree/605cc467a5be93a8408519da331e46f1008df96e/topics/balanced-arrays/critical-aspect), [rank-dependent publication #89](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/89), [corank one #50](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/50), [fixed cap #91](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/pull/91).

On the rank side of the proposed corollary, once the five-thirds theorem is established the complementary dimension range $`n<Cd^{5/3}`$ has $`b=d^2/n>C^{-6/5}n^{1/5}`$. Thus $`r\le c n^{1/5}`$ with sufficiently small $`c`$ gives $`D/d\le r/b`$ small, where the [orientation bound](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/605cc467a5be93a8408519da331e46f1008df96e/topics/balanced-arrays/orientation-normalization.md) supplies the geometric signs. The corank side is a separate coefficient and angular comparison; replacing $`r`$ by $`d-r`$ does not preserve the original cap condition after taking complements.

Greenhill–McKay–Wang Corollary 5.2 is relevant to the underlying capped counts when the prescribed degree product obeys the sparse bound. Its relative exponential error $`O(st/S^{2/3})`$ is $`o(1)`$ at quotas of order $`d`$ with caps $`r=O(b)`$ and $`b=o(d^{1/3})`$. This is an asymptotic fixed-margin input and does not imply a global root count. [GMW author text](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/irregular-final.pdf#page=33), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2005.03.005).
