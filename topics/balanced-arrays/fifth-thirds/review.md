# Independent mathematical referee report

**Review type.** Agent-assisted blind review of the anonymous theorem and proof supplements. I checked the stated conjecture, the new proof, and the relevant prior mathematical inputs.

**Verdict.** I found no substantive mathematical gap in the theorem as stated. The proof gives an absolute height threshold $`n\ge C d^{5/3}`$ for every cap and, for all sufficiently large $`n`$, the two bounds $`r\le c n^{1/5}`$ and $`d-r\le c n^{1/5}`$ in every dimension. In each covered case with $`d\ge2`$ it gives all $`D=\lfloor nr/d\rfloor`$ zeros simple and strictly negative. The constants are existential. The theorem is a partial result on the original conjecture. The complementary parameter range remains open.

## Agreement with the original claim

Alexandersson's problem asks for real-rootedness of the polynomial whose coefficient of $`q^k`$ counts $`n\times d`$ zero-one arrays with each column sum $`k`$ and each row sum at most $`r`$, for every $`d\ge1`$, $`1\le r\le d`$, and $`n\ge0`$. The row and column labels are intrinsic to the array definition. The proof uses this exact polynomial. The cyclic fill realizes every quota from zero to $`D`$, because each row takes at most $`r\le d`$ consecutive positions of the cyclic column sequence. The case $`d=1`$ has $`P(q)=(1+q)^n`$, and simplicity is claimed only when $`d\ge2`$. Constant polynomials satisfy the root assertion vacuously. [Original issue](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2005) · [SymCat conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjBalancedArrays)

## Height proof

- If a universal $`C`$ failed to exist, counterexamples could be selected with $`n/d^{5/3}\to\infty`$. The fixed-positive-quadratic-aspect theorem excludes bounded $`d`$ and any subsequence where $`n/d^2`$ is bounded below. Full rank for $`d\ge2`$ also follows from the unrestricted binomial-power result. In the remaining case $`b=d^2/n\to\infty`$ and $`b=o(d^{1/3})`$. A subsequence of $`r/b`$ has either finite limit or infinite limit. When this limit is infinite, $`D/d\to\infty`$, and the two geometric endpoint bands are disjoint. [Fixed-quadratic-aspect theorem](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/605cc467a5be93a8408519da331e46f1008df96e/topics/balanced-arrays/critical-aspect/proof.md)
- The angular phase uses the argument of each sum on the proper sector of width $`r\pi/d<\pi`$. Its endpoint limits bracket all internal levels, and continuity orders their first hitting times. The height-independent localization inequality implies that if $`k/d`$ diverges, $`y/d=\mu/b`$ diverges, and if $`(D-k)/d`$ diverges, $`(T-y)/d=h/b`$ diverges. At an angular node with $`k/d\to\infty`$, $`\mu\ge1/10`$ eventually. The lower bound $`na/d\ge\min(\mu,d-\mu)/(2b)`$ tends to infinity if the corank divided by $`b`$ diverges, or if the corank divided by $`b`$ stays bounded and the upper quota distance divided by $`d`$ diverges. These are precisely the unbounded quota-distance cases that use the angular estimate. [Phase-location inequality](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/quadratic/proof.md)
- I checked the required cancellation in the transverse logarithm. The conditional fourth moment contributes $`3(s)_2(d-s)_2/(d)_4`$ to the $`Q_2^2`$ coefficient. The size mixing and log-square subtraction leave $`O(a/d)`$, by the $`O(1/d)`$ Lipschitz bound on $`a_s`$ and $`V\le da`$. Since $`Q_2^2/d\le Q_4`$, the fourth-order contribution is controlled by a coordinate fourth moment. For the absolute fifth remainder, the finite population has mean zero and the conditional sample has size $`m=\min(s,d-s)`$ after complementation. Hoeffding's comparison applies to the continuous convex function $`|x|^5`$. The proof using independent draws and the equality pattern also gives the required convex comparison. The independent-draw fifth-moment estimate and logarithmic remainder bound give a coordinate term and a radial term $`Ca^{5/2}Q_2^{5/2}`$. With Gaussian precision comparable to $`na`$, the radial exponent is $`O(U^{5/2}/n^{3/2})`$ for $`U`$ chi-square with $`d-1`$ degrees of freedom. Its central region costs $`O(d^{5/2}/n^{3/2})=o(1)`$, and the fixed-radius chart bounds its remaining exponent by a small multiple of $`U`$. The Gaussian exponential moment absorbs that tail. The cubic uses the zero-sum covariance cancellation. These bounds give the stated $`L^2`$ error even when many diagonal phases matter. The separate formula for the signed fifth cumulant is compatible with this argument, which uses the absolute fifth moment. [Hoeffding, Theorem 4](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)
- The integrated off-chart bound has exponent $`O(d)-cna+O(nV/d^2)`$ after normalization. It tends to minus infinity because $`na/d\to\infty`$ and $`V/d^2\le a/d`$. A constant gap on the saturated chord region and on the far charts in the interior absorbs the larger Gaussian normalization cost, since $`n\gg d\log n`$. When the scaled diagonal variance diverges, the nonprincipal odd labels are summably negligible. When it stays bounded, the retained labels have phase $`\ell k\pi+o(1)`$ for odd $`\ell`$, after subtracting the linear terms in both the radial and determinant arguments. The cumulative retained amplitude has order at least $`1+d/\sqrt{nV}`$ on the principal scale. The discarded near-label sum has the corresponding bound times $`O(e^{-cM^2})`$ for the retention width $`M`$. The analogous near-full estimates use $`h`$ in place of $`V`$ and the deficit atom at zero. At an internal node the expansion gives $`nh\gtrsim d`$, which makes the retained-phase error vanish even if $`h`$ tends to zero. These estimates cover both integral and nonintegral $`T`$.
- Where $`k/d\to0`$, orientation curvature diverges and the alternating tails are bounded by their first magnitudes. Where the fixed-offset log curvatures tend to $`1/v>0`$, the normalized terms converge to $`e^{-j^2/(2v)}`$ for each fixed index offset $`j`$. The outward ratios decrease by global log-concavity. At any fixed cutoff these ratios give a geometric or alternating-tail bound, and the cutoff can then tend to infinity. Poisson summation gives the strict positivity of the limiting alternating Gaussian and covers radii changed by a multiplicative $`e^{o(1)}`$. The fixed near-edge distances needed for these limits diverge in the positive-curvature regimes by the deficit variance bound $`V\le h(1+h)`$. [Orientation inequality](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/orientation-normalization.md)
- The lower join uses the matching $`y(v_k)=k+1/2+o(1)`$ and the increment $`d/(2k)+o(1)`$ in the centered geometric log radius. The complementary join has the opposite phase shift, $`y(v_k)=k-1/2+o(1)`$, and the log-radius increment $`-d/(2(n-k))+o(1)`$. The geometric gaps at the two joins have limits $`1`$ and $`1/(1+\gamma)`$, where $`\gamma=\lim (d-r)/b`$, because the gap is the mean of the adjacent log curvatures. The replacement errors are $`o(1)`$. At the very upper endpoint of the bounded-corank band only the geometric radii are required. The strictly ordered sign changes, including the constant and leading coefficients, produce $`D`$ distinct negative zeros and exhaust the degree.

## Endpoint coefficients and their uniformity

- In each of the three sparse-margin applications, the entries counted (occupied cells or holes) have total $`M\asymp d^2`$ and equal column margins of order $`d`$. The row maximum is bounded by $`O(b)`$ in the bounded-rank ratio, or clipped at $`L=O(b+\log n)`$ in the other two applications. Then the maximum-margin product is positive and is $`O(dL)=o(M^{2/3})`$. The standardized column variance is zero, and the standardized row variance is at most $`O(L)=o(M^{1/3})`$ by bounding the row second moment by $`LM`$. The published hypotheses of Corollary 5.2 therefore hold uniformly, and the error $`O(dL/M^{2/3})`$ is $`o(1)`$. The sign and factor in its correction are $`-1/2+V_{\mathbf s}/(2m(1-p))`$, with $`m`$ the mean row margin and $`p=m/d`$. All of these clipped profiles are feasible by the cyclic fill with the prescribed row sizes. [Greenhill–McKay–Wang, Corollary 5.2](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~csg/papers/irregular-final.pdf#page=33) · [Version of record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2005.03.005)
- The single-row switch bounds for the actual arrays operate within the fixed-column-margin sample. In the occupied calculation the missing receivers are bounded by $`i+M/r`$ out of $`n`$, which is $`o(n)`$ in the range used. In the hole calculation each of the $`s(n-\ell)`$ moves from a row above the floor is allowed. The reverse counts give summable tails in both cases. Thus actual large-row profiles are already negligible before using the margin formula. Independent scalar row laws, in turn, have negligible large-row tails even after conditioning on their integer mean total. Jensen applied to the piecewise-linear concave interpolation of the log mass gives conditioning cost at most the support length, which is at most $`nd+1`$. This application requires interval support, which holds for both row laws and their convolutions.
- In the clipped cases, the independent summands to which Hoeffding's concentration inequality is applied are bounded by $`O(L^2)`$. With tolerance $`d^{-1/10}m`$, the exponent $`n d^{-1/5}m^2/L^4`$ dominates $`L+\log(nd)`$, both for $`b\ge\log n`$ and for $`b\le\log n`$. That control survives the factor $`e^{O(L)}`$ from the margin correction and conditioning on the total. In the bounded-rank ratio case the rescaled summands have range $`O(b)`$ and uniformly bounded mean. The chord estimate for their exponential moment and Cauchy–Schwarz justify weighting the concentration estimate there. No independence of the conditioned variables is needed.
- The scalar laws have log-concave masses and interval support. All applications of the integer-mean local limit are on variances bounded away from zero, after the proof's case distinctions. Exact saturation is represented by a deterministic law. The scalar step bound applies at internal quotas even when a neighboring quota is the saturated endpoint. If the normalized variance tends to zero with deficit bounded away from zero, the modal-atom estimate bounds the variance below while $`-E''=b/V`$ diverges. If the deficit tends to zero, $`e=nh`$ is an integer at an internal quota and both $`e-d`$ and $`e+d`$ belong to the scalar support. The negative-binomial step bound gives curvature of order at least $`b/(h+b/d)`$, which diverges. The variance terms vanish uniformly at these three quotas, including the saturated neighbor. The same scalar proof applies to the reversed occupied binomial weights and to the shifted hole weights since both have positive log-concave coefficients on their support. [Large-variance lattice estimate](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/ba7ddfd7efb37cac33769f4fdfed749e1b5bf58a/topics/balanced-arrays/dense-regime/uniform-lattice-lemma.md) · [Small-variance lattice estimate](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/ba7ddfd7efb37cac33769f4fdfed749e1b5bf58a/topics/balanced-arrays/dense-regime/rank-dependent/small-variance.md) · [Scalar step inequality](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/8bf2220688ce5fb1e4ac745daa655bb3c3813fbf/topics/balanced-arrays/deficit-curvature/scalar.md)

## Rank and corank consequences

- Outside the height range, $`b>C^{-6/5}n^{1/5}`$. For a sufficiently small absolute $`c`$, the condition $`r\le c n^{1/5}`$ then implies $`d^2\ge nr\log 4`$. The orientation criterion is strict for every internal quota, and degree zero and one are immediate. It also gives the independent $`d>n`$ branch. [Orientation criterion](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/orientation-normalization.md)
- In the remaining small-corank branch, set $`j_0=\lfloor d/10\rfloor`$ and $`i_0=n-j_0`$. The corank bound gives $`n-D\le d/1000+1`$, so $`i_0`$ is internal for large $`d`$. On a circle around the $`i`$th unrestricted zero, $`j=n-i\ge j_0`$. The factorization and log root spacing give $`|\mathbb EL-j|\le1+2\operatorname{Var}(L)`$ for the reflected quota $`L=n-K`$. Applying the mode estimate to $`L`$ bounds both its variance and this bias by $`O(1+j/d)`$. The two tail bounds give $`\Pr(L<j/2)\le e^{-\Omega(j)}`$ and conditional probability of a violating row at most $`e^{-\Omega(bj/d)}`$ when $`L\ge j/2`$, since the row law is binomial and its mean is at least $`bj/(2d)`$. After the union bound the error is smaller than the lower root-circle modulus for $`b`$ a sufficiently large multiple of $`\log(n+1)`$, uniformly for $`j/d\ge0.09`$. Strict Rouché transfers exactly one zero to each disjoint conjugation-invariant disk in the left half-plane. [Unrestricted root-circle estimate](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/dense-regime/below-cap/full-rank-root-stability.md) · [Local mode estimate](https://github.com/ProgramComputer/open-math-research/blob/4fb624ba937a8eecc1361a6066185cd81ab7b172/topics/balanced-arrays/dense-regime/below-cap/proof.md)
- The join is strict. At the unrestricted geometric center of index $`i_0`$, log curvature exceeds $`6`$. On the lower boundary of the logarithmic annulus of half-width $`0.1`$, the $`i_0`$th monomial strictly dominates. Rouché puts exactly $`i_0`$ unrestricted roots below that boundary. The two neighboring capped coefficients needed for the capped geometric center are within relative $`O((n+1)^{-2})`$ of the unrestricted coefficients by fixed-quota binomial acceptance. Consequently all transferred disks are strictly below the first capped geometric radius. The remaining geometric radii have log curvature above $`6`$ by the orientation bound, and their signs through the leading coefficient place $`D-i_0`$ distinct negative roots outside the disks. The thresholds $`b\ge C_*\log(n+1)`$ and $`d-r\le b/1000`$ hold in the claimed corank range for sufficiently large $`n`$ after reducing the absolute $`c`$.
