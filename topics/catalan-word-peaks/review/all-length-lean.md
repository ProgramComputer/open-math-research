## Original claim and Lean interpretation

I checked Alexandersson's conjecture in the primary SymCat source and the interleaving convention it links to. The conjecture quantifies over the polynomial formed by weighting each Catalan word of length $n$ by $t$ to the number of strict internal peaks. It asks for real-rootedness and directed consecutive interleaving, $C_n\mathrel{\preceq} C_{n+1}$, allowing weak inequalities. [Conjecture](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjCatalanWordPeaks) · [Interleaving convention](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedInterlacing.htm#interlacing)

- In `CatalanStrictPeaks.lean`, `Word n` is a function from the $n$ labeled positions to the alphabet $0,\ldots,n$. This enumeration is in bijection with the defining natural-valued words. Induction from the initial zero and the upper bound on each successive letter gives $w_i\le i$ at each zero-based index, so conversion to the finite alphabet is always possible and unique. Conversely, taking the values of the bounded letters preserves the original inequalities, and nonnegativity comes from the codomain. The extra top letter $n$ cannot actually occur for positive length. The finite set has one copy of each function and no duplications.
- `peaks` counts starts $j$ of triples with $j+2<n$. The corresponding middle index is $j+1$ in zero-based notation and $j+2$ in the source's one-based notation. Both comparisons are strictly below the middle letter. This excludes ties, plateaus and endpoints. The fallback value outside the word is unused on every counted triple. Thus `C n` is the source's defining word sum over real coefficients. The source's coefficient formula and recurrence are alternative representations of the same polynomial.
- At $n=0$ there is one empty word, no peaks and the constant $1$, consistent with the generating-function convention. At $n=1,2$ the peak statistic still vanishes and the polynomials are $1,2$. The constant pairs have no root inequalities, and the pair from length two to three has just one new root and no old root, so it satisfies the degree-increase clause. I independently generated the words by extending each admissible prefix with every allowed next letter at lengths $0$ through $8$ and matched the archived coefficient vectors. In particular $C_3=4+t$ and $C_4=9+5t$, which exhibit the required orientation in the first pair with two nonconstant rows.
- In `OrientedNegativeInterlace.lean`, `CompleteNegativeRoots` specifies a positive strictly increasing list of moduli of length exactly the natural degree and equality of the real-root multiset to the multiset of their distinct negatives. For the nonzero polynomials `C n`, the multiset has degree many simple real roots. Their product of linear factors, times the leading coefficient, exhausts the polynomial, so there are no nonreal roots. Positivity of the leading coefficient is provided by `leadingCoeff_pos`. The multiset equality matters: mere equality of a set of real roots could leave multiplicities ambiguous.
- If the old and new positive moduli are $a_i,b_i$ starting at index zero, `Oriented` says $b_i<a_i$ and $a_i<b_{i+1}$ whenever those indices exist. In equal degree $m$ the increasing *negative* root order is $-a_{m-1}<-b_{m-1}<\cdots<-a_0<-b_0$. The old row alternates to the left of the new row as required. In the degree-increase case the chain starts with $-b_m<-a_{m-1}$ and ends with $-a_0<-b_0$, so a new root occurs on each outside edge. This is the strict form of the source's other clause. The degree assertions in `CatalanStrictPeaksInterlacing.lean` cover the odd and positive even old lengths, and the initial length-zero pair is equal of degree zero.
