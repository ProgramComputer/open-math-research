# Direct tile review of the pending-state argument

The checker [independent-tiles.py](independent-tiles.py) enumerates individual chronological tile histories. It does not import or evaluate the layer recurrence in the main checker. The two short level tiles are distinct. One rises then falls through its two half steps. The other stays level. Short tiles have duration one, and long tiles place one mark and have duration two. All half-step heights are required to be nonnegative. At the boundary the first half step never decreases height, so testing a tile's final height suffices.

A pending state counts an open tile before its final half step, once per possible opening. An ordinary opening may stay or rise and uses no additional chronological time. A marked opening stays at the same height and uses one additional unit. Either can then close by staying or decreasing height, using one final unit of time. Direct enumeration matches the complete layers at times one, two, and three, and the pending layers at times one and two.

All nonconstant genuine input coordinates in the four layers used for the backward-factorization example are linear. Their negative roots in increasing order are $-7,-5,-9/2,-14/5,-2,-5/3$. In the perturbation with both backward factorizations preserved, the only moved nonzero root changes from $-7$ to $-109/2$. In the perturbation with the latest update preserved, the changed root moves from $-7$ to $-113/2$. In each relevant group of layers it remains the leftmost root. Orders after inserting any fixed multiplicities at zero therefore also agree with the corresponding genuine inputs.

The direct closure count gives two short transitions from height zero to zero and one from height one to zero. A marked long tile gives one transition to zero from either height. Applying these closure counts to the three perturbed inputs reconstructs the discriminants $-12444$, $-107$, and $-123$. The joint-consistency calculation in the note uses both layer identities and only applies to the specified low-degree pattern.

This agent-assisted review uses a separate enumeration implementation and exact arithmetic. It verifies the two genuine pending layers and the local perturbation computations. It is not a new proof or disproof of the established all-length theorem and it does not treat higher-degree layers subject to both consistency conditions.
