# Ferroni–McGinnis’s prism-slice conjecture

Source statement recorded by an independent agent on 2026-11-25 before reading the project’s proof or Lean statements.

Let $n\geq1$, $c\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^n$, $k$ an integer, and $\mathcal R_{k,c}=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:0\leq x_i\leq c_i,\ \sum_i x_i=k\}$. For a nonempty slice write $E_{k,c}(m)=\#(m\mathcal R_{k,c}\cap\mathbb Z^n)$ and $H_{k,c}(t)=(1-t)^{\dim(\mathcal R_{k,c})+1}\sum_{m\geq0}E_{k,c}(m)t^m$. For $0<k<\sum_i c_i$, the dimension is $n-1$, and $E_{k,c}(m)$ counts nonnegative integer tuples bounded by $mc_i$ with sum $mk$. If $k=\sum_i c_i$, the point slice has $H=1$ irrespective of splitting and contributes no counterexample. The height $k=0$ also yields a point slice. Heights above the sum yield an empty slice, outside the usual definition for nonempty lattice polytopes. [Ferroni and McGinnis, Definition (1.1), Theorem 2.5, and §5.3](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X24000233).

In arXiv v1, Conjecture 5.12 requires $c_n\geq2$ and says that $H_{k,c}$ and $H_{k,c'}$ both have real roots and their root lists interlace, where $c'=(c_1,\dots,c_{n-1},c_n-1,1)$. The printed v1 sentence leaves $k$ implicit as the slice height. The version of record states unconditional real-rootedness for all positive $c$ and positive slice heights, together with the same splitting interlacing when $c_n\geq2$. In particular, the published real-rootedness clause expressly covers all-one capacities, including the hypersimplices. A failed splitting pair refutes the splitting clause in both versions. The general real-rootedness clause is a separate claim. [arXiv v1, §5.3, p. 19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11808v1#page=19), [arXiv v2, §5.3, PDF p. 20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11808v2#page=20), [version of record, §5.3, Conjecture 5.12, p. 1034](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/BD4DAAE19B3FBECB840B658A189198ED/S0008414X24000233a.pdf/lattice_points_in_slices_of_prisms.pdf#page=22).

Ferroni and McGinnis state that the roots interlace without giving inequalities in §5.3. An orientation-independent failure of weak alternation of the two complete sorted root lists is enough. For equal lengths either list may start and, for lengths differing by one, the longer list must occupy the two ends. Weak inequalities allow coincidences and multiplicities. Constants of positive leading coefficient have empty root lists. A counterexample with roots real for both polynomials isolates the interlacing clause.
