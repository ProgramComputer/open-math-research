- **`original_middle_phase_slope`.** For $w=x+i\pi$, $r=|w|$ and $s=\pi/r$, write $C=w^k\sum_p(x+ip\pi)^{-k}$ over odd $p$. Differentiating the finite log tilt along $w$ leaves $g_1-kg_k w^{k-1}=g_1+C/w$. The sign and factorial here match archived `moment_poles`, including $x=0$, and $g_1$ is real. Since $\mathrm{Im}(1/w)=\theta'(x)=-\pi/r^2$, the nearest-pole contribution cancels the extra one in $(n+1)\theta'(x)$. The remaining bound $|C-1|\le1+8/(15s)$ counts the conjugate pole once and both far half-lattices. With $ns^2\ge1$, the strict constant uses $1/\sqrt7<8/21$ and $8/21+8/15=32/35$. The declaration measures the derivative in $x$ and therefore includes the scale $\pi/r^2$ on both sides. The angular bound in the README follows by the inverse derivative, since $dx/d\theta=-r^2/\pi$.

- **`original_middle_phase_strictAnti`.** The formal conclusion is strict decrease on `Icc 0 b` under $b^2+\pi^2\le n\pi^2$. The full nonempty interval is obtained by taking $b=\pi\sqrt{n-1}$. Continuity covers the closed endpoints and the derivative is strictly negative on the interior. Negative $b$ gives an empty interval. In angular order this interval is $[\arcsin(1/\sqrt n),\pi/2]$, with the identity $x=\pi\cot\theta$ reversing the order. The narrow formal claim exported here uses $x$. The angular equivalent in the text is a coordinate translation.

- **`original_high_angle_cells`.** This theorem requires $k\ge7$, $n>0$, $n\ge100k\log k$, and both cell endpoints in $[\pi/6,\pi/2]$. Those conditions give $x\ge0$, $r\le2\pi$, and a positive old-row normalizer. At $\theta=\pi/2$ the real Eulerian coefficients at odd powers beyond one vanish and the linear term gives imaginary part $\pi/2$. The principal real and imaginary readouts are $\cos\Psi_n$ and $-\sin\Psi_n$. Thus the literal old-row endpoint signs are $(-1)^j$ and $(-1)^{j+1}$.
