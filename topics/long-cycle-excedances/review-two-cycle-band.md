# Independent review of the two-cycle interlacing argument

This agent-assisted review covers the written front and middle estimates and the exact arithmetic needed to join them. I verified the analytic argument for old row sizes $\max(192,2k)\le n\le3k-2$. The threshold $192$ is a bound in the proof and permits an infinite range beginning at $k=65$.

Alexandersson's conjecture requires real-rootedness for every $E_{n,k}$ with $n\ge k\ge3$ and consecutive interlacing at each fixed $k$. The two-cycle result gives real-rootedness through row $3k-1$ and consecutive interlacing through the pair $(3k-2,3k-1)$. The roots are simple and negative after dividing by $t$. Restoring the common root at zero gives the weak interlacing in the source convention. Pairs beginning at $n=3k-1$ belong to the remaining conjecture. ## Analytic checks

- **Normalization and residual cycle.** A cycle of length $m$ contributes $tA_{m-1}$. Dividing by $m!$ and applying the Eulerian pole formula gives $B_m/m$ in the stated units. The factor $1/2$ in the two-cycle sum is needed even at $j=n-j$, where exchanging the two underlying label sets still gives a second ordering. This gives $w_j=n/(2j(n-j))$ and $W=\sum_{j=k}^{n-k}1/j$. As a check against the original definition, I enumerated permutations directly for the 21 admissible $(n,k)$ through $n=8$ and independently reconstructed their coefficients by distinguishing the cycle containing label one. All rows agreed. In the insertion term $m=n+1-k$ lies below $2k$, so it contributes a single cycle. I recovered the multiplier in the derivative comparison from $t\,d\theta/dt=\pi/r^2$ and the coefficient of the normalized lag from $\binom n{k-1}(k-1)!(m-1)!/n!=1/m$.
- **Small roots.** The upper and lower bounds from increasing runs use injections in opposite directions with distinct domains. The binomial bound for two-cycle coefficients follows by dividing $s^{j-1}(p-s)^{n-j-1}$ by $p^{n-1}$ and restoring the two missing factors as $p/(s(p-s))$. The Hoeffding split gives $\alpha_p\le3$ globally and the single-cycle term gives $\alpha_p\ge99/100$ for the needed small ranks. On the circle around $-1$ and the two origin-centered circles the tail is strictly below the minimum modulus of $1+z$. Multiplication by $z^{h-1}$ gives a polynomial with the same zeros away from zero and makes the three Rouché counts apply. They give one simple real root of rank $h$. For the derivative, subtracting $F$ from $zF'$ makes the two central terms exactly $-1$ and $0$. The tail estimate is below $1/3$. The binomial point mass for $tL$ uses $M=n-1$ and $a=(k-1)/M$, which reaches $1/3$ at $n=3k-2$. Stirling's bounds and the Gaussian sum give the stated $777/(80\sqrt M)$ bound. I independently checked the numerical comparisons for the coefficient, Rouché, derivative, Stirling, Gaussian, and location estimates using rational inequalities, with truncated exponential series for the elementary exponential bounds.
- **Middle roots.** The positive odd pole tail beyond nine is bounded by half the integral of the decreasing weighted majorant. The principal terms of the product in each mesh interval give one $\cos(n\theta)$ and one $\cos((2j-n)\theta)$. In the mixed derivative bound, the two fractional cycle lengths are bounded above by $v$ and $1-u$. The exponents in the error bounds use their lower bounds $u$ and $1-v$. After the common normalization and division by $2\sin\theta$, the derivative has magnitude $|f'|/n$ and the lag is bounded by $J_i$. I checked the provided Python checker against the displayed formulas and ran it. I also reimplemented the 16 rational comparisons independently. The largest normalized squared bound is about $0.98893534$, attained at the upper mass endpoint of the interval beginning at $q=1/3$. The quadratic difference in (1) is convex because its quadratic coefficient is $A^2+B^2-1\ge0$. The endpoints therefore suffice. At a zero of $f$, (1) bounds the phase derivative strictly away from the lag.
- **Join and signs.** The sample at $(H+1)\pi/n$ already satisfies the middle cutoff, and the front roots precede it strictly in increasing modulus. After reflection the samples run through indices $H+1,\ldots,n-H-1$, with $n-2H-2$ gaps. For odd $n$ the central gap is between a reciprocal pair of samples and for even $n$ the central point itself is a sample. Together with $2H$ outside roots the count equals $\deg P_n=n-2$. At an old root the derivative term and the lag both transform under $x\mapsto 1/x$ by the common factor $x^{1-n}$. One can verify this by differentiating the reciprocal identity for $P_n$ and using the reciprocal Eulerian factors of $L$. The constant and leading coefficients of both next-row polynomials are one. Their alternating values at the old roots therefore certify one simple root in every gap and on each end.

## Finite cases and coverage

Ordinary Eulerian interlacing handles $k\le n\le2k-2$. The separately reviewed first-transition argument handles $n=2k-1$ for all $k\ge3$. The front estimate applies already for $n\ge100$ to the specified small roots. The middle estimate is what sets the analytical cutoff for complete pairs at $n\ge192$.

The remaining finite obligation consists of exactly 3,007 pairs, with $3\le k\le95$ and $2k\le n\le\min(191,3k-2)$. There are 379,502 old roots across those pairs, including reciprocal partners. I inspected the finite verifier. It reconstructs each actual row by distinguishing a cycle and checks the insertion identity separately. Its scaled Horner sign tests use integer arithmetic and a rounding error bounded by the degree in the scaled units. It verifies both signed polynomials at both ends of disjoint brackets around the old roots. Adjacent brackets give opposite signs on each intervening gap. Reciprocity, the central check at $-1$, and the known degrees exhaust the roots of both next-row polynomials in the gaps. This implies that their signs stay fixed through each old bracket and hence prove $|L|<|D|$ at the old root. A separate starting Eulerian computation supplies the brackets for each $k$. The default bounds iterate exactly the required pairs and use the earlier boundary pair to start each two-cycle run. I checked the separate full-run log, which reports the expected 3,007 pairs and 379,502 roots. I independently reconstructed the coefficient vectors and evaluated the endpoint signs using homogeneous integer Horner arithmetic for all nine pairs in the full-data smoke archive, covering old sizes from 6 to 12. All nine passed. The complete finite run was executed separately from this review.

I found no substantive objection to the analytic argument or the finite verifier's certificate logic. The sentence that the front estimate “covers this entire band” for $k\ge50$ would be clearer if it said that it covers the specified small roots in every row of that band. Formal status is ⬜ None.
