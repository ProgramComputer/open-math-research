# Independent review of long-cycle excedance interlacing

I checked the integrated topic proof, including the precursor small-rank note and its incorporated version. The argument establishes all-row strict interlacing of nonzero roots at minimum length $k=3$. At every fixed $k\ge3$ it establishes the same result from some row index onward. Simplicity of zero follows from $[t]E_{n,k}=1$ at every $n\ge k$. I found no mathematical gap. The proof leaves arbitrary finite rows at $k\ge4$ open, with no common threshold for all $k$ asserted.

Alexandersson's original conjecture asks for real-rootedness and consecutive interlacing for **each** $k\ge3$ and every $n\ge k$. The partial result uses that same excedance statistic and keeps $k$ fixed when comparing rows. The common simple zero at zero is allowed in the original statement. The ordinary-descent conjecture is separate. The author's original EGF, maximum-insertion recurrence, rows $3$ through $8$ at $k=3$, and known derangement boundary at $k=2$ agree with the proof. The external exact-family note gives the recurrence, a gamma reduction, finite evidence and an obstruction to a uniform lagged-term induction. It claims no proof of the statements verified here. ## Complex coefficient estimate

- Removing singleton cycles from the ordinary Eulerian EGF leaves the linear term $aw$ with $a=-t/(1-t)$. At $k=3$, deleting the transposition term gives $+(a(1-a)/2)w^2$. The pole at $s+ip\pi$ has residue $-T_s(s+ip\pi)$. Deforming outward therefore gives the stated positive residue contribution, with exponent $n+1$ and the factor of two from the conjugate pair.
- The circle estimates apply uniformly even where the ceiling in $v$ jumps. For $s\le4\pi$ use $v\ge2\pi$, and for $s\ge4\pi$ use $v\ge s/2$, to get $(r/R)^2\le17/20$. Subtracting the two adjacent radii and rationalizing shows distances at least $v\pi/(2R+\pi)$ in both directions. Since $R\le\sqrt5 v$ and $v\ge2\pi$, this exceeds $1/2$. In the denominator estimate, if $|\Re w-s|<1/4$ the imaginary distance to a pole height is at least $\sqrt3/4>2/5$. The sine bound gives the required $1/8$ lower bound. Otherwise the elementary modulus gap $1-e^{-1/4}>1/5$ suffices.
- The numerator lower bound is $e^{-\pi^2/8}>1/4$, since $as+b(s^2-\pi^2)/2\ge-\pi^2/8$. The upper bound on $|w|\le R$ follows by expanding $(3s/2+2\pi)^2$ and maximizing $s e^{-s}$ and $s^2 e^{-s}$ separately. Using $e>13/5$ and $\pi<22/7$ gives a bound strictly below 14. The exact rational inequality $16\cdot3^{14}(22/7)400(17/20)^{200}<1/100$ verifies the cutoff. Its bound decreases thereafter. Dividing the circle error by $S$ uses $r/S=\pi/S^2\le\pi n$.
- For the nonprincipal poles, $p(1+(p^2-1)/100)^{-50}$ decreases for $p\ge3$. Its first term and half the integral from three yield exactly the displayed $3(27/25)^{-50}+(25/49)(27/25)^{-49}<1/10$. At $s\ge10$ the exponent's absolute bound decreases and is already below $1/20$ at ten. At $s\le10$, $S^2>2/25$ follows from $\pi>3$. The half-exponent estimate and remaining factor $(25/41)^{n/4}$ give a strict margin below $1/5$ after multiplying by $4e^{14}$. I checked these arithmetic margins independently with rational bounds.
- Subtracting $\cos\theta$ times the old normalized coefficient introduces the factor $z_p-\cos\theta$ in the same contour expansion. Its exact modulus is at most $pS$, including at $\theta=\pi/2$. The main difference is $-S\sin\Psi_n$, and the other pole errors sum to less than $S/5$. The two contour errors cost at most $S/50$. This proves the stated $S/4$ relative bound. At any fixed $k$ the finite numerator polynomial is uniformly small for large $s$ on the chosen circle and bounded on compact $s$-intervals. This yields the same asymptotic strict bounds with a threshold depending on $k$.

## Small ranks and the interface

- The run-color normalization is exact. Each accepted permutation has $p!$ labelings, and there are $p!$ possible permutations of the uniformly sampled run labels, canceling to $a_{n,p}/p^n$. Every color occurring on both halves makes each run boundary ascend, and each color occurring at least $k$ times guarantees the cycle-length constraint for every run permutation.
- For $p\le(3/5)\sqrt n$ and $n\ge\max(400,25k^2)$, the whole-word mean is greater than $8k$. The Chernoff exponent $49\mu/128$ follows with deficit $7/8$. The short-half mean is at least $(4/5)\sqrt n$, and $245/384>3/5$ controls the full-word exponent. The union bound $\epsilon_n=(9/5)\sqrt n e^{-3\sqrt n/5}$ has $\sqrt n\epsilon_n<1/100$, as $720/(13/5)^{12}<1/100$ and the bound decreases from row 400. It also controls the specified coefficients in row $n+1$.
- For $h\le(3/8)\sqrt n$, $h+1\le(17/40)\sqrt n$, so the first omitted ratios on either side are bounded by $q=e^{-5}<1/100$. The left tail on the annulus is at most $2q/(1-2q)$ and the right at most $9q/(1-3q)$. On the small offset circle their sum is at most $6q/(1-2q)$. The displayed bounds certify both annular zero counts and one simple negative root in the offset disk. The absolute ranks are distinct because each such root has exactly its prescribed number of smaller-modulus roots. This also shows the smaller roots used in the last small-rank sign discussion are negative and simple.
- In $E_{n+1}-hE_n$ the normalized coefficient at $p$ is $(p-h)\alpha_{n,p}+p(\alpha_{n+1,p}-\alpha_{n,p})$. The $p=h+1$ term of the first summand is negative and below $-(7/8)(99/100)$. The other contributions cost less than $11/100$, $3/125$, and $1/1000$ as stated. For the last tail, $p-h>4.5$ and $p<3(p-h)$, so the right-tail exponent $\ell=p-h-1$ begins at four. Substitution of $s=1/50$ in the rational geometric sums verifies each bound. The sign after restoring the factor is $(-1)^{h+1}$ for $E_{n+1}$ and $(-1)^h$ for its quotient by $t$.
- The phase derivative at $k=3$ follows from $a_s=-b$ and $b_s=-b(1-2a)$. Its bound $27/e^3+\pi^2/e<6$ proves monotonicity. At any fixed $k$, the correction and its derivative decay as $s\to\infty$, apart from bounded factors polynomial in $s$. At $s=0$ the reciprocal odd-degree Eulerian polynomials vanish at $-1$, so the phase has the claimed endpoints. The first checkpoint lies in $nS^2\ge1$ by $3\pi/8>1$ and the displayed small-angle bound. The explicit $k=3$ cutoff follows from $(9/8)(400/401)-(3(22/7)/8)^3/2400>1$. The rank-$H$ root precedes it by the logarithmic separator, whose lower bound exceeds $800/289-1/8>\log(9/8)$.
- The prescribed checkpoints give $n-2H-2$ bounded intervals. Small ranks and their reciprocal partners add $2H$, giving exactly $n-2$ roots, the degree of $E_n/t$. Hence each root is simple and the interval $j=h$ has absolute rank $h$. For odd $n$, reciprocity makes the central root $-1$ and the phase there is $n\pi/2$. On every old middle root in the lower half, the error bounds force $-\mathrm{sgn}\,\sin\Psi_n=(-1)^{h+1}$ for $E_{n+1}$. On reflection $h$ becomes $n-1-h$, and the parity change in $t^{-(n+1)}$ gives the same rank formula. Dividing by $t$ reverses the signs. Alternating signs with value one at zero and the correct leading sign at negative infinity give every strict consecutive inequality. The removed optional asymptotic interface in the scratch note is unnecessary for this effective join.
