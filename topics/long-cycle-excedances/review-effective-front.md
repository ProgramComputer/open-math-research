# Independent review of the effective front bound and the pole join

I independently checked the argument in [effective-front.md](effective-front.md) and its use with [separated-poles.md](separated-poles.md). I found no substantive gap. For every $n\ge10{,}000$ and $3\le k\le n$, the front proof gives the first $H=\lfloor7\sqrt n/20\rfloor$ simple negative nonzero roots, their reflected counterparts, the strict lag inequality at them, and the claimed consecutive ordering at the small-modulus end. The joined proof gives all-root strict interlacing after removing the shared zero for $n\ge10{,}000$ and

$$
k\ge 2000\sqrt n\bigl(3+\log(3\sqrt{n/k})\bigr).
$$

For $k\le(n+3)/3$, the separated-pole argument supplies the middle comparison. For $k>(n+3)/3$, the reviewed [one- and two-cycle theorem](two-cycle-band.md) applies independently. This is an agent-assisted review of a partial theorem, with scalar checks implemented in Python and no checked Lean or Rocq proof.

Alexandersson's 2026 conjecture in the section “Permutations with long cycles” concerns the univariate excedance polynomials $E_{n,k}$ at every fixed $k\ge3$ and every $n\ge k$. Dividing out the simple common zero gives the polynomials $P_n$ in the review. Strict interlacing for the $P_n$ proves the source's consecutive interlacing conclusion for $E_{n,k}$ on the indicated range. [Primary statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjLongCycleExcedances)

## Probability representation and coefficient estimates

- A permutation with $p$ excedances cuts into $p$ maximal strictly decreasing runs at its upward edges. Recording each label's run and the succession permutation on the runs gives exactly $p!$ encodings of that permutation. Conversely, a word with every color occupied specifies one decreasing run per color, and the run permutation joins their ends. Requiring each run boundary to be upward makes the cut recoverable. The length of each resulting label cycle is the number of labels on its run cycle. The sample space has $p^N p!$ equally likely pairs. Thus the coefficient of $t^p$, divided by $p^N$, is the probability of the simultaneous nonemptiness, boundary and size tests. The size-only probability $f$ allows empty colors on a run cycle with enough labels and rejects a run cycle with fewer than $k$ labels. The event that every color occurs in both halves guarantees nonemptiness and each upward boundary, and each half has at least $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ labels. Taking the union over both halves and all colors proves (1), including odd rows. The counting also gives $[t]E_{n,k}=1$.
- For each lower cutoff $b$, the recurrence $p d(p,b)=\sum_{u=b}^p d(p-u,b)$, with $d(0,b)=1$, gives $1/(2b-1)\le d(p,b)\le1/b$ for $p\ge b$. For the lower acceptance envelope, $b=\lceil4kp/(3n)\rceil\le p$ if $k\le n/2$. A run cycle of size $u\ge b$ has binomial mean $nu/p\ge4k/3$, so the lower-tail Chernoff bound is $e^{-nu/(32p)}$. For $u<p$ the marked-cycle probability with admissible remainder is at most $1/(bu)$, including the zero case $0<p-u<b$. The run cycle of size $p$ receives all labels. The relative error from discarding the boundary test is at most $4p^2 e^{-(n-1)/(2p)}\le n e^{-(n-1)/\sqrt n}$. If $k>n/2$, only one run cycle can pass the size test and $f=1/p$, with an even smaller relative boundary error. For the upper envelope, a run cycle of size $u<\lceil2kp/(3N)\rceil$ must exceed its binomial mean by a factor of at least $3/2$. Exponential Markov at $\log(3/2)$ bounds its success probability by $e^{-k(\log(3/2)-1/3)}<e^{-k/15}$. For $n/2\le N\le n$, $p\le J=\lfloor\sqrt n/2\rfloor$ gives $\mu=N/p\ge\sqrt n$. In the remaining comparison, $a'e^{-a'\mu/15}$ decreases for $a'\ge3/2$, and $(1+\log(x/2))e^{-x/10}$ decreases for $x\ge100$. These reduce the bounds in (2) to the rational checks below.
- In the lag convolution, $[t^j](t A_{k-1})\le j^{k-1}$ follows by encoding a permutation with $j-1$ descents by the run containing each label. After division by $p^n$, the marked component contributes the binomial point mass $b(j/p)$. Independently, size acceptance can change under one additional label only when that label hits an offending cycle with exactly $k-1$ labels. The expected number of marked cycles of $j$ colors is $1/j$, the hit probability is $j/p$, and, conditional on the count $k-1$, the $m=n+1-k$ labels outside it still have independent uniform colors among the $p-j$ remaining colors. The remaining run permutation is uniform. The cycle with all colors already passes. These verify (3), including the vanishing lag for $m<k$ and the case $p=1$.
- In the small-$k$ case of (4), $n/p\ge2\sqrt n$ gives the geometric bound with first term at most $e^{-25/4}$. Otherwise $k-1\ge150$. For $j/p\le(k-1)/n+1/10$, the residual parameter is at least $4/5$ times the old parameter since $m/n\ge1/2$. The ratio upper bound is maximized at $a=151/80$. The samples farther away cost at most $n e^{-n/50}$ after normalizing, using $p/\alpha_{n,p}\le(100/99)(p+(8/3)p^2)<n$. The bound $D(v\|w)\ge2(v-w)^2$ follows from the second derivative in $v$ and equality with zero derivative at $v=w$. For the sum of all sampled binomial masses, omit the largest sample and compare each sample below the continuous mode with the adjacent interval to its right, and each sample above the mode with the adjacent interval to its left. If the mode lies strictly between samples, the central interval bounds the smaller of those two endpoint samples. This gives a maximum plus $p$ times the integral. The beta integral equals $1/(n+1)$, and Stirling gives the claimed peak bound. Combining (1), (3) and (4), the total error in (5) after multiplication by $p$ is at most $4p^2 e^{-(n-1)/(2p)}/\alpha_{n,p}\le2n^{3/2} e^{-(n-1)/\sqrt n}$, plus $8/25$ from the size test. The functions $n e^{-(n-1)/\sqrt n}$, $n^{3/2}e^{-(n-1)/\sqrt n}$, $n e^{-n/50}$ and $\sqrt n/(2(n+1))$ all decrease for $n\ge10{,}000$ by differentiation.

## Front root counts, signs, and consecutive order

For $h\le H$, the floor bounds give $h+1\le(9/25)\sqrt n<\sqrt n/2$, so both adaptive coefficients lie in the range of (2). The ratio from (2) has the stated maximum by maximizing at $a=151/100$ after extracting $\max(1,v/p)$. For $h\ge6$ the neighbor ratio is below $6$, and for the finite small ranks it is below $51/5$. The depressed consecutive bases are $1-1/h^2$ at the immediate left and $1-1/(h+1)^2$ at the immediate right. Farther out they only decrease in the respective directions. At $p>J$ the floor gives $p>\sqrt n/2$ and therefore $\ell=p-h-1>(7/50)\sqrt n\ge14$. This proves each coefficient and index bound used for the far tail. At $h=1$ the left tail is empty.

Multiplication by $z^{h-1}$ makes the normalized Laurent polynomial a nonzero scalar multiple of $P_n(uz)$. Rouché therefore counts precisely one root in $|z+1|<1/8$, $h-1$ inside $|z|<1/2$, and $h$ inside $|z|<3$, with multiplicity. The disk around $-1$ lies in the annulus and is invariant under conjugation, so its unique root is negative and simple and has rank $h$. This proves the rank claim even before the remaining roots are localized. At the root the terms in $zF'-F$ have weight $p-h-1$. This gives central weights $-1,0$ and the two tail weights used in the derivative estimate. The normalized lag numerator is $xL(x)$, and $xP'_n(x)=E'_{n,k}(x)$ at a root. Thus the bound on $|xL(x)|$ proves the strict lag inequality, because $1-x>1$.

Let $D(t)=t(1-t)P'_n(t)$. On the negative axis in order of increasing modulus, each already localized old root reverses the sign of $P_n$ from its constant sign $+1$ at zero. At rank $h$, $D(x)$ consequently has sign $(-1)^h$. Both $D(x)+L(x)$ and $D(x)-L(x)$ have that sign. The induction-free comparison with the next row applies the same Rouché counts at size $n+1$, whose cutoff is at least $H$. Dividing the old modulus by the new adaptive radius gives the four bounds in the checker from (5), with the $p=1$ ratio exactly one. The resulting modulus is strictly inside the new annulus for rank $h$. On entering that annulus from smaller modulus the sign of $P_{n+1}$ is $(-1)^{h-1}$, and its only crossing there is the simple rank-$h$ root. The sign at the old root therefore gives the strict consecutive order stated in the proposal.

## Phase join and root exhaustion

The point $t(\theta)=-\exp(-\pi\cot\theta)$ increases in modulus on $0<\theta<\pi$. At $\theta_0=(H+1)\pi/n<\pi/2$, $\pi\cot\theta_0<n/(H+1)$. The inequality $-\log(1-y)-y\ge y^2/2$ with $y=1/(H+1)$ gives the logarithmic margin for all the front roots. For the sine margin, $H+1>(7/20)\sqrt n$ even when $(7/20)\sqrt n$ is an integer. Apply monotonicity of sine to $(7\pi/20)/\sqrt n<\theta_0$ and $\sin y\ge y-y^3/6$ to this lower angle. The checker proves $n\sin^2\theta_0>6/5$ by enclosing $\pi$ between $333/106$ and $22/7$.

The assumed threshold implies $kS\ge2000(3+\log(3\sqrt{n/k}))$ throughout $\theta_0\le\theta\le\pi-\theta_0$ because $S>1/\sqrt n$ there. It also forces $k\ge32$. In the separated-pole argument, each occupied term permits the simultaneous damping at the largest-exponent pole and the derivative damping. The denominator is exact, $|1-z_q/z_a|=|q-a|S\rho_q$. The near/far split uses spacing two on the odd lattice, with the near harmonic cutoff $\lceil2\sqrt{n/k}\rceil\le3\sqrt{n/k}$, and bounds the far part by at most $3/2$. For fixed distinguished poles the other poles are unordered, which accounts for the $(h-1)!$ and $(h-2)!$ denominators. The geometric remainder margins and single-pole tail are in the checker. The residual uses $m\ge 2n/3$ when $k\le(n+3)/3$, including the transition at $n=3k-1$. The insertion normalization multiplies both $D$ and $L$ by $t r^{n+1}/[n!(1-t)^{n+1}]$, giving $S F'_n$ and $B_kF_m$ respectively. This preserves the absolute comparison. At a middle zero $|\cos(n\theta)|<7/60$ and $|\sin(n\theta)|>19/20$ give $|F'_n|>n/(2k)$, whereas the lag is smaller than $nS/(2k)$.

Reciprocity gives $t(\pi-\theta)=1/t(\theta)$ and $E_{n,k}(1/t)=t^{-n}E_{n,k}(t)$, so the reflected sample at $l=n-j$ has sign $(-1)^{n+j}=(-1)^l$ when the left sample at $j$ has sign $(-1)^j$. If $n$ is even there is one sample at $l=n/2$ shared by the two half intervals. If $n$ is odd the two samples adjacent to $\pi/2$ have consecutive indices and opposite signs. The indices $H+1,\ldots,n-H-1$ give $n-2H-1$ samples and $n-2H-2$ disjoint gaps. All $H$ front roots and their $H$ reciprocal partners lie strictly outside these gaps. The $n-2$ roots so obtained exhaust the degree of $P_n$, proving simplicity. At reciprocal old roots, $D(1/x)=x^{1-n}D(x)$ and $L(1/x)=x^{1-n}L(x)$, so the strict lag comparison transfers. At each old root, the two next-row pencils $P_{n+1}$ and $P_{n+1}-2L$ therefore have sign $(-1)^h$ in order of increasing modulus. Both have constant and leading coefficient one because $L$ has zero constant coefficient and degree at most $n-2$. Together with signs near zero and at negative infinity this provides one root of each pencil in each of the $n-1$ intervals between and outside the old roots. Both pencils have degree $n-1$, establishing the claimed strict interlacing and simplicity.

### Optional extension of the pole comparison

For $m\ge k$ one always has $m\ge(n+1)/2$. With $nS^2\ge1$, the residual therefore satisfies $mS^2\ge1/2$. The same pole sum gives $V_h\le2+(3\pi\sqrt3/4)\sqrt h\le6h$ for $h\ge2$, since $3\pi\sqrt3/4<5$. It also gives $|B_m|\le2+3\pi/4<4.4$. The remainder and lag margins still apply with these bounds. If $m<k$ the lag vanishes. The join above uses the existing one- and two-cycle theorem outside the stated residual range.
