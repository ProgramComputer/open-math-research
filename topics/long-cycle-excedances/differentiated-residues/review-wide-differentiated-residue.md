## Signs for the actual insertion

The common amplitude in the $(n,m)$ normalizations is $M_a=e^{-H(w_a)}z_a/r_0$. The contour and pole product have residues $-(1-t)T_s(w_a)/r_0$ and $-d_\infty Q_a(z_a^{-1})/z_a$, respectively, which give the same amplitude. Direct differentiation uses $s'=-\pi/S^2$, $r'_0/r_0=-C/S$ and $z'_a/z_a=(z_a-C)/S$. Thus $M'_a/M_a=z_a/S+\pi H'(w_a)/S^2$. For the principal pair $z_1=e^{-i\theta}$, so its normalized leading derivative has sign opposite to $\sin\Psi$, with $\Psi=(n+1)\theta+\Im H(w_1)$. Differentiating the Poisson polynomial in $H$ leaves exactly the gamma density of shape $k$ at $bw$. The bound by the real samples of that density follows along each specified vertical segment by $|w/s|^{k-1}$, and the real sample sum is bounded by $1/s$ plus the maximum of the density. The real-axis upper bound on $H(s)$ uses a harmonic sum and the lower-tail Chernoff bound for a Poisson variable at two thirds of its mean. The principal lower bound and all nonprincipal relative bounds follow.

Let $c_N=r_0^N/(N!(1-t)^N)>0$. At an old root, $t'=t\pi/S^2$ and $F'_n=c_n E'_{n,k}(t)t'$. Multiplying the insertion recurrence by $(n+1)c_{n+1}$ gives the exact identity
