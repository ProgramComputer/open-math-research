# Independent agent-assisted review of the terminal-rank obstruction

The two identities and both interlacing obstructions pass mathematical review. This review covers the extreme summands and their pair at the two-cycle boundary.

- Contracting the arrow from the largest letter to the second-largest on a cycle of length greater than $k$ produces precisely the old admissible permutations, recovering each by inserting the largest before the second-largest. The predecessor edge changes from one rise to two edges with one rise. When the cycle has size $k$, the chosen $k-2$ labels can be arranged freely between the second-largest and the closing edge back to the largest. The closing edge gives exactly one rise, and complementing labels turns the internal ascent count into the Eulerian descent count. The independent checker reproduced both extreme-summand formulas on six parameter pairs.
- Inversion gives reciprocity with exponent equal to the number of letters because there are no fixed points. For the correction, $tA_{k-2}$ has reciprocal exponent $k-1$, so its product with the residual row has reciprocal exponent $n-1$, matching the old row. Conjugation reversing all smaller labels preserves one rise among the two edges incident to the largest while reversing every other comparison. The two extreme rows are therefore related by a factor of $t$. The equal-degree rational ratio is nonconstant, analytic on the positive axis, and invariant under inversion. Its derivative satisfies the stated antisymmetry, so it takes both signs. A weak real interlacing ratio could only have one residue sign after common factors were removed and would have a constant polynomial part.
- In the Eulerian series for the denominator, the first three terms at $z=2/4^m$ give $-1+4(9/16)^m$. The ratio of successive magnitudes from the fourth term onward is bounded by $2(25/64)^m<1$. At $z=2^{-m}$, the first four terms give $1-2\cdot2^m+(9/4)^m$, and the ratio from the fifth term onward is bounded by $(18/25)^m<1$. These justify the respective alternating-tail signs. The two numerator factors are strictly positive on the bracket by pairing the alternating terms, even when the first pair cancels at its endpoint. The two denominator roots closest to zero are simple and their derivatives alternate sign. The residue numerators have the same strict negative sign, so the two residues have opposite signs.
- The corrected numerator inside the outside factor $1+t$ has the denominator's monic leading term, since the correction has one smaller degree. Hence the paired ratio has slope one at infinity.
