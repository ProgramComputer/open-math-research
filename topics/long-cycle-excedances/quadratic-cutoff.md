# A quadratic uniform bound for the cycle threshold

**Theorem.** An absolute $B$ suffices for the conclusions of the fixed-threshold theorem whenever $k\ge3$ and $n\ge Bk^2$. This includes simultaneous strict interlacing as $k$ grows up to an absolute multiple of $\sqrt n$. All comparisons are between sizes $n,n+1$ at the same $k$. We use the notation and normalization in the [fixed-threshold proof](fixed-threshold.md).

Write $L(w)=\log T_s(w)$ with its polynomial branch. For $s>0$ the Eulerian power-sum identity gives
$$
 L(w)=aw-\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{(-1)^m e^{-ms}}{m}
                       \sum_{j=2}^{k-1}\frac{(mw)^j}{j!}.
 \tag{2}
$$
For every $s\ge0$ we also have
$$
 L(w)=aw-\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}\frac1j\sum_{p\ {\rm odd}}(w/w_p)^j.
 \tag{3}
$$
For (3), factor $1+e^{w-s}=2e^{(w-s)/2}\cosh((w-s)/2)$. The order-one Hadamard factorization centered at $w=0$ has genus at most one. Its logarithm has coefficient $-\sum_p w_p^{-j}/j$ in degrees $j\ge2$, by absolute convergence. Comparison with the Eulerian EGF gives (3). At $s=0$ the factorization is regular at zero.

For $s\ge10k$, keep the fixed-threshold proof's original circle, which has $R\le2s$. In (2) the truncated exponential with absolute argument $mR$ is at most $4^k\exp(mR/4)$. The whole nonlinear part is $O(4^k e^{-s/2})=O(e^{-s/3})$ throughout the circle; the linear part is $O(se^{-s})$. Along $w_1=s+i\pi$, differentiation of (2) costs at most a factor $m$ in each summand and a second copy of a truncated exponential. It gives the same exponential bound for the total derivative with respect to $s$. In particular the phase and its angular derivative are small there. For $s$ above an absolute constant the pole amplitude ratios are below two on this circle. If necessary increase $B$ to treat finitely many bounded $k,s$ using the compact-interval fixed-threshold argument.

For $0\le s\le10k$ choose a radius $R$ in $[r(1+1/k),r(1+2/k)]$ at radial distance at least $c/k^2$ from every pole. Indeed there are $O(1+s)=O(k)$ distinct pole radii below $3r$ and the interval has length $r/k$. Removing intervals of radius $c/k^2$ around them leaves an admissible choice for an absolute $c>0$. On the circle the reciprocal denominator is $O(k^2)$, by the elementary distance estimate in the fixed-threshold note (with a variable distance). We give three bounds for $L$.

1. For $0\le s\le\sqrt k$ and $|w|\le R$, equation (3) gives $|L(w)|\le C(1+s)\log(k+1)$. Indeed $|w/r|^j\le e^2$ and $\sum_{p\ {\rm odd}} |r/w_p|^j\le \sum_p |r/w_p|^2=O(1+s)$ for $j\ge2$, by comparison with the integral of $r^2/(s^2+\pi^2 p^2)$ away from the first poles. The term $aR$ is bounded. For the angular derivative at $w_1$ set $v_p=w_1/w_p=e^{i\theta}/(\cos\theta+ip\sin\theta)$. Then $|v'_p|=|p-1||v_p|^2$. Thus $\sum_p |v_p|^{j-1}|v'_p|\le\sum_p|p-1||v_p|^3=O((1+s)^2)$ by comparison with the integral or splitting at $|p|=1+s$. Summing over $j$ gives $|\gamma'(\theta)|=O(k(1+s)^2)=O(k^2)$. The linear term has a bounded angular phase derivative since $\Im(aw_1)=\pi a$.

2. For $\sqrt k\le s\le10k$ and $|w|\le R$ we have $|L(w)|\le C(1+\log^+(k/s))$. Here $R-s\le C_1(1/s+s/k)$ and $R/s$ is bounded by an absolute constant. For $m\le M=\lceil 10k/s\rceil$, $m(R-s)\le C_2$, so the absolute value of the $m$th summand of (2) is at most $e^{C_2}/m$. For the remaining terms and sufficiently large $k$, $R/s\le3/2$. Bound the truncated exponential by $2^k e^{mR/2}$. Then $e^{-ms}2^ke^{mR/2}\le e^{-ms/8}$ since $ms>10k$. The geometric tail is bounded uniformly. Finitely many smaller $k$ can again be handled by the fixed-threshold compact-interval argument. Differentiating along $w_1$ costs a factor at most $2m$ before taking the same absolute majorants (one sum runs only to $j=k-2$). Hence $|dL(w_1)/ds|=O(1+k/s)$, including the differentiated linear term. As $|ds/d\theta|=(s^2+\pi^2)/\pi$, this gives $|\gamma'(\theta)|=O(k^2)$.

3. The circle integral in this range divided by its principal amplitude is bounded by $C k^2 r\exp(2\sup |L|)(1+1/k)^{-n}$. Dividing by $S=\pi/r$ costs at most $O(k)$ more. Its logarithm is bounded by $C(1+\sqrt k)\log(k+1)+O(\log k)-n/(2k)$. Increasing $B$ gives $|e_n|+|e_{n+1}|<S/40$ for $n\ge Bk^2$. For other included poles, split $|z_p|^{n+1}$ at exponent $n/2$. Since $s\le10k$ and $n\ge Bk^2$, we have $(n/2)S^2\ge c_0 B\ge1$ after increasing $B$. The fixed-threshold weighted integral bound applies to the first factor. The second is at most $(1+8S^2)^{-n/4}\le\exp(-c_1 n/(1+s^2))$. In $s\le\sqrt{k}$ this exponential absorbs the bound $\exp(C(1+\sqrt k)\log(k+1))$ for the amplitude ratio, uniformly by enlarging $B$. In $s\ge\sqrt k$ it absorbs $\exp(C(1+\log^+(k/s)))$ since $\sup_{x\ge1/10}(1+\log^+ x)/x^2<\infty$. This proves both principal-pole estimates whenever $nS^2\ge1$. In the range $s\ge10k$ these estimates already follow from the original fixed-threshold circle and the first bound after (3).

By the phase derivative bound $\Psi_n$ increases for $n\ge Bk^2$ with the same endpoints. At $s=10k$ its phase is at least $(n+1)\arctan(\pi/(10k))-O(1)$, greater than $(H+1)\pi$ for $H=\lfloor3\sqrt n/8\rfloor$ by choosing $B$ large. Thus the first checkpoint occurs where $s>10k$. At that checkpoint $\theta=\Theta(n^{-1/2})$ because $\gamma$ is bounded there, and consequently $s=\Theta(\sqrt n)$. Its phase correction is then $O(e^{-c\sqrt n})$ uniformly. The lower sine bound, the overlap with the $H$ innermost roots, the exact rank count and the consecutive sign comparison follow from the fixed-threshold proof, with the constant gap between $3\pi/8$ and one. The extreme-rank lemma already holds for every $n\ge\max(400,25k^2)$. This proves uniformity in $k$. The proof establishes existence of $B$; the explicit cutoff 400 at $k=3$ is obtained separately.
