# Sources for the fixed-threshold excedance problem

Per Alexandersson asks whether the excedance polynomials for permutations of $n$ letters whose cycles all have length at least $k$ are real-rooted and interlace consecutively, for every $k\ge3$ and every $n\ge k$. The two polynomials have a common zero at zero. The separate question about ordinary descents asks for real-rootedness. The online statement gives computations for $k\le6$, $n\le22$. The statistic, its cycle generating function and the two different conjectures appear together in the section “Permutations with long cycles.” [Original statement](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjLongCycleExcedances)

On November 21, 2026 Alexandersson reported an exact Sturm search for $2\le k\le20$ through $n=90$, and further rows for $21\le k\le56$ near $n=2k$. At $k=3$ he reduced the claim to an additional interlacing hypothesis. He reported that establishing that hypothesis in every row remained open. An independent investigation opened November 20 records obstructions to directly extending a lagged induction. [Author’s report](https://github.com/PerAlexandersson/RealRooted/issues/2590#issuecomment-7059794955) · [Independent investigation and repository links](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/81)

Alexandersson's 2026 preprint treats other Eulerian families, including ordinary descents on derangements. The statistic and cycle restrictions differ from the claim here. Its Lean formalizations concern the theorems stated in that preprint. [arXiv:2609.07325v2, October 24, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07325v2)

On November 25, 2026 we checked combinations of “Alexandersson”, “excedance polynomial”, “minimum cycle length”, “no 2-cycles”, “cycles of length at least”, “derangements”, “real-rootedness” and “interlacing” against indexed research papers, GitHub, and indexed X posts. The search located the source, its computational update and the independent investigation above. We found no earlier argument establishing every row at $k=3$ or every sufficiently large row at each fixed $k$. Public X indexing and keyword searches are incomplete. We make no definitive priority claim.
