## Occupied poles and error bounds

The Eulerian cycle expansion gives the product over odd poles, and the established variation lemma gives the numerator norm below $2/k$. Every occupied pole costs at least $k$ letters. The partial fractions of $\prod_{p\in I}(1-z_p u)^{-1}$ assign all the remaining degree to the distinguished pole, so the exponents sum to the required row index and each is at least $k$. The identity $1-z_q/z_a=i(q-a)S z_q$ loses at most one power of $\rho_q$ at each nondistinguished pole. For differentiation, $|z_p'/z_p|\le |p|\rho_p$, and the normalized power exponents sum to one. The logarithmic derivative of the inverse separation factor is exactly $-z_q/S$. These facts give both residual inequalities. For the fixed row indices the sums and derivatives have summable envelopes on compact positive angle intervals.

The exponent choices $23,96,64$ follow from $k>n/4$, $m=n+1-k\ge 2n/3$, and monotonicity of $(1+x/n)^n$. The omitted weighted pole sum is bounded by the stated integral because its summand decreases past nine. The double moment estimate follows by distinguishing a tail coordinate and bounding the maximum by a sum of absolute indices. The multiplicity of ordered lists per distinguished unordered set is $(h-1)!$.

I reproduced all four lines of the supplied rational checker byte for byte. I also checked the pole estimates using a separate rational bound. I retained poles through $|p|=17$ and bounded the omitted moment of order $r$ by $2\cdot160^h17^{r+1-2h}/(2h-r-1)$ for the envelope $K^{-h}$. For two sorted poles the full separation kernel is $2/(p_2-p_1)$, and for three it is $2/((p_2-p_1)(p_3-p_2))$. In this check, $(U_2,V_2)$ are bounded above by $(2.116257,4.565920)$ and $(U_3,V_3)$ by $(0.429684,1.395324)$. Upper bounds for $|B_k|$, $|B_m|$ and the principal-pair value and normalized derivative errors in $B_n$ are $2.736466$, $2.088349$, $0.018490$ and $0.055475$. The scaled residual value and power-derivative bounds are below $0.318793$ and $0.692401$, and the scaled inverse-denominator derivative cost is below $0.004132$. The proposed constants give the derivative lower bound above $4/25$ at an old zero and the lag upper bound below $9/100$ in the same units.

## Exterior join and last pair

The established exterior theorem applies without a threshold-ratio hypothesis and places its $H$ roots before sample $H+1$ in modulus. Its scalar checker passed. Using $157/50<\pi<22/7$ and the cubic sine lower bound gives $n\sin^2((H+1)\pi/n)>1.207749$, so the first sample is already inside the new estimate. Reflection puts the other $H$ roots beyond the final sample. For $H+1\le j\le n-H-1$ the alternating signs provide $n-2H-2$ intervening roots. The two exterior groups and these gaps account for the full degree $n-2$, including the gap through $-1$ when $n$ is odd.

At an old zero $x$, the common factor taking $S F_n'$ and $B_kF_m$ to the derivative contribution and lag is $n!(1-x)^{n+1}/(x r^{n+1})$. The recurrence applies to the entire new polynomial. At $n=4k-1$ the two pole expansions used are for $n$ and $m=3k$, so the calculation uses at most three occupied poles even though the new row admits four cycles. The lag starts in degree one and ends at degree $n-2$. Both signed next rows therefore have constant and leading coefficients one. Reciprocal substitution multiplies the derivative contribution and lag at old roots by the same factor. Their old-root sign comparisons, with the endpoint signs, give $n-1$ simple negative next roots in strict order.

In the opening statement, “derivative contribution’s sign” identifies the sign of $x(1-x)P_n'(x)$. The expression “derivative’s sign” by itself could be read as the sign of $P_n'(x)$. This is an expository issue. The bounds and root argument have no mathematical gap within the stated range.
