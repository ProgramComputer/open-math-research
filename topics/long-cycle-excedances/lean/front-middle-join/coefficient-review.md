# Independent review of the finite exterior conditions

Agent-assisted source and mathematical review. The package gives a sufficient condition for the same consecutive pair as the conditional exterior-root reduction. Each condition is tested at lower size $n$ for the whole specified exterior front of that pair. I found no mathematical gap in the two residual tests.

For a positive closed modulus interval $[L,U]$, division by $v^h$ at any $v\in[L,U]$ gives a term of absolute magnitude $|[X^j]R|v^{j-h}$. When $j\ge h$, it is bounded using $U^{j-h}$. When $j<h$, the bound uses $1/L^{h-j}$. The finite support sum therefore bounds $|R(-v)|/v^h$ for both residuals. Equality is allowed at the endpoints in this auxiliary estimate.

The old residual subtracts $aX^h(1+X/u)$ from $P$. Multiplication of the evaluation at $-v$ by $(-1)^h$ normalizes the model to $av^h(1-v/u)$. At $L=7u/8$ and $U=9u/8$ the model is respectively $av^h/8$ and $-av^h/8$. The strict finite certificate bound below $a/8$ ensures strict positive and negative endpoint signs for the signed old row. Continuity gives a root $-w$ with $L<w<U$, including when other old roots happen to lie in the same box.

The successor residual is $Q-GP+(1-X)aX^h$. At the old zero the $GP$ term is exactly zero, with no constraint on the choice of $G$. Signed evaluation gives $(-1)^hQ(-w)+(1+w)aw^h$. The strict bound below $aw^h$ implies $(-1)^hQ(-w)<-waw^h<0$, since $a,w>0$. Consequently the literal successor has sign $(-1)^{h+1}$. For index $i$ with $h=i.\mathrm{val}+1$ this is precisely the required exponent $i.\mathrm{val}+2$. The witnesses use strict interior inequalities, so consecutive window endpoint equality is harmless and strict modulus order follows. The positivity and exterior cap then follow from the interval endpoints. Division by $-w$ flips the successor sign only at the separate reduced-row bridge.

The analytic box-bound entry point uses the same zero-based convention through the actual recurrence adapter. Its lag is the full insertion term including $X$, and its output cap multiplies $(51/5)(H/(H+1))^n$ by $9/8$, yielding exactly $(459/40)(H/(H+1))^n$.

This establishes the sufficiency of the finite coefficient inequalities. A proof of their feasibility for every pair in the large-size sector remains separate.
