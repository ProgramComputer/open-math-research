## Claim and signs

The original row $E_{n,k}$ has coefficient of $t^j$ equal to the number of permutations of size $n$ with exactly $j$ positions $i<\pi(i)$ and all cycle lengths at least $k$. The condition on $n/(200k)$ and $\log n\ge 0$ implies $4000k<n$, so $k\le  n$ needs no extra hypothesis. Every admissible cycle has an ascent and a descent in cyclic label order. The unique one-excedance permutation is the descending $n$-cycle; inversion exchanges excedances and deficiencies without changing the cutoff. The reduced row $E_{n,k}/t$ is therefore monic reciprocal of degree $n-2$ with constant term one, and the larger reduced row has degree $n-1$. The common zero is simple. The conclusion here gives the strict strengthening on these reduced rows and the original weak interlacing on this size sector only.

Write $H=\lfloor 7\sqrt  n/20\rfloor$, $J=\lfloor \sqrt  n/2\rfloor$, $h=i+1$ for exterior index $i\in\mathrm{Fin}(H)$, $P=E_{n,k}$, $Q=E_{n+1,k}$, $c_j=[t^j]P$, and $u_h=c_h/c_{h+1}$. The exterior pair is at ranks $h,h+1$, both in the window $1,\ldots,J$, and $H+2\le  J$. On the endpoints of $[7u_h/8,9u_h/8]$, the old approximation $c_h(-r)^h(1-r/u_h)$ has opposite signs and magnitude $c_hr^h/8$. A strict residual bound gives a root in the open box. At an old root in the box, the successor approximation is $-(1+r)c_h(-r)^h$, so a normalized error below $c_h$ gives literal next-row sign $(-1)^{h+1}=(-1)^{i+2}$. This follows by exact subtraction of $(nt+(1-t)(h+1))P$ from $Q$ and addition of $(1-t)c_ht^h$, so derivative centering has coefficient factor $j-h-1$.

The adaptive cap on $u_h$ becomes $459/40$ after multiplication by $9/8$. The checked log-separation theorem places the whole front below $e^{-b}$, with $b=\pi/\tan((H+5/6)\pi/n)$. The actual phase at $b$ is strictly below $(H+1)\pi$ and the phase at $0$ is $n\pi/2$. Thus the first complete middle cell starts at global index $H$ and has sign $(-1)^{H+2}$, immediately after the last front sign $(-1)^{H+1}$. There are $\lfloor  n/2\rfloor-(H+1)$ middle cells. Their strict interiors exclude modulus one even at the even-size terminal phase hit. Front plus middle give $\lfloor (n-2)/2\rfloor$ increasing moduli below one. Reciprocity supplies the moduli above one and the old central root when $n$ is odd. The center-sign interface supplies the successor sign in that case. Dividing by $t=-r$ reverses all literal signs uniformly. Alternating signs, the positive constant coefficient and the sign at negative infinity of the monic next quotient produce $n-1$ distinct next roots in the $n-1$ complementary intervals. The degree counts force both lists to be simple and exhaust their polynomials, with the larger row giving both extremes.

## Exterior envelopes and the lag

Set $\alpha_j=c_j/j^n$. For positive $i,j\le  J$, use the lower bound at the denominator index $i$ and the two upper bounds at the numerator index $j$.

$$
\alpha_j/\alpha_i\le (100/99)(1+(8/3)(151/100)i/j)\le  (51/10)\max(1,i/j).
$$

In particular $\rho_h=\alpha_h/\alpha_{h+1}$ is bounded by $(51/10)(h+1)/h$ and its inverse by $51/10$. The exact identity $u_h=\rho_h(h/(h+1))^n$ gives the cap, and $\rho_h q_hu_{h+1}=\rho_{h+1}u_h$ with $q_h=(1-(h+1)^{-2})^n$. The ratio of neighboring centers is at least $2200/(51/5)^2>20$, which is enough for all pairwise ordered boxes by iteration. The adjacent argument uses $h+1\le  H$, so its last coefficient is $c_{h+2}$.

The left normalized tail at $r=7u_h/8$ has $j=h-\ell >0$ and growth base $(8/(7\rho_h))q_h$; the right one at $r=9u_h/8$ has $j=h+1+\ell$ and base $(9\rho_h/8)q_h$, with separate prefactor $9/8$. Bernoulli gives the needed upper bound for $1-\ell /h$ on the left and $1+\ell /(h+1)$ on the right. The left includes a separate zero-coefficient case at $j=0$. From $H+1\le (9/25)\sqrt  n$ we get $q_h<1/2200$; for $h\le 5$, $q_h<1/4000$. For $h\ge 6$, $\rho_h\le (51/10)(7/6)<6$, so both multipliers are strictly below $1/300$ in both ranges. The left ratio uses $h/j\le\ell +1$. On the right for $j\le  J$, $\alpha_j/\alpha_{h+1}\le 51/10$. For $j>J$, $j>\sqrt  n/2$ gives $\ell >(7/50)\sqrt  n$, $h+1<(18/7)\ell$, and $j<4\ell$. Thus $1/\alpha_{h+1}<8(\ell +1)$, using $k\le  n$. Together with the global $\alpha_j\le 1$, these give the right envelope $9c_h(\ell +1)300^{-\ell}$. The same estimate applies with raw numerator $j^n$. The global $V_j\le  j^{n+1}$ then gives exactly $36c_h\ell (\ell +1)300^{-\ell}$ for the far lag.

The near lag is the full additive insertion summand $V=\binom n{k-1}(tA_{k-1})E_{n+1-k,k}$. This definition includes the factor of $t$. Let $p>0$ be a coefficient rank independent of $h$, and $d=k-1$. The transformed successor formula gives a sum over $j=0,\ldots,p$ with factor $\binom nd j^d$ and residual acceptance count at $p-j$ colors and $n+1-k=n-d$ labels. Replacing the count by $(p-j)^{n-d}$ and dividing by $p^n$ gives the Bernstein binomial atom of outcome $d$ at $j/p$. The two exponents $d$ and $n-d$ add to the normalization exponent $n$. Both mesh endpoints vanish since $0<d<n$. Its derivative changes from positive to negative at $d/n$, and its integral is $1/(n+1)$. For each side of a single maximum, the adjacent mesh interval on the side towards the mode bounds the summand by $p$ times the integral over that interval. At the one interval straddling the mode the integral bounds the smaller endpoint sample times its width, so only the larger endpoint sample is charged separately. Zuo’s atom bound gives the peak at most $\sqrt  n/(2\sqrt {d(n-d)})\le 1/\sqrt  d$ because $2d\le  n$. This checks the shape, sampling endpoints and use of the supplying theorem. [Supplying binomial estimate](https://github.com/AlexZander-666/long-cycle-excedance-interlacing/blob/46dc6e9cc3d768ae8b1ed41617d4b1eef9b1b554/LongCycleBounds/BandBinomial.lean#L77-L86)

For $k<150$, $n\ge 200p$ follows from $n\ge 10000$ and $4p^2\le  n$ (split at $p=50$), and $kp/n\le 3/4$. Thus at $j\ge 1$ the atom falls in the lower tail with $k\le  3nj/(4p)$. Tilt $3/4$ gives exponent $-nj/(32p)$, and the geometric series is below $1/500$ since $e^{200/32}>501$. This is already below $\alpha_p/8$ since $\alpha_p\ge  .99/3$. For $k\ge 150$, put $x=p/n$, $M=1/\sqrt {k-1}$. The mesh estimate is at most $x+M$. The assumptions imply $x\le 1/200$, $kx^2\le 1/16000$, $M\le 1/12$, $kM^2\le 2$, and hence $kxM\le 1/80$. The product $(x+M)(1+8kx/3)$ is then bounded above by $1/200+1/12+(8/3)(1/16000+1/80)<.99/8$, giving $V_p<c_p/8$. Rank zero is covered by $0\le  V_0\le  0^{n+1}=0$. This is stronger than $8c_p/25$, and there is no gap at $p=J$ or $k=150$.

The normalization compares the actual coefficient with the acceptance probability $f$, with $0\le  f-\alpha_p\le 2n(1-1/p)^n$. Marking an orbit of $j$ colors gives expected count $1/j$ times its binomial load probability; the union estimate needs no independent orbit loads. In the lower bound, using $b=\lceil 4kp/(3n)\rceil$ puts all $j\ge  b$ above the required mean. The lower orbit-only mass $1/(2b-1)$, the error $1/[250(2b-1)]$, and the transform loss scaled by $2b-1$ of at most $3/4000$ yield the claimed normalized lower estimate. The case $p=1$ is exact. In the upper bound when $a=kp/n\ge 3/2$, $b=\lceil 2a/3\rceil$ puts every $j<b$ below the required mean. Tilt $3/2$ gives exponent at most $-k/15$, since $\log(3/2)>2/5$. The upper mass $1/b$ and the stated $0.009/a$ error give $1.509/a\le 1.51/a$; below $a=3/2$ the trivial upper bound one suffices.

Finally the weighted old residual and centered derivative sums are less than $c_h/10$ and $3c_h/20$ respectively, using the finite support injection into separate nonnegative geometric sums for $\ell \ge 1$. The lag sum is at most $[ (8/25)(1+9/8+1/10)+36/(75\cdot 74)]c_h<18c_h/25$, even with the weaker far range $\ell \ge 2$. Since $9u_h/8<1/2$, the normalized successor residual is at most $(3/2)(3/20)c_h+(18/25)c_h< c_h$. These match the strict inequalities needed for the preceding sign argument.

## Attribution and overlap

Zuo’s released exact small-cutoff results extend through $k=7$ as of this check. The all-size $k=7$ result in v1.3.0 is disjoint from $k\ge 8$. The strongest overlap identified here is Zuo’s v1.0.0 `fixed_cutoff_eventual_original`, which quantifies $\forall  k\ge 3,\exists  N(k)$ and gives strict interlacing of consecutive quotients and weak interlacing of the literal rows from upper size $N(k)$. Substituting upper size $n+1$ gives overlap for every fixed $k\ge 8$ at sufficiently large $n$. Its exported statement does not imply the explicit joint $n,k$ sector reviewed here. Zuo’s binomial atom input is also directly reused. An existing repository written large-size theorem already covers $n\ge 10{,}000$ and $n/k\ge 4$ for $k\ge 7$, hence the whole sector. The advance proposed here is a literal formal verification of an explicit range within that written result.
