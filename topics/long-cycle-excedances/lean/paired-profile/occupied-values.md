# Value-only occupied-pole limit

Fix the coefficient degree $N\gt0$, the cutoff $k\ge3$, and a signed odd-pole family $w_q=C+i(2q+1)S$ with $C,S$ real and $S\ne0$. Put $z_q=w_q^{-1}$ and $D=D_k$. The distinguished series is $P_a=D(z_a u)-1$. For $q\ne a$, write $G_{a,q}(u)$ for the primitive factor with coefficient $b_s z_q^{s-1}/(w_q-w_a)$ in degree $s$. Both $P_a$ and $G_{a,q}$ vanish below degree $k$. [Actual factor definitions and majorant](ActualOccupiedValue.lean)

The summation index is a pair $(a,T)$ with an integer mark and a finite subset of integers. A pair with $a\in T$ has term zero. Otherwise use the coefficient of degree $N$ of $P_a\prod_{q\in T}G_{a,q}$. The checked product equivalence uses the canonical increasing enumeration $\mathrm{Fin}(|T|)\to T$. Expanding coefficient multiplication then allocates one degree to each entry of that tuple, exactly as in the written degree-by-degree expansion. The degree sum can be kept inside each occupied term because it is finite. [Tuple product](OccupiedTuple.lean)

For the analytic bound use the polar weight $V(q)=\sum_{j=2}^{N}|z_q|^j$, which is summable. At each degree up to $N$, the distinguished coefficient is bounded by $c_D V(a)$ and each primitive coefficient by $c_B V(q)/(2|S|)$. Here $c_D$ and $c_B$ are finite sums of the norms of their scalar coefficients. The primitive exponent is at least two since $k\ge3$. Successive coefficient convolutions contribute at most $N+1$ per factor. Thus a summable nonnegative majorant is a distinguished weight times a product of summable activities over the finite set. To bound the sum of all such products, any finite collection of sets lies in the powerset of its finite union. The sum over that powerset is $\prod(1+w)$, bounded by $\exp(\sum w)$. [Absolute summability](OccupiedCoefficient.lean) · [Powerset majorant and cofinal support net](OccupiedSupportNet.lean)

For a finite pole set $A$, expansion of each finite primitive product gives exactly the finite slice of marked configurations with support in $A$. The support net is cofinal because the union of the supports of any finite batch is finite. The limit of the slice sums is therefore the absolutely convergent full-lattice sum. Uniqueness against the finite-product limit from #283 identifies the sum with the scaled original normalized row. This covers both the old degree and the residual degree in the intended bounded-aspect sector.
