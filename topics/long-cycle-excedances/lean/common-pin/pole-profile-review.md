The supplying definition `Band.normalizedEnumerator` is the evaluation of the literal permutation polynomial at $t=-e^{-x}$ divided by $(1-t)^n$. This denominator is positive. It is a normalization of $E_{n,k}$, rather than of the reduced polynomial $E_{n,k}/t$. The exact expansion `original_normalized_pole_profile` holds for $x\ge0$, $k\ge2$ and every natural row size. For an allowed cycle of length $a$, the frozen Eulerian identity gives the signed real moment as $(a-1)!$ times the real part of the complete odd-pole series. Dividing by $h!$ in the ordered support sum removes the orders of the $h$ cycles. The bound $h\le\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ is exact for possible cycle counts. At $n=0$ the only term is the empty profile and its error is zero. For $0<n<k$ all profile terms vanish. The quantitative theorem also requires $k\ge8$ and $kS^2\ge1$, where $S=\pi/\sqrt{x^2+\pi^2}$. With $x\ge0$ this is the closed interval $0\le x\le\pi\sqrt{k-1}$. Both $t=-1$ and equality at the angular entrance are included. The bound does not cover cutoff seven or arbitrarily small modulus at a fixed cutoff. Each allowed length $a$ inherits $a\ge8$ and $aS^2\ge1$. To check the per-pole estimate independently, put $\lambda=l^2-1\ge0$. Bernoulli gives $(1+\lambda S^2)^{a/8}\ge1+\lambda/8$. Raising to power $-4$ gives the claimed envelope $(8/(l^2+7))^4$ for the normalized pole at length $a$. Pole $l=3$ costs $1/16$. The frozen weighted tail bound $2/27$ for the positive odd poles from 5 gives an unweighted upper bound $2/81$ via their common lower weight of 3. Their sum $113/1296$ is less than $1/9$. Taking the real part and then accounting for the conjugate pole yields the bound relative to the two-pole scale $2(a-1)!/(\sqrt{x^2+\pi^2})^a$.

For each cycle count the argument propagates the error through every term of the ordered support recurrence. The weight $((10/9)^h-1)$ bounds the accumulated error in replacing the full pole sum independently in each of the $h$ factors. It does control the multi-cycle contribution involving at least one nonnearest pole. The principal profile itself still contains products in which different cycles choose different poles from the nearest conjugate pair. The theorem has no reduction to a single global oscillation, cancellation across adjacent row sizes, or signed insertion comparison. Those steps and any sharper error margins are separate obligations for proving real-rootedness or interlacing.
