## Probability bounds and bin reconstruction

The recurrence $r d_r=1+\sum_{j=k}^{r-k}d_j$ first gives the standard bounds $1/(2k-1)\le d_r\le1/k$. For $2k\le r<3k$, the harmonic upper sum is at most $\log(r/k-1)+1/k$ and the tangent to the logarithm at $7/4$ gives $kd_r\le4/7$. At larger $r$, the same induction uses the initial harmonic block and the $r-3k+1$ indices in the remainder, so the sufficient inequality is $1+\log 2+(1+4/7)/k<12/7$. The lower bound on $3k\le r<4k$ uses $kd_j\ge1/2$ for the residual indices past $2k$, giving $kd_r\ge1/2+(\log2-1/2)/(r/k)>.547$. The induction for $r\ge4k$ follows from $1+\log2+1/2>4(.547)$. The first-block telescoping estimate and the positive jump at $2k$ match the adjoining blocks, and the factorial bound for each subsequent numerator block follows from $sb_s=d_{s-k}-d_{s-1}$ and its short-difference form.

Each factor term of degree $s$ can be marked with the second variable to power $i=\lceil4s/k\rceil$. Then the coefficient of degree $T$ is split across the different sums $b$ of these marks. For every nonempty term, $\max(1,b/5)\le T/k\le b/4$. All contributing $T$ are at most $n-k$, hence omitting primitive degrees greater than $Xk$ and bins greater than $5X$ in nonnegative power-series operations leaves all contributing terms. Removing bins with $\max(1,b/5)+1>X$ is also valid for either output. At the first block the telescoping sum uses the floors of the two scaled bin endpoints. The source certificate's denominators bound these in the correct directions and include the terminal jump separately.
