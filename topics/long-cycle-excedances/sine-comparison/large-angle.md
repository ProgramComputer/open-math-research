# Positive adjacent sine comparison when the scaled angle is at least ten

Let $n\ge10000$, $4\le x=n/k\le48$, $S=\sin\theta>0$, $0<\theta\le\pi/2$, $U=6/5\le nS^2$ and $A=kS\ge10$. Use the literal normalized row $F_m$ and determinant $D_n$ defined in the [topic note](README.md). The claim is $kD_n/S>473268/10^6$. This supplies the large-angle estimate for verification of the bounded-aspect proof.

Use the [small-angle occupied-term identity](small-angle.md), retaining the two principal terms with distinguished degree $n$ and residual degree zero. Their exact contribution to $kD_n/S$ in the value part is $2kd_n\ge1094/1000$. The numerator-difference part for these poles is included in the error. Every other value term of residual degree $T$ has size bounded by $kd_r |a|\rho_a^{r+1}|q_{a,T}|$ at $r=n-T\ge k$. A numerator term has size at most $(k/S)|b_s|\rho_a^s|q_{a,T}|$ for $s=n+1-T\ge k$. Here $a$ is any signed odd number, $w_a=\cos\theta+iaS$, $\rho_a=|w_a|^{-1}$, and $q_{a,T}$ is a product coefficient in the occupied expansion. [Occupied-pole and bin definitions](../degree-blocks/README.md) · [Lattice certificate](../paired-residues/check.py)

Fix a slab $\ell\le x\le X$. Let $K=\lfloor10000/X\rfloor$, and let $A_0$ be the greater of ten and the lower bound $\lfloor4\sqrt{12000}/X\rfloor/4$. Then $A\ge A_0$ by $x^2 A^2\ge nU$. For each bin $b$, set $h=b/4$, $g=\max(1,b/5)$ for $b>0$ and $g=0$ at $b=0$. The residual degree satisfies $g\le T/k\le h$. Write $y=\max(1/X,1-h/\ell)$, $c=yU/192$, and $W_a(c)=(1+c(a^2-1))^{-96}$. For value terms $r/n\ge y$, and for numerator terms $s/n\ge y$. The reviewed Bernoulli bound gives $\rho_a^{r+1}\le W_a(c)$ or $\rho_a^s\le W_a(c)$ respectively. Use $M(c)=\sum_a W_a(c)$ and $N(c)=\sum_{|a|>1}|a|W_a(c)$, each replaced by its rational upper bound. Let $Q_b$ bound the coefficient mass at each fixed pole in bin $b$, using $A_0$ in the [positive exponential majorant](../paired-residues/check.py).

- For $g+1\le X$, bound the old value cost by $Q_b\big(N(c)+2\mathbf1_{b>0}\big)$, with an additional factor $4/7$ whenever $\ell-h\ge2$. This accounts for all remaining principal and nonprincipal terms.
- For $s>k$, $k^2|b_s|\le v$ with $v=1$ if $\ell-h\le2$, and $v=1/(2(\lceil\ell-h\rceil-1)!)$ otherwise. Use $vM(c)Q_b/A_0$. All numerator terms require $g+1\le X+1/K$ and $b<5X$. The latter follows from $b\le5T/k\le5(x-1+1/k)$.
- The exceptional numerator $b_k=1/k$ requires $T=n+1-k>0$ and $b>4(\ell-1)$. For a single primitive in $q_{a,T}$ its degree is exactly $T$, with $T/k>\ell-1$. Thus $k^2|b_T|\le1/(2(\ell-1)!)$. Summing its off-diagonal divisor at $c_{\star}=(\ell-1-1/K)U/(192X)$ contributes at most $U_0(c_{\star})/S$, where $U_0$ is the reviewed supremum of the signed odd lattice sums with divisor $|a-q|$. The entire single-primitive exceptional cost is at most $M(U/(192X))U_0(c_{\star})/[2(\ell-1)!A_0^2]$. The bound counts the one exact degree once across all bins.
- Each remaining product has at least two primitives, and each primitive has a factor $1/A$. If $L_b$ is the rounded-up one-primitive coefficient used in the same exponential majorant as $Q_b$, the mass with at least two primitives in a positive bin is bounded by $(A_0/A)(Q_b-L_b)$. Indeed compare coefficientwise with $\exp(L)-1-L$ before applying the outward rounding to $Q_b$. Since $(A_0/A)/S\le A_0X/U$, the exceptional multiple-primitive cost is at most $M(c)(A_0X/U)(Q_b-L_b)$ in each eligible bin.

The coarse estimate that retains the full first primitive bin mass in the exceptional term fails on $4\le x\le6$. Isolating its exact degree is essential to this replay. The total of the three costs in the [rational checker](check-bounds.py) leaves a scaled margin greater than $473268/10^6$ on every slab. The smallest certified margin is attained by this bound for $6\le x\le10$. [Exact output](bounds-output.txt)
