# Consecutive interlacing for permutations with a minimum cycle length

For $k\ge3$ and $n\ge k$, define $E_{n,k}(t)$ by summing $t^{\mathrm{exc}(\pi)}$ over permutations of $[n]$ all of whose cycles have length at least $k$. Write $P_{n,k}(t)=E_{n,k}(t)/t$. The zeros of $P_{n,k}$ and $P_{n+1,k}$ are simple, strictly negative and strictly interlace, with the larger row supplying both outer zeros. Restoring the simple zero at the origin gives the real-rootedness and consecutive weak interlacing in Alexandersson’s original conjecture. [Primary statement, “Permutations with long cycles”](https://www.symmetricfunctions.com/realRootedOpenProblems.htm#conjLongCycleExcedances)

## Proof by cutoff

Every admissible cycle has at least one excedance. A permutation with exactly one excedance therefore consists of a single cycle. Writing that cycle starting at its largest element forces all entries to occur in descending order before the unique ascent from the least label back to the greatest. Consequently the coefficient of $t$ is one. Inversion preserves cycle lengths and interchanges excedances and deficiencies, giving one permutation with $n-1$ excedances. Thus $E_{n,k}=tP_{n,k}$ with $P_{n,k}$ monic of degree $n-2$ and constant term one.
