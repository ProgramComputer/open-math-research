## Mathematical judgment

The range partition for $`k\ge7`$ is exhaustive. The three-cycle package covers old size $`n<4k`$, including the four-cycle contribution in the new row at $`n=4k-1`$. The exact prefix covers the remaining old sizes through 250. At sizes from 10,000 the intervals $`[4,48]`$ and $`[44,\infty)`$ cover the whole remaining aspect range. Between 251 and 9,999, aspect 20 belongs to the larger-aspect theorem, while the smaller-aspect theorem applies from old size 1,000 and the intermediate certificates fill 251–999. The use of $`N=n+1`$ in coefficient calculations does not change which tests a pair must pass. In particular, a pair with old size 999 is checked against the genuine row of size 1,000.

I find the counting and sign-transfer interfaces mathematically sound. To fix the base convention, splitting at strict excedance edges gives decreasing runs, and labels assigned to those runs, together with the successor permutation on runs, recover the permutation. This gives the coefficient majorant $`p^n`$ at excedance exponent $`p`$. A fixed-point-free permutation and its inverse have complementary excedance counts. There is a unique allowed permutation with one excedance and its inverse is the unique permutation with $`n-1`$. This gives reciprocity of degree $`n-2`$, constant coefficient one, leading coefficient one, and a simple zero at the origin for the original row. I independently enumerated all 21 rows with $`3\le k\le n\le8`$ and compared them with a separate implementation of insertion. This range includes the first cycle-count transition at $`k=3`$.

In the insertion method, the old polynomial multiplied by its coefficient in the differential recurrence vanishes at the old roots. The remaining term is $`x(1-x)P_n'(x)`$ plus the distinguished-cycle lag, with the lag using the residual size $`n+1-k`$. The strict comparison to the lag therefore certifies the sign of the actual next row. In the two-dimensional comparison the second coordinate at an old root is a positive multiple of the actual next normalized row for the positive insertion choice. A disk centered on the unit phase direction gives a strictly positive projection onto that direction. At phase samples this fixes the old signs. At zeros it fixes the second-coordinate signs. The principal coefficient in the low-angle paired proof can be checked through the residue of $`D(z_1 u)`$ in the pole product. The other terms in the principal continuation estimate are exactly the difference between the truncated distinguished-degree sum and that residue, including the artificial extension when the distinguished degree falls below $`k`$. For each finite occupied pole set, partial fractions in the distinct denominators $`1-z_q u`$ multiply the nondistinguished factor at pole $`q`$ by $`(1-z_q/z_a)^{-1}=z_q^{-1}/(i(q-a)S)`$. This recovers the primitive powers $`z_q^{s-1}`$ used in the bin estimates. In a nonempty bin product, each contributing degree is at least $`k`$. Therefore rounding a primitive index upward by at most one gives $`b\le(B+1)T/k`$, and $`T/k\le b/B`$ is immediate from the same bin definition. The proof then retains the positive lower bound on the distinguished power $`r`$ when applying the pole damping.

For the Fourier comparison I rederived the Eulerian Taylor remainder and $`\partial_\varphi f=i e^{ik\varphi}fD`$. At the second integration by parts the product $`fD\cdot D`$ is bounded by two Cauchy–Schwarz kernel estimates, and differentiating the pole kernel costs its squared $`L^2`$ norm. The consecutive contrast uses $`(r/w-C)/(w_p-w)=(z_p-C)/(w_p-w)+z_p/w`$, so the $`f_{n+1-k}`$ correction really is present and the main contrast gains a factor $`S`$. The principal contrast $`-S\sin\Psi`$ dominates the error at an old zero using the old value bound. The old and next normalizations have positive sign as applied to the original rows. Dividing by the negative variable gives next reduced sign $`(-1)^j`$ at an old root in phase gap $`j`$. The proof uses the actual next row in this step, including when a contour encloses several pole pairs.

For the exterior joins, modulus increases with the angle on the half-axis. A Rouché count for the old exterior brackets gives distinct ranks and a sign of the next row where its annulus has no zero. The middle signs either occur on the integer-angle scale or on the continuous adjusted phase scale, as specified in each proof. On the latter scale, first hits and the last lower hit before the next first hit give disjoint intervals with the desired phase even without phase monotonicity. Combining the front with one chosen old root in each open middle gap gives $`\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1`$ roots below modulus one. Reflection gives the same count above modulus one. The old reciprocal polynomial has a forced midpoint root only in odd old size, and the comparison gives the required new sign there. These counts exhaust degree $`n-2`$. At the reflected old rank $`n-1-h`$, the next sign is $`(-1)^{n-1}(-1)^h=(-1)^{n-1-h}`$. Its sign $`+1`$ at zero and its sign $`(-1)^{n-1}`$ at large negative arguments provide both outside intervals. Together with every inside interval this exhausts the new degree $`n-1`$, proving simplicity and strict alternation.

For the low-height middle certificates, evaluating the old endpoints with certified opposite signs and evaluating the actual next polynomial on the entire old bracket makes the first verifier's inference local. The second verifier instead checks two next endpoint signs. Those are enough in this proof because the other old-root signs and every consecutive pair of disjoint bracket signs force a new root in each complementary interval, exhausting the next degree and ruling out a new root inside any old bracket. The small-size three-cycle pencils use the same degree-exhaustion principle. Merely reproducing a digest or checking two same-signed next endpoints without this global count would be insufficient.

I did not identify a substantive mathematical gap in the range assembly or the estimates checked, and I judge the submitted argument a credible computer-assisted proof of the $`k\ge7`$ claim, subject to the stated finite certificates and the extent of replay below. My review of the longer three-cycle exterior computation used its written certificate and reproduced boundary thresholds, with the remaining supplied logs at that join relied on.
