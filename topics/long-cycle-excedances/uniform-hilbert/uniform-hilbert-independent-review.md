## Coefficient identities and signed slab

From $sd_s=\sum_{j\le s-k}d_j$, for $s>k$ one obtains $sb_s=d_{s-k}-d_{s-1}=-\sum_{i=s-k+1}^{s-1}b_i$. The block estimates follow since the interval on the right uses only the current block and its predecessor, with $s>jk$ in block $j$. When $j\ge2$ a positive maximum cannot be bounded by itself divided by $j$, so the previous block supplies the induction. Including $b_k=1/k$ gives the stated exponential bounds. The half-power bound follows by Cauchy–Schwarz in the exponentially weighted sequence.

The partial-fraction denominator is $(1-z_q/z_a)^{-1}=z_q^{-1}/[i(q-a)S]$. Selecting pole $a$ leaves a full factor $D(z_au)-1$ there and a numerator factor at every other occupied pole. The subtraction of one from $Q_a$ removes the singleton contribution. I independently checked that identity and its derivative in all degrees 1 through 20 on two finite pole systems, using Gaussian-rational coefficient arithmetic and two independent product constructions. Its magnitude is bounded by $\pi/2$ because $1-\eta e^{2ix}$ has positive real part, and the phase factor from index $a$ has unit modulus. For differentiating $z_q^r$, the two gamma shapes for $z_q^{r+1}-Cz_q^r$ reduce to the same shape-$r$ density times $X/r-C^2$, with prefactor $C^{-r-1}$. Its variation is bounded by $C^{-r-1}(1/\sqrt r+S^2)$. Taking $r=s-1$ leaves exactly $C^{-s}$ to combine with the Wiener weight. ## Absolute slab and arithmetic

For $|p|\ge3$, the pole envelope follows from $(1+y)^{\ell/4}\ge1+\ell y/4$. The unweighted odd-lattice envelope is decreasing and the $p$-weighted envelope is unimodal. The unweighted integral is $\pi/(4\sqrt c)$, the weighted integral is $1/(2c)$, and the weighted maximum is less than $1/(3\sqrt c)$, with $c=2\ell S^2/9$.
