## Original polynomials and the two engines

On a fixed ordered label set, write a cycle beginning at its greatest label. The initial edge is a descent, the closing edge is an excedance, and the remaining ascents in the word give the ordinary Eulerian polynomial. Thus the weighted cycle contribution is $t A_{\ell-1}(t)$. Taking the cycle containing the least label gives the first engine's binomial coefficient $\binom{n-1}{\ell-1}$. If the cycle exhausts the label set, the reduction by $t$ leaves $A_{n-1}$. Otherwise there is a factor $t$ after multiplying by the reduced residual row. In the reciprocal basis, multiplication adds formal degrees and a factor of $t$ shifts the gamma index by one and increases the degree by two. The formal degree is $n-2$ throughout, consistent with the Eulerian gamma recurrence.

For the rolling engine, starting at threshold $\lfloor(250+1)/2\rfloor+1$ leaves only single cycles through row 251. At cutoff $k$, the updated row retains all old configurations and adds exactly those with $h\ge1$ cycles of size $k$. The multiplicity $\binom n{hk}(hk)!/((k!)^h h!)$ chooses their union and partitions labels into unordered groups. The residual row at threshold $k+1$ has size at least $k+1$ or is empty. Descending row-size order preserves those smaller residual rows until they are read, and the separate empty-case gamma shift of $h-1$ removes the correct common $t$. The reviewed python-flint version creates a new polynomial for the augmented addition used on reused Eulerian entries and leaves the original polynomial intact.

An independent check enumerates allowed integer partitions of the sample sizes, applies the full cycle multiplicity $n!/\prod_{\ell}(\ell!)^{m_\ell}m_\ell!$, and uses the finite-difference binomial formula for ordinary Eulerian coefficients. It matches 37 full coefficient vectors handed to the original certifier, including next-row size 251. I also enumerated actual permutations through size eight and checked 21 ordinary vectors directly from their cycle lengths and excedance counts. The supplied ordinary-vector cross-check passed its full 1,925-vector run through row size 101.

## Signs, exact midpoints and parity

The base gamma row is the constant one at size 3. At each transition the previous strict brackets contain one root apiece because the alternating endpoint signs and their number exhaust the gamma degree. Positive rational bisection thus preserves the sign brackets. If the old polynomial vanishes at the midpoint, that exact root is the unique root in the current bracket. Moving both endpoints strictly towards it preserves the nonzero signs. Each refined old bracket has two nonzero next-row endpoint values of the required sign. These values alone need no inference about interior zeros. The subsequent disjoint next brackets fill the entire next gamma degree and therefore exclude any next zero inside an old bracket. In particular the next row has a strict sign at the old root. The first next bracket starts at zero where the polynomial has value one. An extra last bracket is created precisely when the next gamma degree increases, using a strict sign inequality rather than a numerical root estimate. The two hard iteration limits cause failure if either separation is unresolved.

The Arb queries give rigorously signed values when an enclosure omits zero. An inconclusive enclosure invokes exact rational evaluation, which is also how the midpoint equality case is detected. The reported counters include the main transitions from size $k$ onward, including setup below $4k$. Temporary single-cycle bootstrap objects at sizes below $k$ are discarded before those counters are accumulated.

The map $v=y/(1-y)^2$ is an increasing bijection on $0<y<1$. Each bracketed gamma root produces exactly one reduced-row root there and its reciprocal outside. For even old formal degree the old and new gamma degrees agree, and the new row has the simple central root $-1$. For odd old formal degree the old row has the simple central root, and the next row has an additional gamma root beyond the last old gamma root. In each case the order on the two halves and at $-1$ gives the correct new–old–new order. The top gamma term is nonzero, which also proves the exact central multiplicity or nonvanishing for the two parities. The reciprocal roots and the central root where present exhaust the full degree.

As a separate test, I used a signed integer primitive-remainder Sturm chain on $Q_{n,k}(-y)$ and $Q_{n+1,k}(-y)$ themselves, without a reciprocal reduction. Across ten pairs of both old parities, it counted and isolated all 436 old and new roots on $0<y<\infty$. The joint brackets strictly alternate and direct evaluation at the old bracket endpoints gives the required next-row sign on both ends. This is a representative check using a different root-counting method.
